**Episode description (for podcast apps and YouTube)**
Why does a man wait on the thing he already knows he should do? And why does the waiting feel so much like peace?

Wes Hart hasn't talked to his older brother since a Thanksgiving argument about their mother's care. Two months of quiet have felt like peace. Jonathon calls it what it is, a cease-fire, and admits he has one running in his own kitchen. They dig into why men wait (fear, comfort and waiting to feel ready) and who really pays for it. They open James 4:17 and Matthew 5:23-24, and Jonathon pushes back hard. Isn't the Bible full of "wait for the Lord"? Isn't some waiting just wisdom? And what about a father he isn't sure he should let back in?

Wes tells the story of his dad's 43-year wait to say four words, and of the man who asked him the same question every other Thursday for six years. Then both hosts put real days and times on the calendar. Also in this episode: what "we'll see" really means to a seven-year-old, and a dog named Pancake.

This week's challenge: pick one thing you've been waiting on, give it a day and a time, and tell one man.

## This week's challenge

1. Pick **one** thing you've been waiting on.
2. Give it a **day and a time**. A real day, not "this week."
3. **Tell one man.** Send a text or a voice memo.
4. **Married:** on a calm night, ask your wife, "Is there something you've been waiting on me for?" Don't defend anything. Pick one thing off the list and give it a day.
5. **Single:** ask your closest friend, "What have I been putting off that you've noticed?"
