# Walk Steadfast · Show Bible (v2)

This is the locked canon for every v2 script (E001-E080). It sits beside STYLE-GUIDE.md: the style guide says how an episode sounds, and this file says what is true in the world of the show. Every script has to agree with it.

**Order of authority.** When two sources disagree, the higher one wins:

1. The approved E007 v2 script (`E007-waiting-is-not-peace.md`). Aaron approved it as-is, backstory included.
2. This bible, including the rulings in section 9.
3. Earlier v2 scripts, in release order. A later episode may never contradict an earlier one.
4. The v1 originals and their "New continuity" rows.

**How to use it**

- A v2 script may compress or drop any v1 detail. It may not change one. Ages, dates, names, jobs, places, who said what and how a story came out all stay as v1 had them, unless section 9 rules otherwise.
- New details are allowed as local texture only: the weather, what was for supper, a one-off joke, a stranger at a gas station. Anything a later episode could call back to (a new person, a new family fact, a date, a running bit) has to be logged in section 11 when the episode is written.
- Never invent a death, diagnosis, divorce, pregnancy, job change, move or new family member for a recurring character.
- A callback always gets one line of setup for the new listener ("Back in January, my dad sent me a friend request. I still haven't answered it."). Never use an episode number.
- Everything in this file is writer reference. None of it is read on air as-is.

## 1. The show

- **Name:** Walk Steadfast. Two men at a table work through one hard part of following Jesus.
- **Hosts:** Wes Hart (driver) and Jonathon (color commentator). Jonathon's last name is never used.
- **Schedule:** a main episode every Tuesday (odd numbers) and **Field Notes** every Friday (even numbers). Every episode runs about 20 minutes (2,800-3,200 spoken words).
- **In-story date:** each episode takes place on its release date (calendar in section 10). "Yesterday," "Sunday" and "this week" count from that date.
- **The four marks** (the spine of the show, from Wes's book): Stop Waiting, Pick Up the Weight, Go First, Work for God's Pay. The canon sentence, said in full in E005: "A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's." There is no fifth mark.
- **Wes's book** is *Steadfast Men*. The first chapter is "A Quiet Tuesday." A video training goes with it. Both live at walksteadfast.com. Only E001 and E005 may name the book on air, once, in the body. No episode opens with it, and the close never plugs it.
- **CTA:** every close ends on walksteadfast.com and @steadfastwalk. Handles: Instagram, X, TikTok, Facebook (`steadfastwalk`), YouTube (`@steadfastwalk`). Hashtag `#WalkSteadfast`.
- **Field Notes in v2.** The same skeleton as a main episode (OPEN word for word, segments, CLOSE word for word), built from the Friday parts:
  1. **The check.** Both hosts report honestly on Tuesday's challenge, misses included, and on any "go-first" each promised. Wes always asks Jonathon by name, and Jonathon always asks Wes.
  2. **The stories.** Updates on that week's threads, told as anecdote then reflection.
  3. **From the Table.** A question men ask a lot, labeled honestly ("Here's one men ask Wes all the time"). Never invent a listener letter or email. If the v1 episode framed a story as a man who emailed, keep that framing exactly as v1 had it and add none.
  4. **Tool on the Table.** One practical tool or habit (the phone basket, the index card, the Thursday text).
  5. **Scripture.** At least one verse read aloud, NIV, verified, the same as on Tuesdays.
  6. **The weekend.** A small weekend challenge that carries Tuesday's forward, then the CLOSE, with a horizontal tease to Tuesday.

## 2. On-air rules that are canon, not style

- **Wes never names his family on air.** He says my wife, my daughter, my son-in-law, my oldest, my youngest, my older brother, my little brother, my mom or mother, my dad. His wife has no name in any script.
- **Jonathon names Dani, Ruby and Theo.** His mom, dad, sister and nephew are never named. His grandfather's name is never said on air. Ruby was the first person he said it to.
- **Other people's stories** are told with permission and without names unless section 7 lists a name ("Merle" and "Brent" were cleared on air, "Shane" by asking him). Men at Wes's Thursday table are described by age and situation.
- **No production talk.** No mics, cameras, sets, props, wardrobe, edits or crew. Wes's white diner mug can be mentioned as a mug he drinks from, never as a prop.
- **No episode numbers** and no "last week we said." Say what happened, with a line of setup.
- **The listener is singular** ("you"), always.
- **Nobody says "AI,"** in any script or show-notes file.

## 3. Wes Hart (the driver)

**Voice.** Fifty-two, Texas Hill Country raised, plain and dry. He's not a preacher. Rural pictures come naturally to him (the barn, the feed store, cattle, cedar, fence posts), and he uses them sparingly. He tells the anchor story, keeps time and makes room. In the family, everybody answers the phone with "Well." Wes answers texts with "K." He sends his first emoji (a thumbs-up) in February.

**Age and timeline**

- Born in 1975. He's **52** in every episode, E001-E080. His birthday is never staged.
- He grew up on his dad's place, next to his granddad's land, in the Hill Country, near a small town with "one stoplight and a Dairy Queen" (E007).
- At 18 he left for school 300 miles away with everything in a Buick. His dad shook his hand at breakfast ("Drive careful") and went to the barn. His mom put warm laundry in the back seat, put her hand on the roof of the car, and said, "Your daddy's not going to say this, so I'm going to. You're a man now. You act like one. And don't come home every weekend." She went inside without waving. He came home every weekend the first semester anyway (E031).
- At 22 he sat with his dying granddad once and shaved him: "You did all right, Sport." See ruling R1.
- At 23 he dated a girl most of a year so he wouldn't come home to an empty room (E058).
- He married his wife in **2000**, at 25. They've been married 27 years.
- **The fog years** run from about 2009 to 2011, ending with Mr. Abernathy. Fall 2009 brought layoff rumors and the steering-wheel moment with his daughter, then 7, in her booster seat. In December 2010 he skipped his oldest's kindergarten Christmas program (the boy was a shepherd). His wife calls it "the fog."
- In spring 2011, at 36, came Mr. Abernathy's question (section 7).
- His forties ("different from the fog years") were three church committees, a ball team, 55-hour weeks and "Wes is on fire." At about 45 his wife asked in the truck, "Where are we going?"
- In 2018 his daughter, then 16, was caught drinking. He stayed at work until 9. "I needed you at four o'clock."
- In **October 2018**, on a Sunday night, his dad died in the hospital with Wes holding his hand. Wes was **43**, and his dad had said "I'm proud of you" in that hospital bed. It was the only time.
- In 2019, at 44, a reorganization took the title he'd held nine years: same pay, a desk by the copier. He didn't tell his wife for three weeks. "I work. Same as you" started then.

**Work.** Never named. Almost 30 years in the same kind of job. He likes part of it (mid-morning, the problem nobody wants), and he first said so in July. In October 2027 he has a review with a new, younger reviewer.

**Body.** Arthritis in both knees, the right one worse (diagnosed Feb 3, 2027). No surgery. PT twice a week at the same clinic as his mother, so on Tuesdays they go together and she counts his reps. His dad had both knees replaced in his sixties and "was never the same."

**Habits and objects**

- Mr. Abernathy's white diner mug.
- An index card in his wallet: his sentence on the front, a prayer list of men's names on the back, later his heading ("Love God out loud, keep the people He gave me, and finish").
- He polishes his church shoes on Saturday nights on newspaper, like his dad.
- He pulls onto the shoulder for funeral processions with his hat on his knee, like his dad ("Somebody's daddy").
- Twenty years of emptying his pockets on the dresser before going into the kitchen.
- The back porch.
- A feed-store run every Saturday. His wife has ridden along most Saturdays for seven years.
- His sentence history: "Pay the bills and don't make trouble" (his old one), and "I'll get to it" (his wife's verdict on his real one).

**Faith.** Steady and practical. He prays out loud at the Thursday table and alone on the porch. **He still can't pray out loud with his wife.** That crack stays open through E080. Never resolve it.

## 5. Jonathon (the color commentator)

**Voice.** Late 30s, from the Houston suburbs, a modern regular guy: quick, plain, dry, funny at his own expense first. Phones, group texts, calendars, kids' activities and work reviews are his world. He uses "Honestly," "Okay, hold on," and "Here's my problem with that." No country phrasing, no preacher cadence, no deep-bass radio voice. He brings at least two of his own stories or arguments every episode and at least one real pushback.

**Age and timeline**

- Born in **November 1989**. He's **37** in every episode (see R2). He grew up in **Pasadena, Texas**, 15 minutes from the Battleship Texas at San Jacinto (fourth-grade field trip, a cannon pencil).
- **His dad left when he was nine**, on a Saturday in October 1999. Jonathon was dressed at eight for a fall-ball game, his first start as a pitcher. His dad loaded the truck with a duffel, cassette tapes, his fishing rods and the living-room lamp his mom read under. He said "Be good for your mom" in the hall, and the tailgate with the dent went down the street. His mom drove him to the game 20 minutes late with her sunglasses on. He pitched four innings and remembers no pitch. On air, "My dad wasn't around when I was a kid" (E007) is the short version, and it's consistent.
- At the first Thanksgiving after, relatives told him "You're the man of the house now." In fourth grade he threw away a glitter Father's Day card at the bus stop. For about two years after his dad left he played a baseball video game three or four hours a day at a friend's house.
- From 15 he had a pitching coach twice a week for two years: "Never let them see you hurt" (see R3). In college, 40 minutes from home, he drove back three nights a week. He hid a shoulder injury for two months, and his last season was at 20. Summers at 19 he worked at a car dealership.
- At 23 he sold extended warranties at an electronics store and quit mid-Saturday (the high five on the floor, "Ashamed"). He lived with his mom four months. His first sales manager was in Beaumont: the same tie every Monday, "Not bad, kid" (now retired in Galveston).
- At 25, his first month at #1 in the region, he went to a steakhouse alone, had three drinks and drove home when he shouldn't have. His twenties ran on Thursdays with four or five beers, visiting six churches and joining none.
- He married **Dani at 27** (2017) and bought a house ten minutes from his mom. He bought her ring in March, kept it in the glove box five months, and proposed in August, sort of. Marriage counseling in year three was his suggestion. Late in 2019 they moved to **San Antonio** for Dani's job, right before Ruby was born.
- In **2019 he came to faith at 29.** A customer dragged him to a men's breakfast, and it took him three months to text Wes ("the scariest text I ever sent a man"). His first year was big; then came a flat year after Ruby was born, "when it took." He'd spent about five years before that "getting ready for church" (E007).
- The **1604 years** ran about two years, when Ruby was about 3. He took Loop 1604 home at 6:15 on purpose to miss bath time, while they paid a sitter by the hour. Dani: "Since when do you like 1604?"
- His past with porn stays vague on air ("I've been there. Longer than you'd guess"). It started at 19 with a phone in bed. Dani knows. Wes became his accountability reader about seven years ago and asked, "What do you want me to ask you, and how often?"

**Work.** Regional sales manager for an HVAC company, the South Central region, based in San Antonio. He took the region about four years ago (2023). He's been #1 in the country three weeks running (fall 2023, a plaque in his office). He opened the Monday 8:00 AM regional leaderboard email first thing for eleven years, until he moved it to 10:00. His rule: if it can be fixed, fix it, and don't upsell a repair. The product "keeps people alive." He runs a Tuesday pipeline call with about a dozen men, moved from 5:30 PM to 7:30 AM in July 2027 for Dani's Tuesdays. His VP likes "Let's not make this a thing."

**His sales rule** (E007): "the deal doesn't die in the meeting, it dies in the follow-up." Also from E007: "I've got reminders for my reminders."

**His sentence:** "Win the room," written on the back of Wes's feed-store receipt, which Jonathon keeps. Dani says his real one is "Make sure everybody's okay." His heading: "Make sure everybody's okay, starting at home."

**Habits:** he texts Wes every Thursday ("Did you do it?", later his word for the week). He wears Ruby's cheap digital watch, which beeps on the hour. Phone jail at supper. His phone sleeps in the truck console from August 2027. The coach voice. "Bud" (his father's word), which he's working to stop saying.

## 9. Rulings: where the originals disagreed
