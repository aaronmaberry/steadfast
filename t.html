The book · chapter 1

A Quiet Tuesday

From Steadfast Men: The way of Godly Men. Wes Hart. Opening of chapter one.

If you had asked me at twenty-five to name the days that would decide what kind of man I became, I would have given you a list of big ones. The wedding. The first kid. The funeral. The job. The morning I stood in a river and let a pastor put me under. I would have believed those were the hinges. I would have been mostly wrong.

I have had all of those days now. Not one of them decided much of anything. They marked things. They announced things. The deciding happened in a long string of days I could not tell apart if you paid me.

A man’s life is decided on Tuesdays. I mean that almost literally. Not the Sunday he stands up in church. Not the Saturday he takes his son somewhere worth remembering. Not the Friday the paycheck lands. The day in the middle, with nothing special in it. The day he comes home tired and the house is loud and there is a conversation he could have or not have, a call he could make or not make, a battery he could change or walk under one more time. That is where the marks get made.

A man I will call Anthony left for work at 6:10 on a Tuesday in October. He had slept badly. His wife had said something the night before about money that was not quite fair, and he had let it sit. It was still sitting in the truck with him. He got to the shop. A screw stripped on the first job. Around ten a customer called to complain about something that was not Anthony’s fault, and Anthony was polite in a way that cost him. At lunch he ate in the truck and looked at his phone for forty minutes and could not have told you afterward one thing he had read.

In the afternoon his father called. He let it go to voicemail. He told himself he would call back after dinner. He did not. He finished the day’s work well. He is good at his job. He drove home listening to nothing, pulled into the garage, and stood in the doorway between the garage and the kitchen for a minute the way he does most nights, hearing his kids be loud and his wife tell somebody to sit down, not sure why he was not walking in.

Then he walked in. He kissed his wife on the side of her head without looking at her. Men, that is not a kiss. That is a fly-by. He ate. He got the kids to bed, more or less. By 8:40 he was in the recliner with the game on and his phone in his hand. Neither of them said anything about the money.

Nothing in that day would make a movie. That is the point. The mark he needed was Stop Waiting, and missing it did not look like a crisis. It looked like a Tuesday.

Week 01 · lecture open

Sit down. Then name the Tuesday.

This is Steadfast Men. My name is Wes Hart. I will teach all twenty-four weeks. Same shape every time. I name a day. I put a mark and a passage under it. I give you one thing you can fail at. Then you sit with two or six brothers and you talk.

Do not binge four of these on a Saturday. That is how you get a warm feeling and a cold house. Watch one. Do the work. Come back.
