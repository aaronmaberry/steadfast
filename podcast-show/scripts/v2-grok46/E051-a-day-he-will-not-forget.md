## SEGMENT 1 · The ramp

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on Saturday, because both of us were on a hill that had lumber on it. You first. The lot. The screws.

JONATHON: Theo dropped a coffee can of screws in the lot. An older crew man, seventy-something, knelt and handed them back one at a time for five minutes. Theo then handed screws to everyone all day. The widow is eighty-four. Her husband built the porch in the sixties. She had not been down the steps in two years. Her daughter gets her mail. About four she went down the ramp with her walker to an empty Saturday mailbox and laughed. The crew leader said we do not do pictures. No posts. No newsletter. She did not ask to be anybody's story. I wanted the picture. I did not take it.

WES: Good. Wanting it and not taking it is a hill. Ruby talked about every board at supper for twenty minutes. That is allowed. A child can tell a story a widow did not ask a stranger to post.

JONATHON: Theo prayed, and help the lady get her mail. I never got the screw man's name. The crew goes out again in July and I am going. I want the name. I do not want the photo. I am trying to want the right thing.

WES: We were on cedar the same Saturday. I told the creek story once at lunch, about two minutes. The youngest said huh. He cut till three. I dragged brush till two. I am not fifteen. I wanted the story to land like water. It landed like huh. Huh is allowed. Water can wait until August.

## SEGMENT 2 · The anti-day

WES: Tell the day that marked you with the wrong thing. I do not want a clean origin story for this.

JONATHON: Be good for your mom. That was the day. I was nine and it was a job. Then at twenty-five, first month at number one in the region, I took myself to a steakhouse alone on a Thursday. Three drinks I did not need. I told myself you made it. I drove home when I should not have. I wanted a day I would not forget. I got a booth and a lie. The sentence I did not get at nine was, you are not the man of the house. You are nine. I am still trying to give Theo that sentence early, in a better room than a booth.

WES: I asked my older brother at the barn, six in the morning, near the end of feeding. I asked what I am supposed to say on a morning like that. He said well. Another bale. Dad never did one of those for us. I said no. He did not. I said what am I supposed to say. He said one true thing. I said I have only got about one. He said I will be there. Then, Dad's loppers are on the wall in the tack room. I will bring those. He has kept them ten years. I did not know. That is a witness bringing a tool I had forgotten existed.

JONATHON: Your wife asked to hear the sentence first. Not to fix it. You agreed. That is a kind of witness too. A woman who will tell you if you rush the end.

WES: I kept writing what I wished my dad had said. I tore it up. I am writing from what I have seen in the boy. The shoulder of the road. The chain. The slope. If I write a speech I wanted to receive, I will miss the boy I have. Wes's book is at walksteadfast.com if you want the longer chapter on a day like this. We are not walking a catalog. We are talking about loppers on a tack-room wall.

[BOARD: One true thing is enough.]


## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: Joshua four, six and seven. Stones that wait for a question.

JONATHON: "In the future, when your children ask you, ‘What do these stones mean?’"

WES: The stones are not a party. They are a sign. When the children ask, you tell them what God did. A day he will not forget is a stone, not a cake. Witnesses. Words. Cost. If there is no cost, it is a brunch. If there are no witnesses, it is a speech you can edit later. If there are no words, it is a photo you were asked not to take.

JONATHON: I wanted a photo of a ramp. That would have been a stone with the wrong name on it. Mine. The verse is about their question, not my feed. I am glad I went home with sore knees and no picture.

WES: We're about halfway. The oldest came into this. I asked him Sunday about the headset. I also asked him to be one of the men. Tell me if I can say what he said. I am going to say it anyway, because it is the cost, and I will not hide it.

[BOARD: Luke 16:10 (NIV) · "Whoever can be trusted with very little can also be trusted with much, and whoever is dishonest with very little will also be dishonest with much."]


## SEGMENT 4 · You did not do one for me

WES: He said the headset was the same four guys since sixth grade. One of them is in the Army now. They still play Thursdays. Ten minutes about the friend. Then I asked him to stand with his brother in October. He said you did not do one for me. I said no, I did not. I am sorry. I offered one late. He said I do not know, Dad. Let me get through this one first. He will come, for his brother. That is a wound and a yes in the same mouth. I will not tidy it.

JONATHON: I wrote Theo's morning on the calendar. The August he turns thirteen. Eight years out. I wrote your name. I could not write a second. The date was easy. The men are the hill. I do not have an uncle who called on Thursdays. I have you, and I have a porch I am still scared to use a second time.

WES: The youngest's day is a Saturday in October after the first cold front. The barn at six after feeding. The men: the older brother, the little brother if Abilene lets him, the oldest. My wife hears the sentence first. I will practice it at the barn because I rush the end. The end is what it is for. I rush purpose. I can describe a boy. I get shy when I say what the boy is for.

JONATHON: I do not have the sentence for Theo yet. I have a date. A date without men is a party. I would rather have three men and a short true thing than a bounce house and a speech. I am not against cake. I am against a booth I pay for so I can feel marked.

[BOARD: October after the cold.]


## SEGMENT 5 · Pushback

WES: Push. The man who says this is for people with money and land. And the man who missed his older son's day and thinks he is disqualified.

JONATHON: A ramp in a church lot costs a Saturday and a coffee can. A living room costs coffee. A porch costs dishes. You do not need a barn. You need witnesses and one true thing. If you missed the older one, say you are sorry and do not use him as a prop to make the younger day pretty. Wes already did that part out loud. Copy the sorry. Do not copy a fantasy where the older boy claps.

WES: I am not disqualified. I am late. Late still has to tell the truth. I will not buy the younger a day that pretends the older got one. The older is coming for his brother. That is more honest than a matching ceremony I invent to manage my guilt.

JONATHON: The man who got be good for your mom thinks he cannot give a day without repeating the job. Then give a day that gives the boy his age back. You are thirteen. You are not the man of the house. Here is what I have seen. Here are men who will keep seeing you. That is the opposite of the job.


## SEGMENT 6 · This week's challenge

WES: Write one true thing you have seen in a younger man. Not what you wish someone had said to you. What you have seen. Read it to someone who will tell you if you rush the end. Put a season on the calendar, even if the year is later.

JONATHON: If you cannot write a second name, write the first and leave a blank. The blank is honest. Filling it with a random man so the card looks complete is how you get a booth. I am leaving a blank. I will ask Dani's dad when I have a second question that is not a performance.

WES: Last few minutes. Stones wait for a question. You do not have to explain the day to the internet. You have to be able to answer a boy who asks what this morning meant. If you cannot answer in a minute, you wrote a speech, not a stone.

JONATHON: I will go back in July and get a name. I will not take a picture. I will keep Theo's morning on the calendar with one name and a blank. That is my stone for now. A blank is a kind of faith if you do not decorate it.

WES: I keep seeing those loppers. Ten years on a wall I did not look at. A brother who stayed with the place kept a tool for a day we had not named. That is a witness. I want to become a man who keeps a tool for somebody else's boy.

JONATHON: I keep seeing a man on his knees in a parking lot handing screws to a four-year-old, one at a time. I want that name. I want that kind of witness for Theo when he is thirteen. I cannot buy it. I can show up in July.

WES: I keep wanting this to resolve in one conversation, and it will not. The assignment is the next honest sentence, said on time, without a speech attached. I have left too many good talks on a table because I wanted a better closing line than the one I had.

JONATHON: Honestly, I will try to add the speech anyway. Somebody should stop me. If you are sitting in a driveway doing that too, write the three sentences down before you dial, then read only those three. The fourth sentence is where I get cute and ruin it.

WES: I want this back in your house, not in ours. Pick the one door that is yours. Not your brother's. Not a man on a screen. Yours. Put a day on it before supper, even if the day is tomorrow and the sentence is clumsy.

JONATHON: Mine is already on a calendar. That is the only reason I trust it. A mood is not a plan, and I have used moods for years to sound wise while I stayed still. I do not want to sound wise. I want to be on time.

WES: If you fail the first try, the second try still counts. I have to say that every time or I will use the miss as a way out and then tell a story about the miss like the story was the obedience.

JONATHON: I used the first miss as a way out for most of my twenties. I am trying not to do that at thirty-seven. It is harder than any capacitor, and it is also more ordinary than I want to admit at this table.

## SEGMENT · Sitting with it

WES: I have watched men collect stories like this and never change a Tuesday. Do not be that man this week. Change one Tuesday. Then tell one person you did, because a secret obedience turns back into a mood by Thursday.

JONATHON: I am the man who collects stories. I have a notes app full of them. The only ones that count are the ones that got a day and a time and a person who knows I said I would do it.

WES: I want the listener who thinks this does not apply because his house is quieter. Quiet is not settled. You already know that if you have sat through a polite holiday and called it peace.

JONATHON: My house can be loud and still be a cease-fire. Volume is not the test. The test is whether the true thing got said to the person who had to hear it, not to a mug at this table.

WES: I will check this on Friday, which is the only reason I trust myself. A public calendar is a kind of mercy for a man who likes his recliner and likes to call the recliner wisdom.

JONATHON: I will hold you to it, and I want somebody holding me. That is why I say the time out loud even when it makes me look late. Looking late is better than looking peaceful and being dug in.

WES: A steadfast man does the small thing while it is still small. That is the whole show, said again, because we forget it by Wednesday and start waiting to feel ready, and the ready never comes first.

JONATHON: I forget it by Tuesday afternoon. Ruby does not. She has a better memory for what I said I would do than any review I have ever run, and she does not care that I had a good reason.

WES: If this feels like too many stories, pick one. One house. One name. One time. That is enough weight for seven days, and it is more than most of us will actually carry if we get proud of the list.

JONATHON: I will pick one. If I pick three I will do none, and I already know that about myself from sales and from home. One is how a man like me gets free of his own notes app.

WES: I have a habit of saving the true sentence for a better room. There is no better room. The kitchen is the room. The truck is the room. The porch after the dishes is the room. Say it there.

JONATHON: I have rehearsed in better rooms my whole adult life. The rehearsal is how I stay safe. Dani can hear the difference between a rehearsal and a sentence. She stops me when I start handing people a door before they are in the room.

WES: Some of you will try to make this spiritual so you do not have to be specific. Prayer is good. A day and a name is still required. Do not baptize delay and call it discernment.

JONATHON: I have baptized delay. I have called it being careful. Careful is sometimes fear with a pressed shirt. I am trying to tell those two apart before I waste another month.

WES: The man who says he will start after the busy season is lying to himself the same way I did in my forties. There is always another season. Do the small thing inside the busy, or you will never do it.

JONATHON: HVAC sales does not hand you a quiet month. If I wait for one I will still be waiting when Theo has a license. I have to put the true thing on a Tuesday that already has a meeting.

WES: I do not need you to feel ready. I need you to be on time. Ready is a feeling. On time is a clock. I have trusted the feeling too long, and it has made me late to people I love.

WES: Look at the next seven days and circle one hour. Not a vibe. An hour. If you cannot find an hour, you are not too busy. You are hiding inside busy, and I have done that with a laminated list and called it love.

JONATHON: I hide inside a full calendar better than anyone I know. A full calendar can still be a man running from one phone call. I am trying to leave a hole in Thursday on purpose.
