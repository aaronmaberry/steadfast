## SEGMENT 1 · Check the box

JONATHON: Thursday at two, a scheduled call, fifteen minutes. I had the sentence on a sticky note so I wouldn't decorate it. Then he explained that deals older than three years can't be reassigned in the system. Then he said, "Let's not make this a thing." An hour later, two lines in my inbox. "Thanks for the call. Let's keep this between us."

WES: You built that call up as the room. The room wanted the paperwork clean. "Let's not make this a thing" is a whole kind of man. I know that man. I've been that man.

## SEGMENT 2 · From the table

WES: We're about halfway, and I want a question men asked after Tuesday. Isn't this just blaming yourself for everything? Because I heard you almost do that in the driveway.

JONATHON: That was me last night in the driveway after the rep's text. The deal is my fault, the VP is my fault, the shoes, the cereal. I'm the problem. Everything is me. I sat there and I could feel the pile getting taller, and I almost called that humility.

WES: That's not what Tuesday was. Tuesday was find your part, not take everybody's part. Some men hear "it is not only what was done to you" and they swing all the way to "I am garbage." That's not humility. That's a different way of not looking at it straight. If it's all you, you don't have to sort it. You just feel bad about a pile. Sorting is harder. This part is mine. That part is his. This part is nobody's. It was a hard year.

JONATHON: So the VP shrugging isn't mine. Not asking the rep first is mine. Two different things, and I wanted them in one bag so I could feel worse and be done.

WES: The VP shrugging is the VP's. Yours is the ask you skipped. There is a kind of man this hits hardest. The one who grew up in a house where everything was his fault. Every bad mood, every fight, somehow the kid caused it. You hear "find your part" and your body says, I know, it's always my part. If that's you, your part is probably smaller than you think. What happened to you was real.

JONATHON: And if it tips over into "I am worthless," that's a different thing. Where does a man go with that, because I need a door, not a speech?

WES: That's not God's voice. If what you're hearing is "you're garbage," that's not conviction, and you should not sit alone with it. Tell somebody. A pastor, a counselor, your doctor. Today. Don't wait for a cleaner feeling to make the call. The feeling is the reason to call.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: We've got a little under ten minutes, and I want the Bible open for one prayer, because David already knew how to ask this. Psalm one thirty-nine, twenty-three and twenty-four.

JONATHON: "Search me, God, and know my heart; test me and know my anxious thoughts. See if there is any offensive way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting." I found that myself last night. Dani was asleep. I didn't ask her where it was. David is a king when he writes it. He does not search himself. He asks God to do it.

[BOARD: Hand him the night thoughts. Do not try the case alone.]

WES: When you search you, you either find nothing or you find everything. There is no middle. You're a bad judge of you in both directions. God is thorough, and he is kind. He will find the one thing that's actually there, and he won't invent nine more.

JONATHON: The middle part got me. "Know my anxious thoughts." Two in the morning, after the rep's text. Dani at work. Kids asleep. I ran the lunch, the call, the email, every version where I did it better. The two a.m. trial, and I'm the judge and the defendant and I'm losing.

WES: Those thoughts are loud. Loud isn't the same as true. Hand them over. You know these. You search it. I'm going to sleep. I have prayed that more nights than I want to count. Some nights I was up till four anyway. I wasn't up till four alone. The search is so he can lead you out. It does not say punish me. It says lead me.

[BOARD: Lamentations 3:40 (NIV) · "Let us examine our ways and test them, and let us return to the Lord."]

JONATHON: If you live alone, the voice in your head does not get interrupted. Tell one man what you think your part is, and let him tell you if you're taking too much. I texted my nephew that last night, because he is twenty-two and he is already good at taking the whole pile. He wrote back, "ok. one thing." That's a better theology than most of my paragraphs.

## SEGMENT 4 · Three questions

WES: Last few minutes on a tool older than both of us. The examen. Three questions at the end of the day. First: where was God in today? Start with the good. Always. If you start with the bad you will never get to the end. Second: what is mine from today? One thing. Not a list. Say it to God, short, no because. Third: what do I need for tomorrow? Patience. Courage. Sleep. Ask. Then you're done. Three minutes. If it's taking fifteen, you're doing the everything-is-me thing again. Where do you actually do yours, because I don't believe you wait for a quiet room?

JONATHON: On the drive home. Twelve minutes from the office. I turn the radio off at the light by the Whataburger, and I do the three, and I walk in already having said the thing, so I'm not carrying it into supper. Tuesday's good was Ruby and the cereal. She was honest with me. That's a gift I would have missed if I started with the shoes. Where do you do yours? And don't say a better room.

WES: I do mine with the lights out. My wife falls asleep in about ninety seconds, which is a gift, and I do the three before the house goes quiet. I'm not going to tell you she snores, because she does not, and I would like to stay married. Start with the good. Name one thing that's yours. Ask for tomorrow. Then sleep. If you can't sleep, you already handed it over, and that still counts.

[BOARD: Psalm 51:3 (NIV) · "For I know my transgressions, and my sin is always before me."]

JONATHON: And if you already said the sentence and the room shrugged, don't climb back into the two a.m. trial to collect a bigger feeling. The telling was the lift. The shrug isn't your god. Saturday you get on three rungs. I will text you. I want to hear the fan stop, or I want to hear why it did not, in a sentence a man can stand on.

WES: You'll get a sentence. If weather moves it I will say weather, and I will set the next morning. A click does not get to inherit the gutter's old calendar. The gutter is already done. This is the new I'll-get-to-it, and it has a Saturday.
