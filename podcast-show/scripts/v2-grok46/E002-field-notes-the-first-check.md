## SEGMENT 1 · Check the box

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on what we actually did, because Friday is the receipt. You go second. I already told the funny part.

JONATHON: You told the punch line. You didn't tell the part that sat with you in the truck after.

WES: So. The meal. Tuesday night I put the phone in the console before I walked in. I sat down at the table and I asked my wife the question. What's Tuesday like for you?

JONATHON: How did she look at you when you asked it like that, with no setup and no joke in front of it?

WES: Like I'd been replaced by a different man. She put her fork down. She said, "Why?" I said, "I just want to know." And she said, "Well, Tuesdays I take your mother to physical therapy."

JONATHON: Every Tuesday? Because I thought that was a sometimes thing, the way you talk about it.

WES: Every Tuesday. Since August. Forty minutes each way. And after, my mother cries in the parking lot because her hip hurts and she doesn't want my wife to see.

JONATHON: You didn't know the every-week part. Yeah, and— "sometimes" is how a man stays comfortable. You knew "sometimes."

WES: I knew she took her sometimes. I didn't know it was the standing plan. I didn't know about the parking lot. I've been asking how her day was and hearing "fine" since August.

JONATHON: So did you listen, or did you start fixing it before she finished the sentence?

WES: I listened for about four minutes. Then I said, "I'll take her next week."

JONATHON: That's fixing. I need you to say whether that's allowed, because I already know I did worse.

WES: That's fixing. I asked her if it'd help. She said yes. So I'm taking my mother on Tuesday. I'll allow my own fixing when she asked for it.

JONATHON: I'll allow it if she asked. Now the text, because that's the part you used as the hook.

WES: I sent it to my oldest, the one in the apprenticeship. "How's your Tuesday?" About ten minutes later: "Are you dying?"

JONATHON: That's funny, and it's also a report card, because that boy has been getting your texts for years.

WES: I've texted that boy a hundred times. Did you change the oil. Your mom wants to know about Sunday. Call your grandmother. I'd never once asked him how he was. The only explanation he could come up with was a diagnosis.

[BOARD: A report card in four words.]

WES: I wrote back, "Not dying. Just asking." Then he called me. Twenty minutes. He's thinking about whether to stay with the company when he tops out. He hadn't told anybody.

JONATHON: Because nobody asked him a question that wasn't a chore list.

WES: I asked him one more thing. I said, you're the only single man I can make do things. Do the single version. Lunch with his crew, phones in the trucks, ask one guy about his week.

JONATHON: Did he actually do it, or did he say he'd think about it and then go quiet?

WES: Thursday. He texted me after. "Phones in the trucks. The quiet guy, the one who never talks, has a baby coming in March. Nobody knew." That's the most words he's ever sent me.

JONATHON: That's the whole text, and it's more than most men will say out loud in a year.

WES: After he called the first time, I sat in the truck a while. My dad used to whistle one song when he fed cattle. Same one, every morning. I never knew the name of it. I caught myself whistling it, sitting there. I don't know what that was about. I'm just telling you.

JONATHON: [quietly] Yeah. That's the part that isn't a punch line. That's the part that stays.

## SEGMENT 2 · The second try

WES: Box checked on my side. Both boxes. Now you, and I want the first night, not the cleaned-up version.

JONATHON: I didn't check the box the first time, and I need that on the record before anybody thinks I walked in and got this right.

WES: Good. Tell it.

JONATHON: Tuesday night, Dani's on shift, it's me and the kids. I put my phone in the junk drawer. Felt very spiritual. We sit down, we're eating, and the watch beeps.

WES: On the hour. That watch has never once been on your side.

JONATHON: Seven o'clock. Beep. And I hear the phone buzz in the drawer at the same time. I'm a sales manager. A buzz is a customer. So I get up.

WES: You got up. You already knew what Ruby was going to do.

JONATHON: I opened the drawer, and Ruby says, from the table, "Daddy, you said." Three words. Seven years old. Worse than any performance review I've ever sat through.

WES: "Daddy, you said." That's the whole sermon.

JONATHON: Yeah, and— I can still hear those three words. And it wasn't a customer. It was a dentist appointment reminder. So the box wasn't checked Tuesday.

WES: So Thursday.

JONATHON: Thursday I tried again. Dani was off. Phone in the truck this time, like you. And I asked her the question. What's Tuesday like for you?

WES: What did she say?

JONATHON: She said, "Tuesday I get home at seven-thirty in the morning, I sleep from nine to two, and then I pretend I'm not tired until you get home." Ten years, and I'd never thought about the pretending part.

WES: Did you listen, or did you start shopping?

JONATHON: I offered to buy blackout curtains.

WES: Jonathon.

JONATHON: We have blackout curtains. She told me. She said, "We have blackout curtains. I bought them. You hung them." That's the worst kind of fixing. Fixing a thing that's already fixed.

WES: Then you shut up.

JONATHON: Then I shut up, and she kept talking. She feels like she misses half the kids' week. She finds out what happened at school from me, secondhand. I just sat there. It was the best twenty minutes we've had in a while.

WES: And the text.

JONATHON: I sent it to the guy who invited me to that breakfast seven years ago. The contractor. We still do business. "How's your Tuesday?" He called me. His wife had surgery last week. Nobody at work knew.

WES: How long has he run that crew?

JONATHON: Twenty years. Twenty guys, and not one of them knew his wife was in the hospital. So the box got checked on the second try, not the first.

[BOARD: Second try still counts.]

WES: Second try counts. Tuesday's challenge wasn't do it perfectly the first night. It was do it this week.

JONATHON: I'm going to need that in writing, because I will use the first miss as a reason to quit.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: We're about halfway, and I want the Bible open for one sentence, because Tuesday already handed us the verse. Luke sixteen, verse ten.

JONATHON: The little thing. The one that made 1604 sound like wisdom until it didn't.

WES: "Whoever can be trusted with very little can also be trusted with much, and whoever is dishonest with very little will also be dishonest with much."

JONATHON: So the meal is the little thing. The text is the little thing. The drawer is the little thing. Not the trip. Not the speech.

WES: Friday is just the receipt on the little thing. You don't get a new verse because the first night went badly. You get another Tuesday.

JONATHON: That's annoying, and it's also the only reason I came back Thursday.

WES: Look around your house this week. If the first try failed, that's not the end of the assignment. The little thing is still sitting there.

## SEGMENT 4 · From the table

WES: We've got about six minutes, and I want them on a question men ask a lot. We're labeling it honestly. Not as a letter we invented.

JONATHON: How do I start if I've never been the spiritual one? That was me. Dani's had faith since she was a kid. She sings alto. She knows where things are without the little tabs.

WES: And you needed the tabs.

JONATHON: I still use the tabs. I'm not going to pretend I don't.

WES: Here's what I'd tell that man. You don't have to know a lot to start. You have to start. Nobody at your house is waiting for you to become a scholar. They're waiting for you to show up at it.

JONATHON: Okay, but how, because "just start" is how I ended up with a whiteboard that said Family Devotions at the top.

WES: You did the whiteboard.

JONATHON: I bought a whiteboard. It's Theo's now. He draws dinosaurs on it, and they eat the word Family.

WES: Small and honest. Don't announce a program. Pray one sentence out loud at one meal. "God, thank you for this food and these people. Amen."

JONATHON: I did that. The first time I prayed out loud at supper, I counted it later. Eleven words. And Ruby said, "That was short."

WES: She's a critic. She's also seven.

JONATHON: Dani squeezed my hand under the table. She didn't say anything. She just squeezed. And I prayed the next night.

WES: Your wife doesn't need you to be her pastor. Most wives I've sat with aren't waiting for a sermon. They're waiting to not be the only one. Second piece: tell her. "I want to grow in this. I don't know how. Will you help me?"

JONATHON: That one's hard, because it sounds like I'm admitting she's ahead of me, and I already know she is.

WES: She is ahead of you. She knows it. You're not admitting anything new. You're joining her. Third piece: pick one book and read it on your own. Mark's short. One chapter at lunch. At supper, tell her one thing you read.

JONATHON: And if she knows more about it than I do, which she will?

WES: Then you learned something at supper. That's not a loss. Little things, done faithfully. It counts.

JONATHON: Two more versions, because I know they're sitting here. The guy whose wife isn't a believer. And the guy with no wife at all.

WES: If she doesn't share your faith, you still start, but you don't make her the project. Pray for her, not at her. One sentence at supper if she's okay with it. Let her see the difference at six-fifteen before she hears about it on a Sunday.

JONATHON: So no sermons at supper, and no making her the homework.

WES: The single guy has an advantage. Nobody's watching, so there's no performance. Read Mark at lunch. Pray out loud in the truck. Then text one man. His version of "tell your wife" is "tell a brother."

JONATHON: I prayed out loud in my truck for about a year before I did it at the table. Taco Cabana drive-through, a couple of times. The lady thought I was talking to her.

## SEGMENT 5 · The phone basket

WES: Last few minutes on a tool you can use tonight. I brought a basket from my kitchen.

JONATHON: Does your wife know you took the bread basket, or is that a conversation for later?

WES: She doesn't know yet. She will.

JONATHON: I want the rules, because if you leave this fuzzy I will put the phones on the table and call it done. Are you actually putting yours in first, or is that a rule for everybody else?

WES: Put the basket where everybody walks in. Not on the table. By the door, or on the counter on the way to the table. At supper, every phone goes in. Yours first.

[BOARD: Yours first. Then everybody else.]

JONATHON: Yours first. That's the part I will try to skip if nobody says it twice.

WES: If you're the one making the rule, you're the first one obeying it. Put a charging cord in the basket so nobody needs to charge anywhere else. My daughter does that with her second graders.

JONATHON: What about the real call? Dani's a nurse. If the hospital calls, it matters, and my phone thinks a coupon is the hospital.

WES: Set that one number to ring loud. Let everything else wait. A customer at six-thirty can wait till seven-fifteen. A hospital can't. You know the difference. Your phone pretends it doesn't.

JONATHON: And if you eat alone most nights, same basket, your phone, then eat like a man who's actually in the room.

WES: Look out a window. Pray. Call your mother after. That's a good one this week. It's a good one every week.

[BOARD: A pocket stays a pocket when it sits by the door.]
