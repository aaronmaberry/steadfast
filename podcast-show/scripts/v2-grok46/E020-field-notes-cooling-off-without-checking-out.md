## SEGMENT 1 · Check the box

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the lot, then the knee, then the card, because Friday is the receipt. Saturday, car-care. Eleven cars. Nine single moms, two brought a sister. The eighty-one-year-old mechanic did fourteen oil changes if you count the two he did for practice on volunteer trucks. A twelve-year-old boy handed him wrenches all morning.
JONATHON: A twelve-year-old boy handed him wrenches all morning. Yeah, and—then the mechanic gave him something at the end, didn't he?
WES: At the end the mechanic gave him a socket set. The boy held it like a trophy. The mechanic wants to do this every first Saturday. I made real coffee and I didn't run the lot. I told you I wouldn't. I almost did twice, and I put my hands in my pockets instead.

JONATHON: One of those cars had four bald tires. You told me on the drive. That's the smoke-alarm anger. What did you do with it besides feel holy about it?

WES: We found four used tires by Wednesday. The kids ride in that car. The heat bought rubber. That's the good kind. Then Tuesday I took Mom to PT and I got short with the therapist when she pushed the right knee. What it was guarding: I'm scared I'll end up like Dad. Never the same. I said that out loud to my wife that night.
JONATHON: [beat] You said that out loud to your wife that night. Then you still had Thursday with the therapist. How'd that go?
WES: I apologized to the therapist on Thursday. Mom, at the next table, said, "He's always been a baby about his knees." She was smiling. She was also not wrong. So I told the truth twice, once at home and once to the woman I had been short with. The second one was harder because I wanted to be the calm older man in the room.

JONATHON: I wrote three lines in my notes app the way we asked. First: Theo's shoes. Guarding: being late looks bad at work. Second: a rude customer email to one of my reps. Guarding: never let them see you hurt, again. I didn't send my reply. I called the rep to check on her instead.
WES: You called the rep to check on her instead of sending the reply. That's the second line. What's the third?
JONATHON: Third: Dani's comment on a spreadsheet. Guarding: feeling dumb about money. I said the third one out loud to Dani. She laughed, not mean, and said, "I was asking for a formula, not a verdict on your brain." I had built a whole courtroom around a cell.

WES: That's a good third line. Most men will confess the email and hide the spreadsheet. The spreadsheet is usually the real one.

JONATHON: Most men will hide the spreadsheet. Yeah. That's why I said the third one out loud, even though it made me look dumb about money.

WES: [laughs] She laughed, not mean. Would you have said it if she'd been coming off nights?

JONATHON: I don't know. I said it because the cell had become a courtroom. Then I used the twenty-minute rule twice. Once I sat in the truck and scrolled, which is the old way, the check-out way. Once I set a timer, walked to the end of the driveway, and came back and said the sentence. Dani used to follow me to the garage for about a year.
WES: Dani followed you to the garage for about a year. [beat] Garage arguments. Those are the worst ones. Why?
JONATHON: She said garage arguments are the worst arguments. Echoey. You sound bigger than you are, and you can't see her face. So the rule is: cool off without disappearing. Twenty minutes. Then you come back into the same room. You don't get a new night. You don't get the recliner and a screen.

## SEGMENT 2 · From the table

WES: This is the middle, and I want the anger question on the table. Is it ever right to be angry?

JONATHON: After the bald tires I wanted the answer to be yes, always, because I liked how clean that heat felt. After the spreadsheet I wanted the answer to be no, never, because I looked like a child. I need the middle.

WES: Yes. Some heat is a working alarm, not a personality. Jesus got angry. Paul said be angry and don't sin. Bald tires under kids. A man who won't stop putting his hands on people. A lie that's hurting a room. That heat should move your feet. It should not move your mouth first and your feet never. The test is what it buys by Wednesday. Tires. A call. A doctor. A card. If it buys a speech and a disappeared man, it wasn't the alarm. It was the horn.

JONATHON: And if it's the horn, the twenty minutes isn't a reward. It's a fence. I used to call the truck cooling off. It was checking out. I would leave, scroll, come back with a better argument and a colder face. Dani would still be standing in the same kitchen with the same sentence. I had used the time to load, not to name. That's why the rule has a return. No return is just abandonment with a clock. Dani heard that this week and answered, "Then stop taking the phone with you." She's right. The phone turns twenty minutes into a research project on why I'm correct. I left it on the counter last night and I walked to the mailbox and back. I came in and said, "I felt dumb about the spreadsheet." She said, "I know." Then we ate leftover ham. That's a better ending than a garage.

WES: If you live alone, the return is a text to a brother, or a walk around the block and then the three questions with the lights out. You still don't get to disappear into a screen and call it wisdom. The screen will take the twenty minutes and then the next two hours. I know. I've given it both.

[BOARD: Tires by Wednesday. Not a speech at midnight.]

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: Eight minutes left, and I want James we can walk. Chapter one, verse nineteen.

JONATHON: This is the one I need on a card in the truck, because my mouth is faster than my ears and my ears are faster than my feet. Do you actually hear "slow to speak" as stay in the room, or as go ice-cold until Thursday?

WES: Stay in the room. Ice until Thursday is gone, not slow. "My dear brothers and sisters, take note of this: Everyone should be quick to listen, slow to speak and slow to become angry." Quick, slow, slow. That's a pace, not a personality. You can be a sales guy and still take the first twenty seconds to hear her. You can be a quiet man and still be slow to the ice. Anger is last in the line on purpose.

JONATHON: I've used slow to speak as an excuse to go ice-cold. That's not slow. That's gone. Slow means I'm still in the room, still looking at her, still going to say the smaller word when the heat drops. Gone means I win by absence.

WES: No, hold on. Gone means you win by absence. Is that still how a sales week works when a rep gets a rude email, or only how the garage used to work?

JONATHON: Both. The garage was the old way. The email was this week. I didn't send the reply. I called the rep. That's the return.

WES: Gone is checking out. We're not blessing gone. We're blessing twenty minutes with a return. If you can't come back yet, say, "I need twenty. I will be back." Then be back. A man who says twenty and stays gone for two hours has lied. Put the lie on the list next to the juice box.

## SEGMENT 4 · The twenty-minute rule

WES: Last few minutes on the twenty-minute rule. Twenty minutes. You may leave the room. You tell her you're leaving and when you will be back. You don't take the fight to the garage to win it louder. You don't take the phone to load a better case. You walk. You name what it's guarding, out loud, even if only God hears it. Then you come back and you say the smaller word. Scared. Late. Dumb about money. Hurt. Then you stop. No because. If she needs more, she can ask. If you need a counselor, you call one this week, not after a nicer month.

JONATHON: We got this from a counselor in year three, when I was still the man who left and she was still the woman who followed. It felt stupid. It felt like a kitchen timer for grown people. It's the reason we still have a kitchen. I set an actual timer now, because I will negotiate nineteen into forty. Does that sound like a kitchen timer for grown people if I say it that way?

WES: [laughs] That sounds like twenty minutes with a real clock. Put twenty on a Tuesday that already has a meeting.

JONATHON: The beep isn't spiritual. The beep is kind. I come back on the beep even if I don't feel finished. Feelings aren't the door. The door is the door.

WES: If the anger is the kind that might put hands on somebody, you don't take twenty in the same house. You leave, you call a brother, you get help today. This rule is for juice boxes and spreadsheets and knees. It's not a stall for danger. Danger gets a bigger fence and a phone call.

JONATHON: I told my nephew the rule by text. He wrote, "twenty minutes of what." I wrote, "walk, name the fear, come back, say the small word." He wrote, "ok. roommate just ate my leftovers. starting now." I don't know how that ended. I know he started. That's more than I did at twenty-two. What did you do at twenty-two besides disappear?

WES: I disappeared into being useful. Knees. A lot. A title. That's why the timer still has to beep for me.

[BOARD: Proverbs 29:11 (NIV) · "Fools give full vent to their rage, but the wise bring calm in the end."]

Say one of them out loud. If you can't say it to a person, say it to God and then text one man. My son said scared. I can still see the word. I want that word in more kitchens.
