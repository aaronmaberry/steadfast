## SEGMENT 1 · Cancun

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the deal. I want the ugly version, not the cleaned-up one you tell a regional meeting.

JONATHON: I was a senior rep. He was twenty-three, first big district. Rooftop units, forty-one schools, six months of his life. I walked into the last meeting because I could close it faster. I put my name as lead in the system. I told myself I was helping him. I was helping me. We got the President's Club trip. Dani and I went to Cancun. I toasted a deal that had his fingerprints on every page.

WES: Did you believe the helping story at the time, or did you know?

JONATHON: I knew enough to keep it vague. I told Dani it was a team win. That's a phrase we use when we do not want to do math. Monday I took him to lunch. I said, "The district deal in 2019 was yours. I took it. I'm sorry." No because. He sat there. Then he said, "I know." Then, "I always figured you knew." Then, "You owe me Cancun." He smiled when he said it, and it still landed like a brick.

WES: He gave you mercy and a bill in the same breath. What are you going to do with the bill?

JONATHON: I don't know yet. Thursday I tell my regional VP. I have written the sentence four times. I'm scared of it. Deals that old may not even be able to move in the system. That's not why I'm scared. I'm scared of looking like the kind of man I have been pretending I'm not. I have a version of me that is the honest late bloomer, the guy who came to faith and now tells the truth. Cancun is the crack in that statue. If I do not tell it, the statue is the prize, and we just spent a Tuesday saying cheap prizes die by Thursday.

[BOARD: A team win is often just a rename.]

WES: Look around your house this week. If you have a Cancun, the first move is not the VP. The first move is the man you took it from. You already did that. The second move is not hiding behind "the system cannot fix it." Tell the truth anyway.

JONATHON: My sister would return a nickel. Same dad left. She's forty-one, Pasadena, and she would walk a coin back across a parking lot. I have used her as the good child in my head so I could stay the complicated one. That's mine too. I don't get to be the victim of my dad and the thief of a twenty-three-year-old in the same story without saying both out loud.

WES: Both out loud is Lent. Not only what was done to you. The part that's yours. I have spent a lot of years telling the fog story as if it were only something that happened to me. Some of it was. Some of it was me picking a game over a cafeteria. If I only tell the first half I get sympathy. If I tell the second half I get a chance to repent. I want the second half this season, for me and for you.

## SEGMENT 2 · The shepherd

WES: Mine is smaller and worse. December 2010. Fog years. My oldest was five, a shepherd in the kindergarten Christmas program. I told my wife I had a headache. I stayed home and watched a Monday night game. She knew. She said she could hear it from the driveway. I have carried that like weather. Sunday I called him. He's twenty-one now. I told him. He does not remember the program. He asked, "Was I a good shepherd?" I said, "I don't know. I wasn't there."

JONATHON: That's a brutal honest sentence. Most men would have said yes to make it go away. You gave him the hole instead of a patch. How did he take it?

WES: Quiet. Then he laughed once, the way he does when he does not know where to put a thing. Then he asked about the game, which team, which score, like he was trying to put a picture to the absence. I told him. I did not add a because. I did not tell him about the fog years as an excuse. I told him I was wrong, and I was not there, and I am sorry.

JONATHON: I'll be real with you, I would have built a whole theology around a headache. I would have told him I was protecting the family by resting. That's how I steal and then bless the steal.

WES: I have done that. This time I didn't. I want you to hear that it did not feel holy. It felt like throwing up. Then it felt like a clean floor. Not happy. Clean. I do not need you to admire the call. I need you to make yours. If you have a night you stole from a child, or a deal you stole from a younger man, the clean floor is on the other side of a sentence you do not want to say. Say it anyway. The desire to do good is already in you. Paul said that. Carrying it out is the part we keep postponing.

[BOARD: Do not patch a hole with a kind lie.]

WES: After I hung up I sat in the truck. I do all my important things in the truck. I thought about my dad in a hospital bed saying he was proud. I thought about a five-year-old looking for his dad in a cafeteria and finding a gap. The part that's mine is not that my dad was quiet. The part that's mine is that I repeated him, and I used a headache to do it.

JONATHON: Ruby jailed Dani's phone at breakfast this week. Dropped it in PHONE JAIL and said, "Mom, you said." Dani laughed and left it. That's the little thing still working. I tell you that so I don't only tell you Cancun. The house has a sheriff, and the sheriff is seven, and she is not impressed with President's Club.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: We've got a little under fifteen minutes, and I want the Bible open, because Paul already said the inside war. Romans seven, verse eighteen.

JONATHON: "For I know that good itself does not dwell in me, that is, in my sinful nature. For I have the desire to do what is good, but I cannot carry it out." That's the verse that makes a leaderboard look like a joke. I wanted to be the guy who develops young reps. I carried out the opposite.

[BOARD: Proverbs 28:13 (NIV) · "Whoever conceals their sins does not prosper, but the one who confesses and renounces them finds mercy."]

WES: Paul is not writing a villain origin story. He is writing as a man in Christ who still finds a war in his members. Lent is a good season to stop blaming the war on your dad, your boss, your schedule, and to say, some of this is me. Not all of it. Some. The some is the part that's yours.

JONATHON: Here's my problem with that. It can turn into a man eating himself alive. I already do guilt well. Guilt does not get me to the VP. Guilt gets me a dark kitchen and a second bowl of cereal. So if we say "the part that's yours," are we just handing men a new way to hate themselves?

WES: No. Romans eight comes after seven on purpose. "Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus." You name the part that's yours so you can bring it, not so you can wear it. Confession is how a thing moves. Shame is how a thing stays.

[BOARD: 1 John 1:9 (NIV) · "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just and will forgive us our sins and purify us from all unrighteousness."]

JONATHON: So I tell the VP because it's true, not because I need a beating. If he shrugs, I still told it. If he makes it a thing, I still told it. The telling is the lift. The outcome is not the god.

WES: That's the mark. Pick up the weight. The weight here is the truth about you. Not the truth about who left. We will get to chairs. Today is the mirror. I am not asking you to stare at it until Easter. I am asking you to look once, name one thing, and pick it up. A man can hide in a season the same way he hides in a fog. Lent is not a mood. It is a date on a calendar to tell the truth.

## SEGMENT 4 · Pushback

WES: Keep pushing. I know you've got more than guilt.

JONATHON: What about the guy whose dad did real harm? If I sit here talking about Cancun, a man in a truck whose father hit him is going to think we just blamed him for his childhood. I need us to draw that line in paint, not pencil.

WES: Draw it. What was done to you is real. It counts. It is not nothing. The part that's yours is what you have done with your own hands since. You do not confess your father's fists. You confess your own. A stolen deal. A skipped program. A cease-fire you chose. Those are yours. His sin stays his.

JONATHON: That's the line I needed. I have been using my dad's leaving as a hall pass for a lot of small steals. Not the big ones only. The small ones. Credit. Attention. The last word. I can trace a lot of them back to being nine and wanting to be seen. Tracing it is not the same as handing it back. I still have to put it down.

WES: You still have to put it down. Understanding is not repentance. Understanding is a map. Repentance is walking.

JONATHON: Second push. What if the other man does not want the apology? My rep said he knew. He did not ask for a lunch. I made the lunch about me a little, even in the sorry. I can feel that.

WES: Then you say the sentence and you let him own the next one. You do not buy him Cancun to make your chest quiet unless he actually wants that and it is just. You asked. He joked. You will not force a ritual on a man so you can feel done. That's collecting again.

WES: Third, for the man who cannot see any part that's his. Everything is her, or them, or the job. I would tell him to ask one person who loves him, "What do I refuse to own?" Then write down the answer without editing it. That question has ruined a lot of comfortable Tuesdays, which is the point. If nobody loves you enough to answer, ask a brother anyway. If you have no brother, that is also a part that's yours, and we will get to it, but you can still start with God in a quiet room and one honest sentence. Do not wait for a perfect witness. The witness is already listening.

## SEGMENT 5 · What we're actually doing

WES: We're about halfway through the last stretch. Thursday you tell the VP. What is the sentence?

JONATHON: "In 2019 I took lead on a district deal that belonged to one of my reps. I told him this week. I'm telling you because I don't want it sitting in the dark." Then I stop. I do not explain my childhood. I do not mention Cancun unless he asks. I do not ask him to fix the system. I tell him.

WES: Good. I'm writing my oldest a follow-up this week. Not another confession. A Saturday. He picks the work. I show up. I want a new picture next to the missing shepherd. Not as a patch. As a next inch. I cannot give him the cafeteria back. I can give him a man who shows up now without a headache story. That is a small thing and it is still the test. If I skip the Saturday I will have used the phone call as a plaque, and we are done with plaques that reset by Thursday.

JONATHON: And the fan. You put a date on it. Saturday the twenty-seventh. Your son-in-law on the ladder. You on three rungs because of the knees. I'm texting you Thursday. I want a sentence, not K.

WES: You'll get a sentence. If we miss Saturday it will be weather, and I will say weather, and I will set the next morning. I am not letting a clicking fan become fourteen months again. The gutter is already done. This is the new I'll-get-to-it, and it has a calendar square. I told my youngest I would get to it. He has started counting the clicks. A child counting your delay is a kind of mercy you do not want and still need.

JONATHON: I'm asking Dani for one night to keep going on October. We started. We have not finished. Easter is coming and I can feel the weather. I do not want another cease-fire with a ham on top. I told her I would pick the night this week, not after the ham is already in the oven. She said, "Good. Write it where I can see it." So it is on the calendar on the fridge, in marker, the way Ruby writes chores. If I erase it I will have to explain myself to a seven-year-old sheriff, which is worse than explaining myself to a VP.

WES: That is a better lock than a reminder on a phone you can swipe away. I am taking Mom to PT tomorrow. Tuesday is still Tuesday. I will not use her hip as a reason to skip the Saturday with my son. Both things have names. Both things have a clock. I used to think one hard sentence a month was enough to stay a decent man. It is not. The sentence is the door. The calendar is the house. If I only do the door I am still standing in the yard talking about how honest I am.

WES: There's a training session that walks this chapter if you want the longer version later. We are not walking the catalog today. The work this week is a sentence you already know.

## SEGMENT 6 · This week's challenge

WES: Last few minutes. Name the part that's yours. One thing. Write it in a sentence you could say out loud. Then say it to God, and then to the person if a person is owed. I want this small on purpose. A man will pick a life review so he can postpone the one night. Pick the night. Pick the deal. Pick the word. Leave the memoir for later. God can handle a long life. Your Tuesday cannot.

JONATHON: Do not pick your childhood. Pick your hands. A deal. A night. A word. A silence. If you cannot think of one, ask the person who lives with you. She already has a list.

WES: If you live alone, ask a brother, or ask God and sit still for ten minutes. The thing that shows up first is usually the one. Do not shop for a nicer one. A nicer one is how we stay in charge of the confession. The first one that makes your stomach tight is the assignment. Write it on a card. Say it out loud in the truck. Then, if a person is owed, call him before Friday. Do not wait for a cleaner feeling. The feeling is not the door. The sentence is.

JONATHON: And if you already said it and they already knew, do not demand a ceremony. Let the knowing be enough, and then stop stealing, which is the part nobody wants to talk about after the sorry. I can feel myself wanting the VP conversation to be the ending so I can go back to being the honest guy. If I take the next young rep's work, the lunch was theater. I told Dani that last night. She said, "Then don't." Two words. Better than a lot of my paragraphs.

WES: Stop stealing. That's the second half. Confession without a different Tuesday is just a speech. I can say I'm sorry about a game and then miss the next thing that matters because I found a new screen. Sorry is a door. Walking through it is the house. If you only stand in the door and talk about the door, you are still outside. I have been outside a lot of rooms I knew how to describe.
