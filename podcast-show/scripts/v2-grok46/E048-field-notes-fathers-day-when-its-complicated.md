## SEGMENT 1 · The trash can

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the day as it actually lands, not as a catalog wants it. You first. The card.

JONATHON: From nine to about twenty-nine, Father's Day was a day to get through. Work. Off social media. Then I had kids and I felt like a fraud, thanked for a job I had never seen anybody do. Ruby's card last year had a fish on it before she had ever fished. This year's card has Steve. I have seen the rough draft. I cried in the pantry. Dani did not come in. She knows that pantry.

WES: You made a deal about five years ago. Breakfast and cards in the morning. The afternoon is yours. A drive. The yard. She did not love it the first year. It is still the deal. That is a kind of honesty. A man who hates the afternoon program is allowed to say so without becoming a villain.

JONATHON: The tally is four a day now, all in the car. Hang on, bud. Theo said, Daddy, you said the bud word. He is the referee. I want a referee. I do not want to be the only adult counting.

[BOARD: A glitter tie can go in the can.]


## SEGMENT 2 · The text

WES: We're about halfway. The text. Say it without decorating it.

JONATHON: Sunday I will send my father Happy Father's Day. Period. The first in twenty-eight years. I have rewritten it about nine times. I decided not to send Ruby's fish photo. If he does not answer, then it is her picture he did not answer. Maybe the picture next time. I am still mad. Not yet is still not yet. I can send two words and stay mad. Those are not opposites. I will report Tuesday. I am not asking this table to clap for a text.

WES: Good. Do not clap. Two words is an ask and a blessing and a risk. Leave the photo out. That is a father protecting a child from a silence. That is not cold. That is weight.

JONATHON: Saturday I will call the nephew. Name first. He tells the truth when it costs him, the one in the morning call. I hope he becomes a man somebody calls at one in the morning. Then I will shut up. If I add a paragraph he will say do not make it weird, and he will be right.

[BOARD: The afternoon can be his.]


## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: Ephesians six, verse four. A verse for men who think Father's Day is only about being thanked.

JONATHON: "Fathers, do not exasperate your children; instead, bring them up in the training and instruction of the Lord."

WES: Do not exasperate. That includes making a boy perform a holiday he cannot feel. It includes rationing a name. It includes using a child as a prop in a text to a man who may not answer. Bring them up is slower than a Sunday post. It is a bath and a knee and a two-word text you send yourself.

JONATHON: I have exasperated Theo with a leftover word. I have exasperated myself with a holiday I pretended to enjoy. I want the instead. Training. Instruction. A name. A blessing that does not need a brunch.

[BOARD: Isaiah 43:1 (NIV) · "Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have summoned you by name; you are mine."]


## SEGMENT 4 · Bless a younger man

WES: Last few minutes. Tool is bless a younger man. Saturday morning I will call the twenty-four-year-old after his shift. The bus. The donuts. I hope he gets a car, and I will help him pick a used one if he wants. I may be wrong about the car. I will still say his name and one true thing I have watched. If he does not want a car, I will listen. I have been wrong about other men's hills before.

JONATHON: If Father's Day is the worst day of your year, do not pretend. Cook what your dad would cook if that helps. Wear his hat. Tell your kids one story. A man at Wes's church, his dad died in March, was told that and it did not fix the day. It gave the day a handle. A handle is enough.

WES: All week I have been saying my youngest's name first. He asked why I keep saying his name like that. I said because it is your name. He said weird. Weird is allowed. I will not argue when my daughter says the kind thing on Sunday. I will say thank you. She can ask me Monday if she wants the longer truth. I used to argue myself out of a blessing. I am tired of that argument.

JONATHON: If you cannot bless a father, bless a younger man. If you cannot send the text, write it and wait. I am sending mine. I may get nothing back. Nothing back is not a reason to skip a nephew. I will not stack them so I can fail both and feel deep.

WES: Look around your house this week. The day is allowed to be complicated. The blessing is still simple. A name. One true thing. No photo that is not yours to risk.

JONATHON: I will send two words. I will call a twenty-two-year-old and not make it weird. If I come back with a novel about my father, stop me. The novel is how I skip the nephew.

[BOARD: Bless without a project.]

WES: I used to let Father's Day turn me into a boy who needed a review. I am a grandfather-aged man this year if you count the years. I do not need a review. I need to say a young man's name after a night shift.

WES: I keep wanting this to resolve in one conversation, and it will not. The assignment is the next honest sentence, said on time, without a speech attached. I have left too many good talks on a table because I wanted a better closing line than the one I had.

WES: I want this back in your house, not in ours. Pick the one door that is yours. Not your brother's. Not a man on a screen. Yours. Put a day on it before supper, even if the day is tomorrow and the sentence is clumsy.

JONATHON: Mine is already on a calendar. That is the only reason I trust it. A mood is not a plan, and I have used moods for years to sound wise while I stayed still. I do not want to sound wise. I want to be on time.

## SEGMENT · Sitting with it

WES: If you fail the first try, the second try still counts. I have to say that every time or I will use the miss as a way out and then tell a story about the miss like the story was the obedience.

WES: I have watched men collect stories like this and never change a Tuesday. Do not be that man this week. Change one Tuesday. Then tell one person you did, because a secret obedience turns back into a mood by Thursday.

JONATHON: I am the man who collects stories. I have a notes app full of them. The only ones that count are the ones that got a day and a time and a person who knows I said I would do it.

WES: I want the listener who thinks this does not apply because his house is quieter. Quiet is not settled. You already know that if you have sat through a polite holiday and called it peace.

JONATHON: My house can be loud and still be a cease-fire. Volume is not the test. The test is whether the true thing got said to the person who had to hear it, not to a mug at this table.

WES: I will check this on Friday, which is the only reason I trust myself. A public calendar is a kind of mercy for a man who likes his recliner and likes to call the recliner wisdom.

JONATHON: I will hold you to it, and I want somebody holding me. That is why I say the time out loud even when it makes me look late. Looking late is better than looking peaceful and being dug in.

WES: A steadfast man does the small thing while it is still small. That is the whole show, said again, because we forget it by Wednesday and start waiting to feel ready, and the ready never comes first.

JONATHON: I forget it by Tuesday afternoon. Ruby does not. She has a better memory for what I said I would do than any review I have ever run, and she does not care that I had a good reason.

WES: If this feels like too many stories, pick one. One house. One name. One time. That is enough weight for seven days, and it is more than most of us will actually carry if we get proud of the list.

JONATHON: I will pick one. If I pick three I will do none, and I already know that about myself from sales and from home. One is how a man like me gets free of his own notes app.

WES: I have a habit of saving the true sentence for a better room. There is no better room. The kitchen is the room. The truck is the room. The porch after the dishes is the room. Say it there.

JONATHON: I have rehearsed in better rooms my whole adult life. The rehearsal is how I stay safe. Dani can hear the difference between a rehearsal and a sentence. She stops me when I start handing people a door before they are in the room.

WES: Some of you will try to make this spiritual so you do not have to be specific. Prayer is good. A day and a name is still required. Do not baptize delay and call it discernment.

WES: The man who says he will start after the busy season is lying to himself the same way I did in my forties. There is always another season. Do the small thing inside the busy, or you will never do it.

JONATHON: HVAC sales does not hand you a quiet month. If I wait for one I will still be waiting when Theo has a license. I have to put the true thing on a Tuesday that already has a meeting.

WES: I do not need you to feel ready. I need you to be on time. Ready is a feeling. On time is a clock. I have trusted the feeling too long, and it has made me late to people I love.

JONATHON: I wait for ready like it is a weather window. Then the window closes and I tell a funny story about almost doing it. I do not want another funny story. I want a clumsy sentence that landed.
