## SEGMENT 1 · She talked to him

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the receipt. Thursday. Your mom. The AC.

JONATHON: She let the man in. She talked to him. She called me at eleven. "He says it was the capacitor. He explained it to me. I understood about half." Then she said, "You can still come see me." Not should. Can. That word sat on me the rest of the day. I am driving to Pasadena Sunday, three and a half hours each way, with nothing to fix. I am going to ask three things before I mention the Bluebonnets.

WES: That is the assignment. Eat. Ask. Do not arrive as the guy with a tool bag. What are the three questions, or are you still writing them on a receipt?

JONATHON: I have drafts. How was watercolor. How is Bev, who is a lot. What did she read this week. I keep wanting to add a fourth about the unit, just to have a job. I am crossing it out.

WES: Cross it out again in the car. Now the confession you promised.

JONATHON: For about ten years, Dani has returned my mom's Sunday calls for me. Consistent. I would see the name, hand Dani the phone, and go find something in the garage. I told myself she likes talking to Dani. She does. I also told myself I was busy. I was hiding. I let my wife be the voice so I could stay the guy when it was useful and disappear when it was just a mother who wanted to talk.

[BOARD: Do not outsource the Sunday voice.]

WES: Look around your house this week. If your wife has been the switchboard, that is not a small thing. Thank her. Then take the next call yourself, even if you are bad at it. I have done the garage version in my own house. I called it giving them girl time. It was me not wanting to sit in a conversation I could not fix. Fixing is not the assignment on Sunday. Sitting is.

JONATHON: I already told her I would take Sunday. She said, "I know." She has known the whole ten years. That is the part that sat with me in the truck. She never made a speech. She just kept answering.

## SEGMENT 2 · The one who stayed

WES: Wednesday I told my older brother about the driveway. The Buick. Mom's hand on the roof. The sentence she said to me. He said, "Well." Then he said, "She never said that to me." Then, "I never left. Nobody stands in the driveway for the one who stays."

JONATHON: Oh, man. That is a whole second show sitting in one line. What did you say back, because I already know I would have tried to fix it.

WES: I said I was sorry. He said it is not my fault, it is just how it went. Then cattle prices. I do not have a lesson. I am not going to invent one so we can feel better. He stayed. He got the place and the hip and the recliner. He did not get a roof speech. I left and I got one. Both of those can be true, and I cannot settle it from this table.

JONATHON: So you just let cattle prices be the end of it. I would have written a paragraph. I am trying to learn your version.

WES: Cattle prices was his way of staying in the conversation without staying in the wound. I can live with that. I wanted a bow I could bring back here. He would not give me one. We're about halfway, and I want the other text on the table, because it is not in person and it should not be cleaned up. A young man checking in by text is still checking in. Do not turn it into a visit he did not ask for.

JONATHON: My sister's boy. Twenty-two. Pasadena. He texted. "the challenge thing. i said you. dont make it weird." Then, "day thirty six btw." I stared at it for fifteen minutes and wrote back, "ok." That is the whole correspondence. He listens. He is doing his own work. I am not going to show up at a game and make it a scene. A text is how he checks in. A text is how I answer.

[BOARD: Leave the extra sentence off.]

WES: Good. You did not make it weird. I would have written a speech and lost him. "Ok" was the grown answer, and it took you fifteen minutes to trust that.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: One verse. Paul, later in the love chapter, after all the poetry men stitch on pillows. First Corinthians thirteen, verse eleven.

JONATHON: "When I was a child, I talked like a child, I thought like a child, I reasoned like a child. When I became a man, I put the ways of childhood behind me."

[BOARD: Luke 2:51 (NIV) · "Then he went down to Nazareth with them and was obedient to them."]

WES: He is not insulting childhood. He is naming a change. Talking, thinking, reasoning. Then a man puts those ways behind him. Not his mother. Not his people. The ways. The part where someone else runs the capacitor and you stay useful so you never have to be lonely. Paul wrote that to a church that loved big talk. He is saying love grows up. It stops making other people carry your adult sentences.

JONATHON: So putting childish ways behind me is not hanging up on my mom. It is stopping the hide-in-the-garage move. It is asking about watercolor. It is letting her be a person with a Tuesday night class and a friend named Bev.

WES: That is it. Childhood in this verse is not age. It is a way of being in the world where somebody else carries the adult sentences. You can be thirty-seven and still talking like the boy who sat at the dealership desk. Growing up, here, is asking a question that does not end with you being the hero of the call.

## SEGMENT 4 · From the table

WES: We've got about six minutes for the question. My mom still calls me every day. Is that a problem?

JONATHON: I have a guy on my team. Thirty-one. Single. Very good at the job. Nice apartment. A girlfriend of a few months. His mom in Corpus Christi calls at seven-fifteen every morning on his drive, about twenty minutes, and has for nine years. He reports everything. He said he has never had a bad day he did not tell his mom about by seven-thirty-five the next morning.

WES: And he asks her whether she is eating, and he did not know her best friend's name.

JONATHON: He did not. The girlfriend thinks it is sweet and also says he sounds younger on those calls. Last Tuesday his mom called during a customer meeting. He answered, "Hey, Mama, I'm with a customer," and the customer said, "Tell her hi." This morning, seven-fifteen, he was laughing in his car. Then he leaned in my door and said, "Her best friend's name is Pat." Pat is seventy-three. Pat has a boyfriend.

[BOARD: Ask her a question that is not about you.]

WES: So the daily call is not automatically a problem. The daily call that is only a report, with no question about her life, is a boy on a phone. Ask about Pat. Ask about the class. If every call is you downloading your weather, you have not become a man on that drive. You have just found a new desk.

JONATHON: If she calls because she is lonely and you are the only person she has, that is heavier. You do not hang up and call it growth. You help her build a Tuesday. A class. A friend. Then you still call, but you call as a son, not as her whole world. I am late to that. Watercolor is already happening. I just never asked.

WES: First year of marriage, 2000. I called my mom from a pay phone at a used-truck lot before I told my wife a thing I should have told my wife first. She said, "Did you call your mother before you asked me?" Then, "Okay. Next time, call me from the pay phone." She has been the first call ever since. That is the other test. Who gets the news first.

JONATHON: Dani gets it first now, or she is supposed to. I still fail that. The garage is close. The honest order is wife, then mom, then the group chat. I have to say that out loud or I will keep sliding.

## SEGMENT 5 · The Sunday call

WES: Last few minutes on the tool. The Sunday call. Not a work order. A clock time. A few questions that are about her.

JONATHON: Put it on the calendar the way you put a review. Thirty minutes. Phone in your hand, not your wife's. Ask three things. Do not mention the unit unless she does. If she starts a job, write it down and pick one you will not take.

WES: If you live in the house, the Sunday call is a walk. After dishes. You ask, you do not inspect. If she has died, call the sister or the friend and ask what she loved that you never asked about. Write it down. I found a scholarship at a graveside because I waited too long to ask a living man a living question. Do not copy that delay with your mother while she can still answer.

JONATHON: I am doing mine on the drive Sunday, and then again at her table. I am not bringing a tool bag. I am bringing three questions and an appetite. If I talk about the Bluebonnets first, you can give me a look next Friday.

[BOARD: Eat first. Jobs later. Or never.]

WES: Tomorrow is truck Saturday twelve. Seven in the morning. I bought donuts. I would put money on the donuts. Then he is done, and I have to learn how to be his dad without a paper on the fridge. That is my version of handing a job back. I keep wanting to invent Saturday thirteen so I do not have to feel the quiet. I am not going to invent it. I am going to buy donuts and wave from the driveway and come inside when my wife tells me to.

JONATHON: Bring the donuts. Do not add a new condition in the driveway. You already wrote the paper. Let the paper be the paper.

WES: I came back through the kitchen to the porch after the last truck Saturday and I still had to lift the back door. Paper bag in one hand. Plane on the workbench. I looked at it and I kept walking. I am good at waving from a driveway. I am still not good at a jamb.

JONATHON: You bought donuts for a finished paper and you walked past a door that is still yours. I'll ask if the plane moved. Ok is allowed. Weather is not.
