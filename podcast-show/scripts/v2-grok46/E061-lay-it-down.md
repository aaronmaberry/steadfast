## SEGMENT 1 · The call

WES: Pull up a chair. Let's take the next few minutes on a calendar, because that's where this started, and it looks smaller than the verse.

JONATHON: Every Tuesday at five-thirty I run a regional pipeline call from the truck. About a dozen men. "Good week, Odessa." "What happened, Laredo?" That call has been my room for four years. Thursday I moved it to Tuesday at seven-thirty in the morning, before I knew if Dani gets in. The West Texas reps hate it. They drive mornings. I did it anyway.

WES: You wrote a speech.

JONATHON: Three red lights. I was going to tell her I had died to myself. Then I heard it. The speech was the paycheck. I wanted credit. At supper Ruby told a lizard story and I heard the whole thing. I did not give the speech. Dani listens to this. So, hi, babe. I moved a call. That's all it is.

[BOARD: Credit is not the same as a cross.]

WES: That's the whole show in one sentence. Men love a death we can narrate. We hate a death that looks like a calendar change nobody claps for.

JONATHON: Second year of marriage I was gone four nights a week for a month. I came home with two dozen roses. Dani thanked me and asked who was picking up Ruby on Monday. I had bought flowers instead of a Monday.

WES: At Ruby's birth you did the sleep math.

JONATHON: It came out me. I told myself Dani was a nurse so she could take the night. That was a speech I gave in my head. I now ask once a week, "How's the song?" On her shift nights I eat standing at the counter with the kids. That's not romance. That's a man getting in line.

---

## SEGMENT 2 · The letter

WES: I was thirty-one. Oldest boy a newborn. Daughter four. After a fight I wrote my wife a three-page letter. I said I'd die for her, twice. I quoted a song. I left it on her pillow. I did not get up at two. Morning she said, "That was sweet." Then she said, "Can you get up with him tonight?" She keeps the letter in a shoebox in the closet. I keep the memory of not standing up.

JONATHON: That's the roses in a different font. Ink instead of stems. Same dodge.

WES: When the youngest was born she had pneumonia in January. Three weeks I was up at two with the light over the stove. Nobody wrote me a letter about that, and I didn't need one. That was the death. It did not feel holy. It felt like a kitchen.

JONATHON: I'll push on that. Some men hear "lay it down" and think they should disappear. No hobbies. No friends. No opinion. That's not Ephesians. That's a disappearing act you can resent later.

WES: Agreed. Christ did not disappear. He gave himself. Giving yourself includes a body that still has a name. The test is whether she can find you when it costs.

JONATHON: Deer camp. You skipped it.

WES: About fifteen years ago her mother was sick. I skipped camp. Then I brought it up for three years like she owed me a statue. I had laid it down and then I sent her the invoice. That's not death. That's a loan.

---

## SEGMENT 3 · The visor

WES: Saturday I looked up her degree program. Online. What's left. Cost. Monday I printed it at the office. I kept it in the truck visor, then I moved it to my Bible. I will not give it to her unless she asks. I am not going to turn her sentence into my project.

JONATHON: That's hard for you, because you like a clean loop, and a printout in a Bible is an unfinished loop.

WES: It is. I'm leaving it unfinished on purpose. She said she wanted me to hear it. Hearing is the job this month. If I slide a packet across the table I am saying "What for?" with better stationery.

JONATHON: I want to fix Dani's Tuesdays with a chart. Bath, socks, supper, a show. She did not ask for a chart. She asked me to be okay out loud. A chart would be roses.

[BOARD: A packet can be another way to say what for.]

WES: Sunday in the church parking lot I griped to Merle about missing the Rangers' eighth inning because the television is off at supper now. Merle is seventy-one. He said, "I'd give a good deal to be told to turn off the TV." His wife died in February a year and a half ago. I shut my mouth.

JONATHON: That's the invoice again. You wanted credit for a rule she has been asking for since car seats. Merle would buy that rule at any price.

---

## SEGMENT 4 · The Word

WES: I've got a little under ten minutes, and I want the Bible open. Ephesians five, verse twenty-five.

JONATHON: "Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her"

[BOARD: Ephesians 5:28 (NIV) · "In this same way, husbands ought to love their wives as their own bodies. He who loves his wife loves himself."]

WES: Paul is not asking for a speech. He is asking for a cross-shaped week. Gave himself up. Past tense in Christ, present tense in a kitchen. The church did not get a letter on a pillow. She got blood.

JONATHON: "Gave himself up." That's hours. That's a call at seven-thirty. That's two in the morning. That's not a bouquet you can photograph.

WES: First John says he laid down his life for us and we ought to lay down our lives for our brothers. Same shape. Smaller room. If your death needs an audience, it is still a paycheck.

[BOARD: John 15:13 (NIV) · "Greater love has no one than this: to lay down one’s life for one’s friends."]

JONATHON: That verse is going to make a man think of a fire or a war. Most of us will never get that scene. We will get a Tuesday. If we miss the Tuesday we have not been saving ourselves for a better death. We have been refusing the one in front of us.

---

## SEGMENT 5 · Pushback

WES: We're about halfway, and I want the push. A man says, I already die at work. Why do I have to die at home too?

JONATHON: Because work pays you. Home does not. If the only place you lay yourself down is the place with a check, you have not learned Ephesians. You have learned employment.

WES: A man says, she doesn't notice.

JONATHON: Sometimes she doesn't. Do it anyway. I laid down a call and Dani did not throw a parade. I still moved it. Notice is not the wage.

WES: A man says, I'm tired of being the only one who gives.

JONATHON: Then say that to a brother, not as a weapon at supper. And check whether you have been giving speeches instead of hours. I was the only one giving roses. I was not giving Mondays.

WES: Last few minutes on what we're actually doing. I go in the kitchen door first now. I give her five minutes before the dresser. Every day. No announcement. If I announce it I have written another letter.

JONATHON: I'm laying down one unnamed thing for Dani every day. Friday's report is only yes or no. I'm not going to tell you what it is, because if I name it here I will want credit again.

---

## SEGMENT 6 · The challenge

WES: This week's challenge. Lay down one thing that costs you and does not come with a speech. A call. A chair. A Tuesday. Write yes or no. Tell one man the yes or no, not the essay.

JONATHON: If you're dating, lay down the need to be impressive for one evening. If you're single, lay down one hour for somebody who cannot advance you. If you're divorced, lay down the need to be right in one conversation with your kids' mom.

WES: Look around your house this week. The death is probably in a doorway, not in a journal. I have a printout in a Bible and a kitchen door I have been skipping for twenty years. I'm going in the kitchen.

JONATHON: And if you fail Wednesday, don't write a letter about the failure. Do Thursday. I already know I can fail a Wednesday. The question is whether I tell the truth about it on Friday.

---

## SEGMENT 7 · The kitchen door

WES: I want to stay on the door, because a man will hear a verse and look for a war. I have been coming in and going to the dresser for twenty years. Wallet. Watch. Change. Then I find her. That order taught her that my absence means I'm fine. Fine is a closed door with a polite face.

JONATHON: Dani learned the same thing from me and a laptop. If I sit down at the table first, she looks surprised, like I have been replaced. That's not cute. That's a report card I wrote with my feet.

WES: So the go-first is the door. Kitchen first. Five minutes. No announcement. If I announce it I have written another letter. She can have the minutes without a speech attached. I can stand there and be a man who arrived.

JONATHON: I'll be real with you. Five minutes feels like nothing until you try to give it when you are tired and late and you want the dresser. Then it feels like a cross. That's how you know it's the right size.

WES: My dad fed cattle every morning of his life. Christmas. The morning the granddad died. The morning I was born. Nobody wrote him a letter about it. He just went. I have been wanting a letter for things he would have called Tuesday.

JONATHON: I want a letter for a calendar change. That's the paycheck again. If I cannot lay down a call without narrating it, I will not lay down a year when it comes.

---

## SEGMENT 8 · Roses and math

WES: Stay on the roses a minute, because men buy things when they feel guilty. Flowers. A trip. A big sentence. Then they skip the Monday. I have done the trip version. I booked a weekend and left her with the laundry from it.

JONATHON: I have done the big sentence. "I'd do anything for you." Then I did not do the small thing in front of me. Anything is cheap. Monday is expensive.

WES: When I skipped deer camp I thought I had laid something down. Then I brought it up for three years. That's how you can tell it was a loan. A real death does not send reminders. A loan does.

JONATHON: I am trying not to send Dani a reminder about the seven-thirty call. If I mention it every night I have not laid it down. I have rented it to her as gratitude.

WES: We're about halfway through the thing I actually have to do, and I want you to hear this if your wife has a want that isn't about you. Her want is not a verdict. Your speech might be a verdict on your pride.

JONATHON: And if your kids are in the room, they are watching which one you pick. Ruby watched me hear the lizard story. That's a better sermon than any pipeline call I have ever run.

---

## SEGMENT 9 · Men who disappear

WES: Last few minutes on the man who hears this and erases himself. That is not the verse. Christ had a name. He prayed. He ate. He asked for company in a garden. Laying down is not vanishing. Vanishing is how you come back later with a bill.

JONATHON: I vanished into work and called it provision. Then I showed up with roses. Provision without presence is just a paycheck with petals.

WES: If you need a hobby so you don't resent her, keep the hobby and lay down the announcement. If you need a brother so you don't dump the week on her, call the brother. Dying for her does not mean she becomes your only friend and your chaplain.

JONATHON: That's the push I needed. I can turn Dani into my priest if I'm not careful. She is not my priest. She is my wife. She needs a husband who has a body and a friend and a Tuesday he already explained.

WES: Look around your house this week. The unnamed thing is probably already on the calendar, wearing a better name. Move it. Don't narrate it. Then tell one man yes or no.

JONATHON: Friday I'll say yes or no. I will not tell you the thing. If I tell you the thing I will want you to clap, and then we are back at the roses.

---

## SEGMENT 10 · What it costs

WES: I printed a degree page and hid it in a Bible like a man hiding a gift. It is not a gift yet. It is me wanting to be useful. Useful can be a way of not listening. I'm leaving it there until she asks. If she never asks, I still heard her.

JONATHON: I want to build Dani a Tuesday chart so I can feel ready. Ready is another speech. She asked me to be okay out loud. Okay is a sentence. It is not a binder.

WES: Merle would buy the television rule at any price. I griped about an eighth inning. That's how far I still have to go. A living man would like to be told to come home. I have a living woman telling me, and I made it about baseball.

JONATHON: I made Tuesdays about my fear of socks. Socks are not the cross. Being the man in the house while she sings is the cross. I can do socks. I have been pretending socks are the hard part so I don't have to say I'm afraid she'll love a room I'm not in.

WES: Say that to her. Not as a speech. As a confession. Then pick up the socks anyway.

JONATHON: I will. And if I fail Wednesday, I will not write her a letter about the failure. I will do Thursday. That's the only death I can offer this week.

WES: I want one more room before we go, because a man will hear kitchen door and think his house is already fine. The room is the truck visor. I printed a life for her and hid it like a prize. That is still me wanting to be the man who solves. Solving is how I said "What for?" the first time.

JONATHON: I have a visor of my own. It's a chart in my head for Tuesdays. Bath. Socks. A show I can turn on so I don't have to be present. If I build that chart I will have laid down nothing. I will have built a hiding place that looks like help.

WES: Then burn the chart. Keep the door. Keep the unnamed thing. Keep the yes or no. I have about twenty-seven years of practice at the other thing. I'm late. I'm still going in the kitchen.

JONATHON: I'll move the call. I'll skip the speech. I'll pick up the child on Monday. If I buy roses this month I will also buy the Monday, or I will not buy the roses.

WES: A man at my table said he would die for his wife and then he would not get up at two. I was that man. The letter is still in a shoebox. The boy is grown. I cannot get those nights back. I can get tonight.

JONATHON: That's the sentence I need in my pocket. I cannot get the first two years of marriage back, when I bought flowers and missed Mondays. I can get this Tuesday at seven-thirty, and the next one, and the one after Dani sings.

WES: Then take tonight. Kitchen door. Five minutes. No announcement. If she asks what's wrong, tell her nothing is wrong. You just came in. That's enough of a death for a Thursday.

JONATHON: And if she doesn't notice, do Friday anyway. Notice is not the wage. I keep saying that so I will believe it when Ruby is talking about a lizard and I want to check a score.

WES: I've got one more minute and I want it on you, not on us. Pick the thing. Put a day on it. Then go do it tired, and do not write a speech on the way.
