## SEGMENT 1 · The call

WES: Pull up a chair. Let's take the next few minutes on a calendar, because that's where this started, and it looks smaller than the verse.

JONATHON: Every Tuesday at five-thirty I run a regional pipeline call from the truck. About a dozen men. "Good week, Odessa." "What happened, Laredo?" That call has been my room for four years. Thursday I moved it to Tuesday at seven-thirty in the morning, before I knew if Dani gets in. The West Texas reps hate it. They drive mornings. I did it anyway.

WES: Wait, back up. You moved a four-year call before you knew if she got in? What did Odessa actually say?

JONATHON: [laughs] Odessa said, "Who died?" I almost told him I had. Then I heard it. I wrote a speech at three red lights. I was going to tell her I had died to myself. The speech was the paycheck. I wanted credit.

WES: Credit. Yeah. That's the tell. Did you give her the speech at supper?

JONATHON: No. Ruby told a lizard story and I heard the whole thing. Dani listens to this. So, hi, babe. I moved a call. That's the whole report.

[BOARD: Credit is not the same as a cross.]

WES: That's the whole show in one sentence. Men love a death we can narrate. We hate a death that looks like a calendar change nobody claps for.

JONATHON: Can I tell you the uglier version, because the call isn't my first dodge?

WES: Go.

JONATHON: Second year of marriage I was gone four nights a week for a month. I came home with two dozen roses. Dani thanked me and asked who was picking up Ruby on Monday. I had bought flowers instead of a Monday.

WES: At Ruby's birth you did the sleep math. What did it come out?

JONATHON: It came out me. I told myself Dani was a nurse so she could take the night. That was a speech I gave in my head. I now ask once a week, "How's the song?" On her shift nights I eat standing at the counter with the kids. That's not romance. That's a man getting in line.

---

## SEGMENT 2 · The letter

WES: I was thirty-one. Oldest boy a newborn. Daughter four. After a fight I wrote my wife a three-page letter. I said I'd die for her, twice. I quoted a song. I left it on her pillow. I didn't get up at two. Morning she said, "That was sweet." Then she said, "Can you get up with him tonight?" She keeps the letter in a shoebox in the closet. I keep the memory of not standing up.

JONATHON: That's the roses in a different font. Ink instead of stems. Same dodge. Did she keep the letter because it was true, or because it was pretty?

WES: [beat] Pretty. I hated hearing it that way, and it's true. Same dodge wearing ink.

JONATHON: I still buy the roses in my head. When the youngest was born, how many weeks were you actually up?

WES: When the youngest was born she had pneumonia in January. Three weeks I was up at two with the light over the stove. Nobody wrote me a letter about that, and I didn't need one. That was the death. It didn't feel holy. It felt like a kitchen.

JONATHON: I'll push on that. Some men hear "lay it down" and think they should disappear. No hobbies. No friends. No opinion. That's not Ephesians. That's a disappearing act you can resent later. How do you tell the difference?

WES: Agreed. Christ didn't disappear. He gave himself. Giving yourself includes a body that still has a name. The test is whether she can find you when it costs.

JONATHON: Deer camp. You skipped it. Then you sent the bill, didn't you?

WES: [laughs] You remember the invoice. Yeah. About fifteen years ago her mother was sick. I skipped camp. Then I brought it up for three years like she owed me a statue. I had laid it down and then I sent her the invoice. That's not death. That's a loan.

---

## SEGMENT 3 · The visor

WES: Saturday I looked up her degree program. Online. What's left. Cost. Monday I printed it at the office. I kept it in the truck visor, then I moved it to my Bible. I won't give it to her unless she asks. I'm not going to turn her sentence into my project.

JONATHON: That's hard for you, because you like a clean loop, and a printout in a Bible is an unfinished loop. Are you actually going to leave it, or are you going to slide it across the table Friday when the house is quiet?

WES: Yeah — I'm leaving it unfinished on purpose. She said she wanted me to hear it. Hearing is the job this month. If I slide a packet across the table I'm saying "What for?" with better stationery.

JONATHON: Hold on. I want to fix Dani's Tuesdays with a chart. Bath, socks, supper, a show. She didn't ask for a chart. She asked me to be okay out loud. A chart would be roses. Is a chart just a packet in a different font?

WES: Yes. A chart is roses you can laminate.

[BOARD: A packet can be another way to say what for.]

WES: Sunday in the church parking lot I griped to Merle about missing the Rangers' eighth inning because the television is off at supper now. Merle is seventy-one. He said, "I'd give a good deal to be told to turn off the TV." His wife died in February a year and a half ago. I shut my mouth.

JONATHON: That's the invoice again. You wanted credit for a rule she has been asking for since car seats. Merle would buy that rule at any price. Did you tell her you griped?

WES: Not yet. That's a speech too. I'm still deciding if the confession is for her or for me.

---

## SEGMENT 4 · The Word

WES: I've got a little under ten minutes, and I want the Bible open. Ephesians five, verse twenty-five. You want to read it?

JONATHON: "Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her"

[BOARD: Ephesians 5:28 (NIV) · "In this same way, husbands ought to love their wives as their own bodies. He who loves his wife loves himself."]

WES: Paul isn't asking for a speech. He's asking for a cross-shaped week. Gave himself up. Past tense in Christ, present tense in a kitchen. The church didn't get a letter on a pillow. She got blood.

JONATHON: "Gave himself up." That's hours. That's a call at seven-thirty. That's two in the morning. That's not a bouquet you can photograph. So which is it, a verse about dying, or a verse about a Tuesday?

WES: Hours. Not a bouquet. I keep wanting the photograph. First John says he laid down his life for us and we ought to lay down our lives for our brothers. Same shape. Smaller room. If your death needs an audience, it's still a paycheck.

[BOARD: John 15:13 (NIV) · "Greater love has no one than this: to lay down one’s life for one’s friends."]

JONATHON: That verse is going to make a man think of a fire or a war. Most of us will never get that scene. We will get a Tuesday. If we miss the Tuesday we haven't been saving ourselves for a better death. We've been refusing the one in front of us.

WES: That's fair, but— no, that's actually it. The Tuesday is the death. The fire is the story we tell so we can skip the Tuesday.

---

## SEGMENT 5 · Pushback

WES: We're about halfway, and I want the push. A man says, I already die at work. Why've I got to die at home too? Go ahead and push.

JONATHON: Because work pays you. Home does not. If the only place you lay yourself down is the place with a check, you have not learned Ephesians. You have learned employment. I don't totally buy that a regional call is a cross, and I still moved it. Does that make me a hypocrite?

WES: No. It makes you a man who did the Tuesday before he felt holy. A man says, she doesn't notice.

JONATHON: Sometimes she doesn't. Do it anyway. I laid down a call and Dani didn't throw a parade. I still moved it. Notice isn't the wage. Wait, are you going to announce the kitchen door if she doesn't notice?

WES: Last few minutes on what we're actually doing. I go in the kitchen door first now. I give her five minutes before the dresser. Every day. No announcement. If I announce it I've written another letter.

JONATHON: I'm laying down one unnamed thing for Dani every day. Friday's report is only yes or no. I'm not going to tell you the thing, because if I name it here I will want credit again. Can you live with not knowing?

WES: I can live with not knowing. That's the point of unnamed.

---

## SEGMENT 6 · The challenge

WES: This week's challenge. Lay down one thing that costs you and does not come with a speech. A call. A chair. A Tuesday. Write yes or no. Tell one man the yes or no, not the essay.

JONATHON: If you're dating, lay down the need to be impressive for one evening. If you're single, lay down one hour for somebody who can't advance you. If you're divorced, lay down the need to be right in one conversation with your kids' mom. What are you picking, because if you pick three you'll do none?

WES: Kitchen door. Five minutes. No announcement. I've got a printout in a Bible and a kitchen door I've been skipping for twenty years. I'm going in the kitchen.

JONATHON: And if you fail Wednesday, don't write a letter about the failure. Do Thursday. I already know I can fail a Wednesday. The question is whether I tell the truth about it on Friday.

WES: Look around your house this week. The death is probably in a doorway, not in a journal. Pick one.

---

## SEGMENT 7 · The kitchen door

WES: I want to stay on the door, because a man will hear a verse and look for a war. I've been coming in and going to the dresser for twenty years. Wallet. Watch. Change. Then I find her. That order taught her that my absence means I'm fine. Fine is a closed door with a polite face.

JONATHON: Dani learned the same thing from me and a laptop. If I sit down at the table first, she looks surprised, like I've been replaced. That's not cute. That's a report card I wrote with my feet. Are you actually going to leave the announcement off, or are you going to tidy it when she asks what's wrong?

WES: If she asks what's wrong I'll say nothing is wrong. I just came in. Don't hold me to a speech. Hold me to five minutes without a speech.

JONATHON: [laughs] Fine. Five minutes. No speech. I'll be real with you. Five minutes feels like nothing until you try to give it when you're tired and late and you want the dresser. Then it feels like a cross. That's how you know it's the right size.

WES: My dad fed cattle every morning of his life. Christmas. The morning the granddad died. The morning I was born. Nobody wrote him a letter about it. He just went. I've been wanting a letter for things he would have called Tuesday.

JONATHON: I want a letter for a calendar change. That's the paycheck again. If I can't lay down a call without narrating it, I won't lay down a year when it comes. Does HVAC sales even have a year that isn't a number?

WES: [laughs] It sounds like a man who knows his calendar. Put the unnamed thing on a Tuesday that already has a meeting. Or you never will.

---

## SEGMENT 8 · Roses and math

WES: Stay on the roses a minute, because men buy things when they feel guilty. Flowers. A trip. A big sentence. Then they skip the Monday. I've done the trip version. I booked a weekend and left her with the laundry from it.

JONATHON: I've done the big sentence. "I'd do anything for you." Then I didn't do the small thing in front of me. Anything is cheap. Monday is expensive. No, hold on. You skipped camp and then you collected interest. That's the part I needed to hear. How do you stop collecting?

WES: A real death does not send reminders. A loan does. If I mention the kitchen door every night I haven't laid it down. I've rented it to her as gratitude.

JONATHON: I'm trying not to send Dani a reminder about the seven-thirty call. If I mention it every night I haven't laid it down. I've got rented it to her as gratitude. That's fair, but— I already told her once. Once is a report. Twice is an invoice.

WES: We're about halfway through the thing I actually have to do, and I want you to hear this if your wife has a want that isn't about you. Her want isn't a verdict. Your speech might be a verdict on your pride.

JONATHON: And if your kids are in the room, they're watching which one you pick. Ruby watched me hear the lizard story. That's a better sermon than any pipeline call I've got ever run. Did your kids watch the letter, or the two a.m.?

WES: They watched the letter. They were little. They still learned which one I picked. I picked ink.

---

## SEGMENT 9 · Men who disappear

WES: Last few minutes on the man who hears this and erases himself. That isn't the verse. Christ had a name. He prayed. He ate. He asked for company in a garden. Laying down isn't vanishing. Vanishing is how you come back later with a bill.

JONATHON: I vanished into work and called it provision. Then I showed up with roses. Provision without presence is just a paycheck with petals. Can I ask you something hard?

WES: Go.

JONATHON: Are you going to keep a hobby so you don't resent her, or are you going to vanish and call the vanishing holy?

WES: If you need a hobby so you don't resent her, keep the hobby and lay down the announcement. If you need a brother so you don't dump the week on her, call the brother. Dying for her does not mean she becomes your only friend and your chaplain.

JONATHON: That's the push I needed. I can turn Dani into my priest if I'm not careful. She isn't my priest. She's my wife. She needs a husband who has a body and a friend and a Tuesday he already explained.

WES: Look around your house this week. The unnamed thing is probably already on the calendar, wearing a better name. Move it. Don't narrate it. Then tell one man yes or no.

JONATHON: Friday I'll say yes or no. I won't tell you the thing. If I tell you the thing I will want you to clap, and then we're back at the roses.

---

## SEGMENT 10 · What it costs

WES: I printed a degree page and hid it in a Bible like a man hiding a gift. It isn't a gift yet. It's me wanting to be useful. Useful can be a way of not listening. I'm leaving it there until she asks. If she never asks, I still heard her.

JONATHON: I want to build Dani a Tuesday chart so I can feel ready. Ready is another speech. She asked me to be okay out loud. Okay is a sentence. It isn't a binder. So what are you picking this week, because the visor is still a speech if you slide it?

WES: Not sliding it. Kitchen door. That's the pick. Merle would buy the television rule at any price. I griped about an eighth inning. That's how far I still have to go. A living man would like to be told to come home. I've got a living woman telling me, and I made it about baseball.

JONATHON: I made Tuesdays about my fear of socks. Socks aren't the cross. Being the man in the house while she sings is the cross. I can do socks. I've been pretending socks are the hard part so I don't have to say I'm afraid she'll love a room I'm not in. Should I say that to her as a speech, or as a sentence?

WES: Say that to her. Not as a speech. As a confession. Then pick up the socks anyway.

JONATHON: I will. And if I fail Wednesday, I won't write her a letter about the failure. I will do Thursday. That's the only death I can offer this week.

WES: Then take tonight. Kitchen door. Five minutes. No announcement. If she asks what's wrong, tell her nothing is wrong. You just came in. That's enough of a death for a Thursday. I can't get those two a.m. nights back. I can get tonight.

JONATHON: That's the sentence I need in my pocket. I can't get the first two years of marriage back, when I bought flowers and missed Mondays. I can get this Tuesday at seven-thirty, and the next one, and the one after Dani sings. And if she doesn't notice, I still do Friday. Notice isn't the wage.

WES: I've got one more minute and I want it on you, not on us. Pick the thing. Put a day on it. Then go do it tired, and don't write a speech on the way.
