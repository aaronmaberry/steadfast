## SEGMENT 1 · Check the box

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on what we actually did. I'll go first. I wrote the sentence on the back of the card. That was easy. My weakest is stop waiting, which nobody who's been at this table is surprised by. The gutter is done. The brothers aren't. The fan still clicks. And I told my son-in-law.

JONATHON: Your son-in-law? Not the Air Force guy? Because I thought that man already knew everything about you after he held your ladder and counted the seconds you spent deciding which screw.

WES: The Air Force guy already knows too much. My son-in-law is twenty-seven, works for the county, married my daughter two years ago. And I realized this week I've never once had a real conversation with that young man. We've talked about football. We've talked about his truck. We've talked about the weather a lot.

JONATHON: Rain's a big deal out there. I get it. But two years of weather is a long time to call a man family and never tell him anything true.

WES: Sunday after lunch we were out by the trucks, and I said, "Can I tell you something?" I showed him the card. Told him my weakest one. I told him about the gutter, and the fan, and the call I still haven't made. He was quiet a second, then he said, "Mine's probably the same." And then we stood out there forty-five minutes. My daughter came out twice to see if somebody was hurt.

JONATHON: Two men standing by trucks for forty-five minutes not talking about rain. She thought there'd been a death. That's the most words some families will say in a year, and they said them next to a tailgate.

WES: I asked my wife which one I do best. She said, "Pick up the weight." Didn't think about it. She reminded me of when her dad was sick, years ago, before we met you. I drove him to treatments for most of a year. I'd forgotten that. She hadn't.

## SEGMENT 2 · Except with your dad

JONATHON: Nightly check, two out of five. The card's in the truck. I'm not in the truck at bedtime. That's a design flaw. I took a picture of the card and it's my lock screen now. I look at my lock screen ninety times a day. It's the one note I'll actually see.

WES: I'll allow it. We said phones are where notes go to die. A lock screen is the one they can't hide from.

JONATHON: My weakest one is the pay one. I said that Tuesday. And I told the contractor. The guy from the breakfast. He's kind of my guy now, which I didn't expect. I told him mine, and he told me his. He said, "Go first." He hasn't told his crew his wife was in the hospital, because he doesn't want to look weak in front of them. He's sixty. He's run that crew twenty years.

WES: And?

JONATHON: He said he'd tell them Monday. I'm checking on him Monday. Then he told me he wants to sell the company in five years and teach welding at the community college. He's never told anybody that either. He's got a binder. Sixty, and he's planning a second career, and the binder is thicker than my whole follow-up system at home.

WES: That's how it happens. You tell a man your weak one, and he hands you a binder he has been carrying alone. A binder. At sixty. Then Dani.

JONATHON: I asked her which one I do best. She said, "Go first." And I said, really? Because I'm the one that's always talking about how I'm bad at stuff. And she reminded me, in year three of our marriage, I was the one who said we should see a counselor. Not her. Me. You told me to do it. I'm the one who said it to her.

WES: You went first. That's the one I would have missed if I was keeping score from the outside.

A man can tell the truth about a button and still leave the button alone until he has a date to decide. Pending forever isn't a decision. It's just waiting with a better story.

JONATHON: I know. I just wanted to say it out loud. That's the name of this one. I said it. It's still sitting there. Both of those things can be true at the same time.

## SEGMENT 3 · From the table

WES: We're about halfway, and I want the question men keep asking. Do I really need all four, or can I just work on the one I'm weak in?

JONATHON: In sales you coach your weakest skill. But you sell with your strongest one. Every day. So my instinct is, pick the weak one and ignore the rest until next quarter.

WES: Work on the one you're weak in. That's right. That's where your effort goes. But don't think you can drop the other three while you do it. They come together. Your strong mark is how you carry your weak one. My best one is pick up the weight. If I'm going to stop waiting, I do it by picking up something specific. The gutter was a weight. I stopped waiting by picking it up. Look around your house this week. Your strong one is probably already in your hand. The question is whether you're using it to move, or using it to hide.

JONATHON: So the strong one pulls the weak one along. That's actually close to how I run a team. You don't bench a guy's best pitch while you fix his slider.

WES: Usually. But here's the warning, and I learned this the hard way. Your strongest one can turn into your hiding place. I'm good at picking up the weight. So for years, when things got hard with my wife, I fixed things. I'd fix the sink. I'd fix the car. I'd drive her dad to treatment. All good things. And all of it was a way to not go first in the conversation we actually needed to have. I picked up every weight in the house except the one that required me to talk.

[BOARD: 2 Corinthians 5:17 (NIV) · "Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come: The old has gone, the new is here!"]

JONATHON: So your strength was your hiding place. A busier recliner. With tools. Mine's go first. So my version is, I go first on everything that's easy to go first on. The counselor. The scary text. And I use those to feel like I'm a guy who goes first, so I don't have to go first on my dad.

WES: You said it, not me. So the answer's yes, you need all four, and no, you don't need all four this week. Work on one. Watch your strong one so it doesn't turn into a place to hide. And don't panic that you're not all four yet. Nobody is. There's a line in Philippians that's carried me through this for twenty years.

JONATHON: Read it. I need it in your voice this time, because if I read it I'll turn it into a locker-room poster and miss it.

WES: "being confident of this, that he who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion until the day of Christ Jesus."

[BOARD: He started it. He finishes it. You show up.]

WES: He began it. He'll finish it. Your job isn't to be all four by Tuesday. Your job is to keep showing up while he works. You're not the one who has to finish you.

JONATHON: He who began it. That's not me. That's the best news in this whole Friday, and I almost missed it because I was still thinking about my dad's little blue button.

## SEGMENT 4 · The voice memo

WES: Last few minutes on a tool, and this one's yours. You brought it.

JONATHON: I'm a sales guy. My tool is my phone. And you hate this a little, which I respect, because you hate hearing your own voice, and I do too, and that's kind of the point.

WES: I don't hate the phone. I hate hearing my own voice. That's different.

JONATHON: The tool is a sixty-second voice memo. Once this week, you open the app, and you record sixty seconds. Not more. You say your sentence out loud. You say your weakest mark out loud. And you say one specific thing you're going to do about it tomorrow. When it's in your head, it's mush. When it's out loud, it's a promise.

WES: And then what? Because a lot of men will record it, feel spiritual for eleven seconds, and never listen again.

JONATHON: Then you've got two choices. You can send it to your one man. Instead of a text. A text is two seconds. A voice memo is your actual voice, and it's harder, and it's better. Or you keep it and listen to it once, the next morning, in the truck. That's it. One time. And you hear yourself make a promise.

WES: Do it. Right now. I made you write on a receipt. You can talk into a phone for a minute.

JONATHON: [into the phone, a little self-conscious] A steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's. My weakest one is God's pay. Tomorrow, I'm going to do one thing at work nobody will see and nobody will thank me for, and I'm not going to tell anybody I did it. Including Wes.

[BOARD: Sixty seconds. A promise you can hear.]

WES: [laughs, quietly] Who are you sending it to?

JONATHON: You. And you're not allowed to reply with a thumbs-up. You send the letter K, which is the coldest thing a man can send, and I need an actual sentence back, even if it's awkward.

WES: K means okay. I'll write a sentence. I won't like it. I'll write it anyway. A letter isn't a conversation, and I've used it like one for too long.
