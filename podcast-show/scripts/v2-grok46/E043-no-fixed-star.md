## SEGMENT 1 · Fort Sam and the back of the stone

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on Monday morning, because I was allowed into a walk I didn't earn. He took the cup with sugar and we walked. Flags on every stone. Scouts had done that Saturday. He stopped at the grave of a man he served with, died nineteen sixty-nine, age twenty-two. He said the name once, aloud, and stood about twenty minutes. Walking back he talked about the traffic on Loop four-ten, how they have ruined it. Then, see you Saturday. Car-care. I don't know the man's story. I don't know why I was allowed. I carried the second coffee and tried not to make it a lesson. Can you live with a walk you didn't earn?

JONATHON: I can if you can. You wanted an invitation in English. You got a time and a gate. That's more than most of us get from older men. I called Shane after I told you I had a half. He said is Ruby okay. I said I'm calling about you. He said that's weird, man. Twenty minutes on the model's anchor chain, stuck two months. Weird is how men say thank you sometimes.

WES: Before dawn I read the back of the courthouse memorial. Seven Vietnam names. Phone flashlight. Two share a last name. I looked them up. Brothers. I've walked past the front of that stone for years. The back is where I should have started. I don't want to keep walking the front of that memorial and skipping the back.

JONATHON: Austin bought the mini chips. His son said these are better than last time. He took the credit. I didn't correct him. A man who has been holding pancakes like a life raft is allowed to take credit for chocolate. I'm trying not to turn every kindness into a teaching moment. That's my worst habit.

## SEGMENT 2 · Abilene and a pecan

WES: Saturday in Abilene. Most of the day in the hospice family room. Deputy was allowed in and herded the nurses. A ballgame with the sound off. The father-in-law is still alive, barely. At the truck my brother said, you came. I missed the barn. First miss. Older brother said, cows will be here. That was the whole mercy. I wanted a speech. I got livestock.

JONATHON: You went. That's the heading for that Saturday, even if you didn't have a sentence yet. I keep thinking about headings because my VP asked for one and I gave him a trophy. I don't want to skip the pecan. You have a pecan.

WES: I was thirteen. I disked the thirty-acre bottom field on my granddad's old tractor, watching the disk. The row wandered. He walked out, climbed the step, and said, quit looking at what you did. Look at the tree. The pecan still stands. My older brother still lines up on it. At the barn in May he said, he told me that too. We got the same sentence and still managed to miss each other for decades. Do you have a tree, or just a number?

JONATHON: I go wild on the mound when I watch the runner on first instead of the glove. Same sin. Eyes on the wrong thing. In my twenties the wrong thing was four or five beers on weeknights to come down from the leaderboard. No fence. No tree. Just a number and a way to turn the number off. I don't drink on weeknights now. I still stare at the row I already cut.

WES: In my forties I had three church committees, a ball team, fifty-five-hour weeks. My wife asked where we were going. I said where do you want to go, I can do Saturday. That's a full week with no heading. I called it serving. She was asking for a tree.

## SEGMENT 4 · She wants to sing
