## SEGMENT 1 · Fort Sam and the back of the stone

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on Monday morning, because I was allowed into a walk I did not earn. He took the cup with sugar and we walked. Flags on every stone. Scouts had done that Saturday. He stopped at the grave of a man he served with, died nineteen sixty-nine, age twenty-two. He said the name once, aloud, and stood about twenty minutes. Walking back he talked about the traffic on Loop four-ten, how they have ruined it. Then, see you Saturday. Car-care. I do not know the man's story. I do not know why I was allowed. I carried the second coffee and tried not to make it a lesson.

JONATHON: You wanted an invitation in English. You got a time and a gate. That is more than most of us get from older men, and you still do not know the story. I can live with that if you can. I called Shane after I told you I had a half. He said is Ruby okay. I said I am calling about you. He said that is weird, man. Twenty minutes on the model's anchor chain, stuck two months. Weird is how men say thank you sometimes.

WES: Before dawn I read the back of the courthouse memorial. Seven Vietnam names. Phone flashlight. Two share a last name. I looked them up. Brothers. I have walked past the front of that stone for years. The back is where I should have started. I do not want to be a front-only man anymore.

JONATHON: Austin bought the mini chips. His son said these are better than last time. He took the credit. I did not correct him. A man who has been holding pancakes like a life raft is allowed to take credit for chocolate. I am trying not to turn every kindness into a teaching moment. That is my worst habit.

[BOARD: The floor teaches first.]


## SEGMENT 2 · Abilene and a pecan

WES: Saturday in Abilene. Most of the day in the hospice family room. Deputy was allowed in and herded the nurses. A ballgame with the sound off. The father-in-law is still alive, barely. At the truck my brother said, you came. I missed the barn. First miss. Older brother said, cows will be here. That was the whole mercy. I wanted a speech. I got livestock.

JONATHON: You went. That is the heading for that Saturday, even if you did not have a sentence yet. I keep thinking about headings because my VP asked for one and I gave him a trophy. I do not want to skip the pecan. You have a pecan.

WES: I was thirteen. I disked the thirty-acre bottom field on my granddad's old tractor, watching the disk. The row wandered. He walked out, climbed the step, and said, quit looking at what you did. Look at the tree. The pecan still stands. My older brother still lines up on it. At the barn in May he said, he told me that too. We got the same sentence and still managed to miss each other for decades.

JONATHON: I go wild on the mound when I watch the runner on first instead of the glove. Same sin. Eyes on the wrong thing. In my twenties the wrong thing was four or five beers on weeknights to come down from the leaderboard. No fence. No tree. Just a number and a way to turn the number off. I do not drink on weeknights now. I still stare at the row I already cut.

WES: In my forties I had three church committees, a ball team, fifty-five-hour weeks. My wife asked where we were going. I said where do you want to go, I can do Saturday. That is a full week with no heading. I called it serving. She was asking for a tree.

[BOARD: Look toward the pecan.]


## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: Proverbs twenty-nine, verse eighteen. People like the first half. I want both halves.

JONATHON: "Where there is no revelation, people cast off restraint; but blessed is the one who heeds wisdom’s instruction."

WES: No revelation, no restraint. A full calendar can still be a man with no fence. Blessed is not the man with the most boards. Blessed is the one who heeds. A heading is a thing you heed, not a thing you announce. We're about halfway. I want your heading in a sentence, not in a slide.

JONATHON: The VP asked for one sentence, my region three years out. I wrote number one in the South Central region by Q4, and stay there. Second version adds, and every rep on my team takes all their vacation days. He said interesting. Interesting is not a blessing. It is a man hearing a number and a conscience in the same line and not knowing which one I meant.

WES: Which one did you mean.

JONATHON: Both, which means I had no tree. I had two rows. I stared at both and wandered. Dani heard me talking about it and then she said something that was not about my job at all.

[BOARD: Habakkuk 2:2 (NIV) · "Write down the revelation and make it plain on tablets so that a herald may run with it."]


## SEGMENT 4 · She wants to sing

WES: Tell it the way she gave you permission. Not as a project you closed.

JONATHON: She said I want to sing again. Alto in church choir from girlhood through college. She sang one verse at our wedding before she cried. She has not sung in front of anyone since Theo was born. There is a community chorale downtown. Auditions in August. Rehearsals Tuesday nights. She works Tuesdays. She has had it bookmarked for a year. She said there is never going to be a better season. I started fixing the Tuesday nights. She said stop. I do not need you to solve it tonight. I just wanted you to know I want it. I can say she wants it. I cannot say she is doing it. That is her line, on the air, with permission.

WES: Good. You tried to be the heading for her life. She asked you to hear a want. Hearing is a heading. Fixing is a wander. I have wandered through a lot of my wife's sentences with a toolbox. It does not make me a leader. It makes me loud.

JONATHON: I wrote my heading on one of her yellow sticky notes. Make sure everybody is okay, starting at home. She said okay. Then, starting when. Then do the dishes. That is the most accurate vision review I have ever had. No slide. A sink.

WES: Mine is on the back of an index card under the prayer list. Love God out loud, keep the people He gave me, and finish. Out loud costs me. I still pass on praying out loud at the Thursday table. I am not going to pretend a card fixed my mouth. The card is the tree. My mouth is still the row.

[BOARD: A number is not a north.]


## SEGMENT 5 · Pushback

WES: Push. The man who says vision is for executives. And the man who has a slogan on a wall and a house with no heading.

JONATHON: If you are not an executive you still have a home. A heading can be, I am home for bath three nights. It can be, I call my mom and ask about her first. It can be, I do not plan my wife's Tuesday nights. That last one is on my page now. Dani said she is going to hold me to it. I want to be held to it.

WES: A slogan on a wall is how men hide. We had a mission statement at work I could not have quoted if you paid me. I could quote the pecan. I could not quote a heading for my own house until I wrote it on a card. If your wall is louder than your porch, you have decoration.

JONATHON: I steered by the Monday leaderboard. I checked Austin's number before my own. That is a heading I never wrote down and obeyed anyway. Written is better, because then somebody else can see it and tell you when you wander.

WES: I steered by whoever called last. That is how you get three committees and a wife asking where you are going. Last few minutes I want a sentence, not a defense of your busyness. Busyness is the wander. The tree is the sentence.


## SEGMENT 6 · This week's challenge

WES: Write one heading in a sentence. Say it to one person who lives with you. Do not make it a slogan. If you cannot say it on a porch, it is not ready, or you are not. Either way, write it.

JONATHON: I already said mine at breakfast. Ruby asked if starting at home counts the dog. I said we do not have a dog. She said yet. Then she wrote her own. I will let Friday hold that. Today I want the listener to write his, not steal ours.

WES: If you have been lining up on a row you already cut, pick a tree. A pecan. A card. A sticky note. Look at that when the week gets loud. The loud is not the heading. The loud is the disk.

JONATHON: If you start fixing your wife's want, stop. Hear it. Write yours. Do the dishes. That is how a heading gets into a house instead of into a speech.

WES: I still do not know why the mechanic let me walk. I do not need to know. I need to show up Saturday with donuts and not ask him to make it a story. Some gifts die if you interview them.

WES: I keep wanting this to resolve in one conversation, and it will not. The assignment is the next honest sentence, said on time, without a speech attached. I have left too many good talks on a table because I wanted a better closing line than the one I had.

JONATHON: Honestly, I will try to add the speech anyway. Somebody should stop me. If you are sitting in a driveway doing that too, write the three sentences down before you dial, then read only those three. The fourth sentence is where I get cute and ruin it.

WES: I want this back in your house, not in ours. Pick the one door that is yours. Not your brother's. Not a man on a screen. Yours. Put a day on it before supper, even if the day is tomorrow and the sentence is clumsy.

JONATHON: Mine is already on a calendar. That is the only reason I trust it. A mood is not a plan, and I have used moods for years to sound wise while I stayed still. I do not want to sound wise. I want to be on time.

WES: If you fail the first try, the second try still counts. I have to say that every time or I will use the miss as a way out and then tell a story about the miss like the story was the obedience.

JONATHON: I used the first miss as a way out for most of my twenties. I am trying not to do that at thirty-seven. It is harder than any capacitor, and it is also more ordinary than I want to admit at this table.

WES: I have watched men collect stories like this and never change a Tuesday. Do not be that man this week. Change one Tuesday. Then tell one person you did, because a secret obedience turns back into a mood by Thursday.

JONATHON: I am the man who collects stories. I have a notes app full of them. The only ones that count are the ones that got a day and a time and a person who knows I said I would do it.

## SEGMENT · Sitting with it

WES: I want the listener who thinks this does not apply because his house is quieter. Quiet is not settled. You already know that if you have sat through a polite holiday and called it peace.

WES: I will check this on Friday, which is the only reason I trust myself. A public calendar is a kind of mercy for a man who likes his recliner and likes to call the recliner wisdom.

JONATHON: I will hold you to it, and I want somebody holding me. That is why I say the time out loud even when it makes me look late. Looking late is better than looking peaceful and being dug in.

WES: A steadfast man does the small thing while it is still small. That is the whole show, said again, because we forget it by Wednesday and start waiting to feel ready, and the ready never comes first.

JONATHON: I forget it by Tuesday afternoon. Ruby does not. She has a better memory for what I said I would do than any review I have ever run, and she does not care that I had a good reason.

WES: If this feels like too many stories, pick one. One house. One name. One time. That is enough weight for seven days, and it is more than most of us will actually carry if we get proud of the list.

JONATHON: I will pick one. If I pick three I will do none, and I already know that about myself from sales and from home. One is how a man like me gets free of his own notes app.

WES: I have a habit of saving the true sentence for a better room. There is no better room. The kitchen is the room. The truck is the room. The porch after the dishes is the room. Say it there.

JONATHON: I have rehearsed in better rooms my whole adult life. The rehearsal is how I stay safe. Dani can hear the difference between a rehearsal and a sentence. She stops me when I start handing people a door before they are in the room.

WES: Some of you will try to make this spiritual so you do not have to be specific. Prayer is good. A day and a name is still required. Do not baptize delay and call it discernment.

JONATHON: I have baptized delay. I have called it being careful. Careful is sometimes fear with a pressed shirt. I am trying to tell those two apart before I waste another month.

WES: The man who says he will start after the busy season is lying to himself the same way I did in my forties. There is always another season. Do the small thing inside the busy, or you will never do it.

JONATHON: HVAC sales does not hand you a quiet month. If I wait for one I will still be waiting when Theo has a license. I have to put the true thing on a Tuesday that already has a meeting.

WES: I do not need you to feel ready. I need you to be on time. Ready is a feeling. On time is a clock. I have trusted the feeling too long, and it has made me late to people I love.

JONATHON: I wait for ready like it is a weather window. Then the window closes and I tell a funny story about almost doing it. I do not want another funny story. I want a clumsy sentence that landed.

WES: Look at the next seven days and circle one hour. Not a vibe. An hour. If you cannot find an hour, you are not too busy. You are hiding inside busy, and I have done that with a laminated list and called it love.

WES: If your people are used to you disappearing into a screen after supper, tell them you are going to be late to the recliner this week. Then be late to the recliner. That is a kind of going first nobody applauds.

JONATHON: Theo notices the recliner more than he notices my speeches. If I stay at the table ten extra minutes he treats it like a holiday. That is how small this has to be to matter.

WES: I will not give you a poem. I will give you a time. If you need a poem after you have been on time, fine. Most of us want the poem first so we can skip the time.

JONATHON: I want the poem first. I always have. Then I get cute and the time slips. If I write the time on a sticky note where Dani can see it, I am much less poetic and much more likely to go.
