## SEGMENT 1 · The coach voice

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the first shape, because a four-year-old already named it. Coach-pitch is back. You first.

JONATHON: The Bluebonnets. Zero and six last fall. Undefeated in losing. Saturday was the first practice. Twelve kids, a bucket of balls, and me. I learned every name and one fact ahead of the first throw. Harper has a lizard. One of the twins is scared of the ball. The other one isn't. A boy cried because his glove was the wrong color, and I sat him on a bucket and talked him down in about ninety seconds. I'm loud and fun and everybody gets a high five. I can feel the parents thinking I'm the best dad on the field.

WES: Then you went home.

JONATHON: Noon. Couch. Phone. Somebody pulled a plug. About two o'clock Theo comes over with his glove. He is four. He's not on the team. He just wants to throw. I said, "Not now, buddy," without looking up. He stood there and asked why I only do the coach voice at practice. Dani said from the kitchen, "Daddy's tired, bud. He coached all morning." She was covering. She was kind. She was wrong. I wasn't tired. I would have coached two more hours if somebody in the bleachers had asked. I was done performing. There was nobody in the bleachers in my living room.

WES: That's skip the house. It almost never looks like a man who is never home. You were home. You skipped it from four feet away. You had plenty of energy. You spent it where there was an audience.

JONATHON: I bring that voice to sales meetings. I bring it to the men's breakfast. I bring it to my VP. The people who get the leftovers are two kids under four feet and Dani when she is awake. I know when it started. I was nine. The week my dad left, my mom said I was the man of the house. I figured out that if I was loud and funny and on, she would laugh, and the house felt less empty. So I was on. For her. I can feel that being a because. I'm not using it. It's where it started. It's not why I said not now to Theo.

WES: That's the last sentence. Keep it. Sunday you did something different. Tell that too, because I don't want a man to hear the couch and quit.

JONATHON: Sunday I did twenty minutes of catch in the backyard with the full coach voice. Theo threw the ball over the fence and we knocked on the neighbor's door to get it. He thought that was the best part. Monday I was back on the couch. So I'm not a new man. I'm a man who did one contrary afternoon and then sat down again. That's still the assignment. One afternoon. Not a personality transplant.

[BOARD: The leftover version is the one they actually live with.]

WES: I was on three rungs Saturday morning before your practice, holding a bracket, because the fan still had a date. We will tell you Friday how that went. I'm saying it so you know the house got a morning from me before the rest of the week tried to take it back. Skip the house can look like a man who is busy with a good project and still missing the person in the room.

## SEGMENT 2 · The title

WES: Mine is the third shape. Wearing the job as your name. I didn't know it was mine until they took the name off me. 2019. I'm forty-four. Same title nine years. Tuesday, a conference room, a man from out of town, a slide with boxes. Same pay. Different desk. My box is gone. I report to a man I used to sit next to.

JONATHON: Same pay, though. On paper nothing happened. That's the kind of loss a man can hide because there is no funeral and no story that sounds big enough.

WES: I hid it. I drove home and I didn't tell my wife. Three weeks I went to the new desk and came home and said fine at supper. If I wasn't that title I didn't know what to say at the table. I had been that title at that table for nine years. Meeting stories. People under me. That was my whole supper. I got short. Not loud. Short. Snapping at my youngest about homework. Sighing. One night my daughter, seventeen, put her fork down and said, "Are you mad at us?"

JONATHON: That's a terrible question for a girl to have to invent. What did you say, and did you tell your wife that night or did you wait again?

WES: I said no, which was true. I wasn't mad at them. I had lost my name and I was taking it out on the people who never called me by it. They called me Dad. That name was fine. I just did not count it. I told my wife that night in the kitchen. She said, "Three weeks? You've been like this for three weeks over a title?" I said yes. She said, "Honey, I didn't even know what your title was." She knew where I worked. She knew what I did all day. She never knew the words on the door. Nine years. The most important thing about me, to me, and the woman I married could not have told you what it said.

JONATHON: That's a mercy that feels like a bruise. You got reorganized into being a dad. A man from out of town with a slide did what a lot of sermons could not.

WES: Small stuff after. I quit telling meeting stories, mostly because I didn't have good ones. There was a hole, and the kids filled it. Student teaching. A video game that lasted a year. Last month my daughter, twenty-five now, said, "That was the year you started asking us stuff." I didn't know she had noticed. I didn't know it had a year.

JONATHON: She dated it. "The year you started asking us stuff." That's a name she gave you.

[BOARD: A title is a thin name to bring home.]

WES: Sunday a new man at church asked what I do. I told him the family first. The job second. It felt clumsy in my mouth, like speaking a language I learned late. I want clumsy. Clumsy is how a name changes. If I had said the title first I would have been the 2019 man again, just in a nicer shirt.

## SEGMENT 3 · The crew

WES: Middle shape. Run the room. You have a man for this.

JONATHON: Our contractor. Sixty. Twenty guys. You want him on a job site. Everybody knows the next move, nobody stands around, every decision is fast and his. For thirty years he ran his house the same way. Vacation, car, church, school. Fast, and his. He thought that was leading. About ten years ago he is laying out a roof in the kitchen and she sets down a dish towel and says, "I'm not on your crew." He said it was a two-by-four. She had been on his crew twenty years without being asked if she wanted the job. He sat in the truck an hour. Did not start it. Came back in and asked what kind of roof she wanted. She said, "Metal. I've wanted metal for six years." He had been about to put shingles on it again. They have a metal roof now. He says it's the best roof he has ever put on, and he didn't pick it.

WES: Then he wanted to teach. After twenty years of being the boss, he wanted a room that could talk back.

JONATHON: He wants to sell in five years and teach welding. I asked why. He said, "I've been the boss my whole life. I'd like to work with somebody who's allowed to talk back." Yeah, and— that sentence has been living in my phone. I run rooms. I run sales meetings. I run the Bluebonnets. I can feel myself deciding the vacation, the church night, the tone of the kitchen, and calling it care. Dani isn't on my crew. If I forget that I will get a dish towel someday, and I will deserve it.

WES: Run the room looks like leadership until the people in the room go quiet. Quiet isn't peace. We have already had that fight. Quiet is a crew that stopped talking because the boss already decided.

JONATHON: I asked Dani this week if I run the room. She said, "With your mom, sometimes. With me, you try and I stop you." That's a mixed report card and I will take it. I texted my sister too, because she knew the nine-year-old version of me. She has not answered yet. I will tell you Friday. I also texted my nephew, who is in the parent group chat because he is Ruby's cousin. He wrote, "you sound like me at parties." Twenty-two years old and he already knows the room. I told him to pick one person this week and ask a question he does not already have the answer to. He wrote, "ok. roommate." That's a start.

## SEGMENT 4 · The Word

WES: We're about halfway, and I want the Bible open, because Proverbs already named the way that looks right. Proverbs fourteen, verse twelve.

JONATHON: I know this one and I don't like it, because it does not let me keep a shape that's working at the office.

WES: "There is a way that appears to be right, but in the end it leads to death." Skip the house appears to be right. The kids are fed. The practice was excellent. The parents are happy. Then a four-year-old asks why the voice only lives where there are bleachers. Run the room appears to be right. Decisions get made. Nobody stands around. Then a wife says she's not on the crew. Wear the job appears to be right. The bills are paid. Then a seventeen-year-old asks if you're mad at them.

[BOARD: A useful way can still empty a living room.]

JONATHON: Hold on. If I put that verse on a board next to my Saturday, a man whose job actually feeds his kids is going to think we just called work death. I need the line. Work isn't the death. Making work the name is the death. Coaching isn't the death. Saving the voice for the bleachers is the death.

WES: That's the line. Work is a gift. A title can be a tool. Coaching twelve kids is a good thing. The death is when the way that appears right steals the house, or steals her voice, or steals your real name. Proverbs isn't anti-job. Proverbs is anti-fooling yourself.

JONATHON: Here's my problem with naming shapes. I will hear three and I will pick the one that makes me look interesting. I will say I skip the house because it sounds like a confession, and I won't ask Dani which one she actually lives with. The interesting shape is a new plaque.

WES: Then don't pick. Ask. That's the whole challenge. One person who lives with you, or one brother if you live alone. "Which of these do you see?" Then write down the answer without editing it. If you edit it you're running the room again.

[BOARD: Proverbs 27:23 (NIV) · "Be sure you know the condition of your flocks, give careful attention to your herds."]

## SEGMENT 5 · Pushback

WES: Keep pushing. I know you're not done.

JONATHON: What about the man whose house is fine and whose job is on fire? If I sit here talking about leftover energy, a guy working nights to keep the lights on is going to think we just shamed him for being tired. I need paint, not pencil.

WES: Draw it. Tired is real. Two jobs are real. A night shift is real. Skip the house isn't the same as being tired. Skip the house is having a voice left for the bleachers and none for the living room. If you have nothing left, that's a different conversation about load, and we have already had some of that. If you have a coach voice in you and you spend it everywhere except home, that's this Tuesday.

JONATHON: Second. Run the room can look like the only adult in a house that needs one. Somebody has to decide the pediatrician and the leak. If I wait for consensus I will still have a leak.

WES: Then decide the leak and ask about the roof. Fast on pipes. Slow on people. The contractor still ran a crew. He stopped running his wife. Those aren't the same skill. If you can't tell them apart, ask her. She can tell them apart.

JONATHON: Third. I still think run the room is worse. You think skip the house is worse. We've got two chairs and we haven't sat in the same one.

WES: I will keep skip the house, because I've watched a man be excellent in a conference room and a ghost at six-thirty. You can keep run the room. Both kill something. Pick the one that's yours this week, not the one you enjoy judging. Sunday in the car my wife said, "Three kids and your mother." I had told the new man at church my family first and I had left her out. She was medium mad. She was right. I can name the kids and Mom and still skip the woman in the passenger seat. That's how a shape hides inside a better sentence.

JONATHON: That's a good catch and I hate it for you in a brother way. I left Dani out of a parent-text I sent to the group chat this week. I wrote like I was the whole house. She forwarded it back to me with one word. "We." I deserved that. The nephew wrote in the same thread, "lol. you sound like a coach." He wasn't wrong. He was also not in the room. He is twenty-two and he checks in by text, and I'm glad, because if he sat in the bleachers I would perform for him too.

WES: Text is enough. One man asking which shape. Not a crowd. A crowd is how the coach voice comes back.

## SEGMENT 6 · This week's challenge

WES: Last few minutes. Ask one person which shape they see. Skip the house. Run the room. Wear the job as your name. Write the answer down. Don't argue in the first ten minutes. Then do one contrary act this week. If you skip the house, give the living room the voice you save for the bleachers. Twenty minutes. Full voice. If you run the room, ask a question you don't already have the answer to, and let the answer stand. If you wear the job, tell one person who you are without the title, and then ask them a question about their Tuesday.

JONATHON: I'm asking Dani again, cleaner. Not "do I run the room sometimes." I'm asking, "Which one do you live with." Then I'm doing catch with Theo before I sit down. Not after. Before. If I do it after I will negotiate myself back onto the couch. My nephew is asking his roommate a question he does not know the answer to. I told him to text me what he heard, not what he meant to hear.

WES: I'm asking my wife the same clean question, and I already know she has a vote. I'm also asking my oldest by text, because he saw the 2019 version and he is twenty-one now and he might remember a year I have polished. If he says the job, I won't explain the slide. I will write it down. Explanation is how I run the room with a man who isn't even here.

JONATHON: And if nobody will answer you, ask God and sit still for ten minutes. The first shape that makes your stomach tight is the assignment. Don't shop for a nicer one. A nicer one is how we stay in charge of the confession. Then tell one man. Thursday I will text you "Did you ask." I want a sentence.
