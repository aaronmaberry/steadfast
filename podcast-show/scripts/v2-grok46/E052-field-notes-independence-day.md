## SEGMENT 1 · The porch and the card

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on a prayer I almost made pretty. Alone. Out loud. Thanks: the chain. Person: the youngest, for October. Put down: the oldest saying you did not do one for me. I cannot fix that. You can. I heard myself ask for the day to go good, and I asked again, because good is a mush word and I was rushing the end. I still cannot pray out loud with my wife. That crack is still a crack. I am not closing it for a holiday.

JONATHON: Wednesday after the dishes you read her the four lines. She said say it slower. You rush the end. You said that is better. She read the card twice to herself and went to bed. The end is what it is for. You practice that at the barn. I needed to hear a woman edit a blessing. That is a kind of freedom. Not being the only adult in the sentence.

WES: I will practice at the barn. I rush purpose. I can describe a boy all day. I get cheap when I say what he is for. Cheap is a yoke. I pick it up because it feels humble. It is just fear in a soft shirt.

JONATHON: Sunday on the New Braunfels back porch I asked Dani's dad to be one of the men at Theo's morning. His coffee. I poured. He said I will be eighty-two. I said yes, sir. He said well, put it on my calendar too. He asked Dani where the calendar is. It is on his phone. She entered Theo's morning while he watched. The handshake at the car held about two seconds longer. I have a second name. The blank filled without a performance. I did not expect that.

[BOARD: A porch light can be enough.]


## SEGMENT 2 · A truck and a hundred days

WES: We're about halfway. You prayed in a truck. I want that next to my porch so nobody thinks this only works with a view.

JONATHON: Wednesday. Driveway. Three false starts. Dani knocked, thinking it was a work call. I said I am praying. She said oh. I prayed for the man with the screws. I still do not know his name. I will ask in July. The nephew is past a hundred days. I will send him this one. He will text back k. That is allowed. I will not need a paragraph to count it.

WES: My Fourth is fireworks in town Sunday night, a lawn chair, my wife. In my family the Fourth was the creek. I will not make the town fireworks pretend to be the creek. Both can be good. Only one of them taught me a hill. I can sit in a chair and still remember water.

JONATHON: I keep seeing Ruby's card for my father, half done on the counter. She asks every few days. I say soon. Soon is me not deciding. I am scared of what he does not say back to a seven-year-old. That is not freedom. That is a yoke I picked back up after two words on Father's Day. I need to either help her finish it or tell her the truth about why we wait. Soon is a lie that sounds gentle.

[BOARD: Put it on his phone too.]


## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: Galatians five, verse one. A holiday week is a good week to read it without a flag on it.

JONATHON: "It is for freedom that Christ has set us free. Stand firm, then, and do not let yourselves be burdened again by a yoke of slavery."

WES: Freedom is not a day off. Freedom is not fireworks. Freedom is not a boy at nine being told he is the man of the house. Freedom is standing firm and not picking up the old yoke because a holiday made you lonely. I pick up the yoke when I rush the end of a sentence. I pick it up when I try to fix a wound with a matching ceremony.

JONATHON: I pick it up when I say soon to a girl with a marker. I pick it up when I want a work-call face for a prayer. I want the verse in a driveway. Stand firm then. Then is after you have been set free. I have been set free and I still reach for the booth and the job and the photo I was asked not to take.

[BOARD: Joshua 4:6-7 (NIV) · "In the future, when your children ask you, ‘What do these stones mean?’"]


## SEGMENT 4 · The porch prayer

WES: Last few minutes. Tool is the porch prayer. Alone if you must. Out loud. Thank God for one concrete thing. Name one person. Put one thing down you cannot fix. If a neighbor light comes on, finish anyway. If you cannot pray with your wife yet, do not lie about that. Tell the truth and go to the porch. The crack can stay a crack while you still open your mouth.

JONATHON: What stuck. Bud is mostly gone. Theo still catches it in the car. The first text got sent. What is not stuck is the card on the counter. I will not call that a season review win. I will call it the yoke I can see.

WES: What stuck. Proud, said to two sons. Staying out of my daughter's marriage enough to let a couch lose its feet. What is not stuck is starting sentences to the youngest without his name. Did you move the truck. I still do that. The name is the freedom. The missing name is the old yoke, small and ordinary.

JONATHON: If freedom means anything this week, I will either finish the card with her or tell her why we wait, in words a seven-year-old can stand. I will not say soon again. Soon is how I stay enslaved to a maybe.

WES: Look around your house this week. Sit on the porch. Say the three parts. Do not post it. Do not wear a flag as a personality. A chair and a sentence are enough.

JONATHON: I will pour coffee again if I have to. I already got a name. I do not need to turn eighty-two into a speech. I need to stand firm when I want to pick up soon.

[BOARD: The floor teaches first.]

WES: I asked for the day to go good and heard how cheap it sounded. I asked again. Asking again is allowed. Asking again is not the same as decorating. I want the second ask to be slower. That is the whole practice.

WES: I keep wanting this to resolve in one conversation, and it will not. The assignment is the next honest sentence, said on time, without a speech attached. I have left too many good talks on a table because I wanted a better closing line than the one I had.

WES: I want this back in your house, not in ours. Pick the one door that is yours. Not your brother's. Not a man on a screen. Yours. Put a day on it before supper, even if the day is tomorrow and the sentence is clumsy.

JONATHON: Mine is already on a calendar. That is the only reason I trust it. A mood is not a plan, and I have used moods for years to sound wise while I stayed still. I do not want to sound wise. I want to be on time.

WES: If you fail the first try, the second try still counts. I have to say that every time or I will use the miss as a way out and then tell a story about the miss like the story was the obedience.

JONATHON: I used the first miss as a way out for most of my twenties. I am trying not to do that at thirty-seven. It is harder than any capacitor, and it is also more ordinary than I want to admit at this table.

## SEGMENT · Sitting with it

WES: I have watched men collect stories like this and never change a Tuesday. Do not be that man this week. Change one Tuesday. Then tell one person you did, because a secret obedience turns back into a mood by Thursday.

WES: I want the listener who thinks this does not apply because his house is quieter. Quiet is not settled. You already know that if you have sat through a polite holiday and called it peace.

JONATHON: My house can be loud and still be a cease-fire. Volume is not the test. The test is whether the true thing got said to the person who had to hear it, not to a mug at this table.

WES: I will check this on Friday, which is the only reason I trust myself. A public calendar is a kind of mercy for a man who likes his recliner and likes to call the recliner wisdom.

JONATHON: I will hold you to it, and I want somebody holding me. That is why I say the time out loud even when it makes me look late. Looking late is better than looking peaceful and being dug in.

WES: A steadfast man does the small thing while it is still small. That is the whole show, said again, because we forget it by Wednesday and start waiting to feel ready, and the ready never comes first.

JONATHON: I forget it by Tuesday afternoon. Ruby does not. She has a better memory for what I said I would do than any review I have ever run, and she does not care that I had a good reason.

WES: If this feels like too many stories, pick one. One house. One name. One time. That is enough weight for seven days, and it is more than most of us will actually carry if we get proud of the list.

JONATHON: I will pick one. If I pick three I will do none, and I already know that about myself from sales and from home. One is how a man like me gets free of his own notes app.

WES: I have a habit of saving the true sentence for a better room. There is no better room. The kitchen is the room. The truck is the room. The porch after the dishes is the room. Say it there.

JONATHON: I have rehearsed in better rooms my whole adult life. The rehearsal is how I stay safe. Dani can hear the difference between a rehearsal and a sentence. She stops me when I start handing people a door before they are in the room.

WES: Some of you will try to make this spiritual so you do not have to be specific. Prayer is good. A day and a name is still required. Do not baptize delay and call it discernment.

JONATHON: I have baptized delay. I have called it being careful. Careful is sometimes fear with a pressed shirt. I am trying to tell those two apart before I waste another month.
