## SEGMENT 1 · A sticky note

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the fridge, because a child beat us to honesty. You said your sentence at breakfast. She wrote hers.

JONATHON: I said make sure everybody is okay, starting at home. She asked if that counts the dog. I said we do not have a dog. She said yet. Then she wrote, be nice to Theo most of the time. Because I want it to be true. It is on the fridge. I have a heading that sounds like a man. She has a heading that sounds like a house. I know which one I trust.

WES: I said mine to my wife on the porch Tuesday night. Love God out loud, keep the people He gave me, and finish. I said the out loud part. She said yes. Then okay. That is all. I wanted a longer review. She gave me okay. Okay is how my people bless a thing they intend to remember.

JONATHON: I steered by the Monday leaderboard. I checked Austin's number before my own. You steered by whoever called last. Those are headings we never wrote and obeyed anyway. Written is better. A child can read written. A child cannot read whoever called last.

[BOARD: The floor teaches first.]


## SEGMENT 2 · A sister and a badge

WES: We're about halfway. My wife told me something that is hers, with permission. Her sister has called her every Sunday at four since Fredericksburg, after twenty-five years of holidays and birthdays. She said she has been the one who calls her whole life. And somebody is calling me. She said I can put that on the show. That one is mine. So I am putting it on. A heading can be a phone that finally rings the other way.

JONATHON: The twenty-four-year-old at car-care, no car, overnights at a warehouse, early bus home, sleeps till two. Raised by his grandmother, now in the nursing home by the hospital. He visits Tuesdays and Thursdays. He wrote live with purpose, love hard, leave a legacy on the back of his work badge in Sharpie, then crossed it out. He said he cannot do the harmony stuff at church. He just sings the melody loud. His grandma says that is the hard part.

WES: I notice Merle needs a lead. I am not connecting them. Merle has a phone. It is not my business to make a young man's badge into a quartet. Some of you will want me to fix that. I will not. A heading you force on another man is a slogan.

JONATHON: My page has three lines. I am home for bath three nights a week. I call my mom and ask about her first. I do not plan Dani's Tuesday nights. She said she is going to hold me to number three. I want that more than I want a pretty badge. Pretty is how I stay general. Three lines is how I get caught.

[BOARD: Somebody is calling her now.]


## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: Habakkuk two, verse two. A prophet told to write it plain enough that a runner can take it.

JONATHON: "Write down the revelation and make it plain on tablets so that a herald may run with it."

WES: Plain. Not clever. Not a brand. A herald has to run with it. If your wife cannot repeat your heading, it is not plain. If a seven-year-old can write a better one, listen to her and then write yours smaller.

JONATHON: Most of the time is plain. Starting at home is plain. Out loud is plain and expensive. I do not want a tablet that would impress a VP. I want one a child can read on the way to cereal.

[BOARD: Proverbs 29:18 (NIV) · "Where there is no revelation, people cast off restraint; but blessed is the one who heeds wisdom’s instruction."]


## SEGMENT 4 · The one-page house plan

WES: Last few minutes. Tool is a one-page house plan. Three lines. What you will do at home. Who you will keep. What you will refuse to manage that is not yours. Say it on a porch. Do not laminate it unless your wife already owns a laminator and you are making a joke.

JONATHON: What stuck this season. Austin, four Thursdays of did you eat. K, K, K, then yes. tacos. That is a heading I did not write that still happened because I kept a Thursday. What is not stuck is a second question to Dani's dad. I am scared the second answer will not be as good. That is a bad reason and it is mine.

WES: What stuck for me is two calls with my little brother since Deputy, plus Abilene, plus a Friday-noon call about a cold. What is not stuck is the barn. Two Saturdays, one missed, and four sentences if you count the phone. Hand me that. Same time next week. Cows will be here. He told me that too. Four sentences is a start. It is not a heading yet. The card is the heading. The barn is how I heed it.

JONATHON: If you cannot write three lines, steal the shape, not the words. Home. A person. A refuse. Then tell one person in the house. If you only tell a table of men, you have a slogan again.

WES: Look around your house this week. The fridge is allowed to hold the plan. The porch is allowed to hear it. The week will try to pull your eyes back to the row. Look at the tree anyway.

JONATHON: I will not plan Tuesday nights. I will do dishes. I will call my mom and ask about her first. If I come back with a story about a badge I improved for a young man, stop me. That is me wandering with a toolbox again.

[BOARD: Plain enough to run.]

WES: I wanted my wife to say more than okay. She said okay and went back inside. I stood on the porch like a man who had given a speech. I had given a sentence. A sentence is enough. I am trying to believe that.

WES: I keep wanting this to resolve in one conversation, and it will not. The assignment is the next honest sentence, said on time, without a speech attached. I have left too many good talks on a table because I wanted a better closing line than the one I had.

WES: I want this back in your house, not in ours. Pick the one door that is yours. Not your brother's. Not a man on a screen. Yours. Put a day on it before supper, even if the day is tomorrow and the sentence is clumsy.

JONATHON: Mine is already on a calendar. That is the only reason I trust it. A mood is not a plan, and I have used moods for years to sound wise while I stayed still. I do not want to sound wise. I want to be on time.

## SEGMENT · Sitting with it

WES: If you fail the first try, the second try still counts. I have to say that every time or I will use the miss as a way out and then tell a story about the miss like the story was the obedience.

JONATHON: I used the first miss as a way out for most of my twenties. I am trying not to do that at thirty-seven. It is harder than any capacitor, and it is also more ordinary than I want to admit at this table.

WES: I have watched men collect stories like this and never change a Tuesday. Do not be that man this week. Change one Tuesday. Then tell one person you did, because a secret obedience turns back into a mood by Thursday.

JONATHON: I am the man who collects stories. I have a notes app full of them. The only ones that count are the ones that got a day and a time and a person who knows I said I would do it.

WES: I want the listener who thinks this does not apply because his house is quieter. Quiet is not settled. You already know that if you have sat through a polite holiday and called it peace.

JONATHON: My house can be loud and still be a cease-fire. Volume is not the test. The test is whether the true thing got said to the person who had to hear it, not to a mug at this table.

WES: I will check this on Friday, which is the only reason I trust myself. A public calendar is a kind of mercy for a man who likes his recliner and likes to call the recliner wisdom.

JONATHON: I will hold you to it, and I want somebody holding me. That is why I say the time out loud even when it makes me look late. Looking late is better than looking peaceful and being dug in.

WES: A steadfast man does the small thing while it is still small. That is the whole show, said again, because we forget it by Wednesday and start waiting to feel ready, and the ready never comes first.

JONATHON: I forget it by Tuesday afternoon. Ruby does not. She has a better memory for what I said I would do than any review I have ever run, and she does not care that I had a good reason.

WES: If this feels like too many stories, pick one. One house. One name. One time. That is enough weight for seven days, and it is more than most of us will actually carry if we get proud of the list.

WES: I have a habit of saving the true sentence for a better room. There is no better room. The kitchen is the room. The truck is the room. The porch after the dishes is the room. Say it there.

JONATHON: I have rehearsed in better rooms my whole adult life. The rehearsal is how I stay safe. Dani can hear the difference between a rehearsal and a sentence. She stops me when I start handing people a door before they are in the room.

WES: Some of you will try to make this spiritual so you do not have to be specific. Prayer is good. A day and a name is still required. Do not baptize delay and call it discernment.

JONATHON: I have baptized delay. I have called it being careful. Careful is sometimes fear with a pressed shirt. I am trying to tell those two apart before I waste another month.

WES: The man who says he will start after the busy season is lying to himself the same way I did in my forties. There is always another season. Do the small thing inside the busy, or you will never do it.

JONATHON: HVAC sales does not hand you a quiet month. If I wait for one I will still be waiting when Theo has a license. I have to put the true thing on a Tuesday that already has a meeting.
