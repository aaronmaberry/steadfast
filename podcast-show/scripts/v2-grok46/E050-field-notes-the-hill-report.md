## SEGMENT 2 · The chain and the games

WES: We're about halfway. Tuesday the youngest said the chain went dull at the end. Uncle said it's dull. Wednesday night the saw was in the shop vise, his phone on a coffee can, a man in Oregon, watched twice, every tooth filed. I stood in the door about a minute. I said night. He said night. That's his now. I didn't take the file. I wanted to. My hands know that file. His hands needed it more.

JONATHON: After my dad left I played a baseball video game at a friend's house three or four hours a day for about two years. It was the one place where if I worked at something, it got better. The game taught me how to practice. I just didn't have anything real to practice on. I'm not romantic about it. I'm telling you why a screen can be a hill when a house isn't.

WES: A man at the table, forty-one, son fourteen, uses timers, a cord, a router password. That's a fence. It's not a hill. A fence can help. A hill is a Saturday that gets better when you work at it. I don't want us to confuse a password with a creek.

JONATHON: Your oldest gamed all through high school. Friday nights. A headset. You were out of the fog by then. You said turn that off and never sat down. You're going to ask him Sunday what those Friday nights were. I want you to ask before you judge my closet. I still have a closet full of that hunger. I don't want you to unplug a life you have never sat beside.

## SEGMENT · Sitting with it

WES: I've watched men collect hill reports like this and never date a Saturday. Don't be that man this week. Date the project. Stay on the floor. Then tell one person you did, because a secret Saturday turns back into a mood by Thursday.

JONATHON: I'm the man who collects stories. I've got a notes app and a six-minute cry I timed. The only ones that count are the ones that got a coffee can and a no at the Velcro.

WES: I want the listener who thinks this doesn't apply because his house is quieter. Quiet isn't settled. You already know that if you've sat through a polite holiday and called it peace. A dull chain is still a report.

JONATHON: My house can be loud and still be a cease-fire. Volume isn't the test. The test is whether I stay after minute six, not whether I can tell you about a headset.

WES: I'll check the jamb on Friday, which is the only reason I trust myself. The back door is still unfinished. A public calendar is a kind of mercy for a man who likes his recliner and likes to call the recliner wisdom.

JONATHON: I'll hold you to the plane, and I want somebody holding me to Saturday. That's why I say the coffee can out loud even when it makes me look late. Looking late is better than looking holy and unplugging a life I never sat beside.
