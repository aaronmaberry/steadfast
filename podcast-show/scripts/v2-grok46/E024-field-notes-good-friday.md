## SEGMENT 2 · From the table

WES: We're about halfway, and I want the question. What do I do with Good Friday as a dad?

JONATHON: I wanted a craft. Ruby wanted a part. Theo wanted the floor. The craft would have been a hide. The part was better. Read the thing. Let a seven-year-old be the crowd. Answer the question she actually asks. Don't turn it into a lecture about substitutionary anything. She asked why she had to be mean. I told her we all are, a little, and he came anyway. That's enough for a Friday supper. The rest can wait until she's older. The reading can't wait.

WES: If you have no kids, read it anyway. Out loud. In a truck if you have to. Noon to three, something off. A radio. A phone. A habit. You don't need children to keep a three-hour quiet. You need a clock and a willingness to be unproductive in a world that won't bless that. My dad never explained it. I'm explaining it once, and then I'm doing it. The doing is the better explanation.

JONATHON: And if your kids won't sit, let one sit under the table holding an ankle. That counted. Theo heard more than I think. He asked later if Jesus was sad. I said yes. He said, "Okay. I can be the shark another night." That's theology I didn't plan. I wanted a tidy supper and I got an ankle and a misspelled tab and a question I could not outsource. I'm glad. Tidy would have been a hide. I've hidden in tidy most of my life. Sales likes tidy. Good Friday does not.

WES: I hid in tidy for years. A clean truck. A finished report. A radio that stayed on because silence felt like a hospital. Today I will sit in three hours of it and I won't call it productivity. If I get restless I will remember a man who never explained himself and still taught me more with a knob than I've taught with a paragraph. I want to be that kind of quiet for my kids once, even late. Late is still allowed. We keep saying that because we need it.
