## SEGMENT 1 · Check the box

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the radio, the supper, and the four o'clock I did not miss. My oldest called Wednesday night. "Did Grandpa really turn off the radio on Good Friday? I remember it from the truck." He is doing it on the job site this year, noon to three, no music. He asked his foreman. The foreman said, "Fine, but you're telling the crew." I am doing it too. Radio and phone off, noon to three. I stopped in the fog years. I am done stopping. I do not need a speech about why. My dad never gave one. The quiet was the sentence.

JONATHON: We tried John eighteen and nineteen at supper Wednesday. Ruby made tabs. She wrote NARATOR with one R and she would not let me fix it. She read the crowd's lines. Theo lasted about four minutes, then sat under the table holding my ankle. After the crowd's line she stopped and asked, "Why do I have to be the mean people?" I answered her myself this time. I did not hand it to Dani. I said, "Because all of us are, a little. That's why he did it. He knew, and he came anyway." Ruby said, "Even Mommy?" Dani said, "Especially Mommy." We are reading it again tonight, all the way through. I want that on the record so the first four minutes are not the whole story.

WES: That's the walk. You took the car question in a smaller form. You answered. Good.

JONATHON: Thursday the twin's dad was not early. He was ten minutes late. He walked straight to his daughter and played catch the whole practice. He did not look at the other parents. I still went to her once when she missed a throw. He was already there. That's a good late. I will take a good late over a parking-lot promise that dies at supper.

WES: Wednesday at 4:10 my daughter called during a meeting. I stepped out and I picked up. She wanted Grandma's roll recipe for Easter. Homemade. I still have not told her about the five hours in 2018. I told her I would find the card. I found the card. I sent a picture. She said thanks, Dad, and she did not ask why I sounded like I had run. I had run. I ran to a phone. That's the four o'clock I could reach this week. I will take it.

JONATHON: Screen numbers, week two. You were two hours fifty-eight. I was four thirty-one. Both down. I reached for the nightstand twice, not four. I sent the number Thursday. You sent yours first. I hated that you sent first and I also needed it.

WES: What stuck. You, for me: you ask now. In January you told. Me, for you: you stopped landing every story. What did not stick: my wife and I still do not pray out loud together. Still no. Put it on the list. Your coach voice still goes back in the drawer on Mondays. And the hidden thing I will not name. I said I would tell her probably by Easter. I have not. ## SEGMENT 2 · From the table

WES: We're about halfway, and I want the question. What do I do with Good Friday as a dad?

JONATHON: I wanted a craft. Ruby wanted a part. Theo wanted the floor. The craft would have been a hide. The part was better. Read the thing. Let a seven-year-old be the crowd. Answer the question she actually asks. Do not turn it into a lecture about substitutionary anything. She asked why she had to be mean. I told her we all are, a little, and he came anyway. That's enough for a Friday supper. The rest can wait until she is older. The reading cannot wait.

WES: If you have no kids, read it anyway. Out loud. In a truck if you have to. Noon to three, something off. A radio. A phone. A habit. You do not need children to keep a three-hour quiet. You need a clock and a willingness to be unproductive in a world that will not bless that. My dad never explained it. I am explaining it once, and then I am doing it. The doing is the better explanation.

JONATHON: And if your kids will not sit, let one sit under the table holding an ankle. That counted. Theo heard more than I think. He asked later if Jesus was sad. I said yes. He said, "Okay. I can be the shark another night." That's theology I did not plan. I wanted a tidy supper and I got an ankle and a misspelled tab and a question I could not outsource. I am glad. Tidy would have been a hide. I have hidden in tidy most of my life. Sales likes tidy. Good Friday does not.

WES: I hid in tidy for years. A clean truck. A finished report. A radio that stayed on because silence felt like a hospital. Today I will sit in three hours of it and I will not call it productivity. If I get restless I will remember a man who never explained himself and still taught me more with a knob than I have taught with a paragraph. I want to be that kind of quiet for my kids once, even late. Late is still allowed. We keep saying that because we need it.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: We've got a little under eight minutes, and I want one verse we can sit under. Isaiah fifty-three, verse five.

JONATHON: I need the wounds, not a mood. A mood will not get me through Sunday night.

WES: "But he was pierced for our transgressions, he was crushed for our iniquities; the punishment that brought us peace was on him, and by his wounds we are healed." That's why a girl can be the crowd and still be loved. That's why a man in the trees is not the last word. He took the mean parts. He did not wait for us to become the kind of people who would not have shouted.

[BOARD: Romans 5:8 (NIV) · "But God demonstrates his own love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us."]

JONATHON: Still sinners. Still in the trees. Still handing a question to Mom. Still missing four o'clock. He came anyway. I do not want to use that as a blanket for another delay. I want it as a reason to walk. I told Dani that last night and she said, "Then walk Sunday and walk tonight. Both." She is right. Tonight is the reading. Sunday is the phone. If I mix them I will do neither well. I have mixed holy things before so I could feel busy and stay still.

WES: Use it as a reason to walk. Then turn something off from noon to three. Then read the thing tonight if you have not. Then tell one person what you turned off. I will tell my wife at lunch what I turned off, which is the same phone I used to hide a five-hour gap. The irony is not lost on me. Irony is not repentance. Turning it off is closer. If I pick it up at 12:10 to see if the world ended, I will say so here. I would rather look small than look holy.

## SEGMENT 4 · Read it at supper

WES: Last few minutes on the tool. Read the Passion at dinner. John eighteen and nineteen, or Luke twenty-three. You do not have to finish in one sitting. You do have to start. Assign a crowd if you have a child who will take it. Let a little one sit under the table. Phones in the basket. If you eat alone, read it out loud anyway. Then sit quiet. Then do the noon-to-three if you can. If you work a site, tell the crew. My son is telling his. That is a walk.

JONATHON: I will not decorate the tabs. One R is fine. I will not correct the theology of a seven-year-old in the middle of a line. I will answer the question she asks. I will not decide the friend request at the table. That's Sunday. Tonight is the reading. Mixing them is how I hide in a holy week.

WES: If you still have a hidden thing you promised to tell, do not tell this table first. Tell her. I am saying that at myself. I have not done it. I will not use Good Friday as a costume for a man who is still hiding a small true thing. The small true thing is still the test. I almost dressed this Friday up as a man who has arrived because he remembered his father's knob. Remembering a knob is not the same as telling a wife the thing I said I would tell. I can do the noon quiet and still be a coward at six. I want both walks. I have only scheduled one. That's honest, and it is not enough, and I am leaving it ugly on purpose so I cannot collect.

JONATHON: My nephew texted, "good friday. do i turn something off." I wrote, "yes. pick one thing. three hours if you can. tell the roommate." He wrote, "phone. drawer. roommate knows." That's a twenty-two-year-old walking. I can walk too. I told him I was reading with my kids and I was still scared of Sunday. He wrote, "then do tonight first." He is not wrong. Tonight first. Sunday after. I keep wanting Sunday to excuse tonight. It will not. A lamp on a porch does not read John for you. I have to open my mouth at a table and let a girl be the crowd. That's the walk I can take before the sun goes down.
