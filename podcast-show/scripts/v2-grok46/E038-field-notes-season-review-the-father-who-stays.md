## SEGMENT 1 · Shane Texas

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the name, because you actually asked. Tell it from the embarrassing sentence, not from the ending.

JONATHON: I said I've got to tell you something embarrassing. He said, you don't know my name. He had known since March. Everybody calls me Harper's dad. I figured you were one of those. I asked what he does when he's not out there. He said he's building the Texas. A three-foot model of the battleship in his garage. Nine years of nights. His grandfather served on it. His wife says it's the other woman.

WES: Forty photos of the deck guns, I assume, because a man does not spend nine years and then give you a summary. Ruby asked if she can see it. He said it's never a bother. That's a door you didn't have on Thursday morning.

JONATHON: In my phone it now says Shane Texas. If I know his name, he's a person. I've kept a lot of men as somebody's dad so I would never have to find out. My sentence went from Wes, because it's Tuesday, to Shane, maybe. That's a small sentence. It's the first one that's not you.

WES: Good. Don't turn it into a testimony yet. A garage visit is coming. Let the model be a model. You asked. He answered. That's the whole check I wanted.

## SEGMENT 2 · Abilene and a text

WES: We're about halfway. I called my little brother. I asked a real question. He said, is this the show. Then okay. My knees. Then, my father-in-law's dying. They told us about a month. He has driven to Abilene alone every weekend since March, and nobody in the family knew. I asked which weekend I can drive up. He said I don't have to do that. I said I know. He said the twenty-ninth.

JONATHON: You're going. That's a third call that's not a birthday and not Christmas. I keep thinking about how many dog videos he sent while he was carrying that. You thought the videos were the whole man. They were the lid.

WES: They were the lid. I'm going Saturday the twenty-ninth. I don't have a speech. I've got a truck and a day. My older brother still talks to Dad in a barn. My little brother has been sitting in a hospice family room by himself. I got the hospital word. He got the miles. I think he was the one Dad knew, and I'm late to knowing him.

JONATHON: The nephew texted at six-fifteen in the morning. He said his dad has not been around much, which is his to tell. I said your dad isn't the only man who gets to teach you things. You can learn on purpose. He wrote, ok. thats actually good. dont make it weird. day fifty nine btw. I answered ok in five minutes. I didn't add a paragraph.

WES: Good. Five minutes is better than fifteen of wisdom. A father who stays mostly looks like nothing. A text that says ok isn't nothing. It's a boy who is still counting days and still willing to send them to you.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: Proverbs twenty, verse six. A short question that will clean out a season if you let it.

JONATHON: "Many claim to have unfailing love, but a faithful person who can find?"

WES: A lot of men claim. A faithful person is rarer. Shane showed up on a field for months as a role. You found a person. My brother showed up in Abilene as a son-in-law and told nobody. That's the faithful person the proverb is hunting. Not the man with a speech about loyalty.

JONATHON: I'm still mad at my dad. I can make a call and stay mad. Those aren't opposites. Not yet is still not yet. I'm not turning this Friday into a reunion I haven't chosen. I can still write a line about a grandfather we named, and I can still learn to fish in June from a man who isn't my dad.

WES: My brother does not know he's teaching you yet. That's fine. June is coming. Today I want a stone, not a plan for a dock.

## SEGMENT 4 · The memorial stone

WES: Last few minutes. Tool is one written line. I took a creek rock from by the barn and put it on the shelf by the back door. Dad stayed. So did my brother. That's the whole thing. I can see it when I hang the keys. I still have to lift that door to hang them. The jamb is swollen. The plane is still on the workbench. The rock sits there watching me not do Saturday.

JONATHON: Mine is a card on Ruby's mirror. We learned his name. The grandfather's name stays off this table. The card is for her to see that we asked. I want her to grow up thinking names are normal, not a special project for men who finally got brave at thirty-seven.
WES: A card for men who finally got brave at thirty-seven. [beat] Yeah. The rock is doing its job. The door is still doing yours.
JONATHON: The rock is doing its job. The door is doing yours, and you keep walking past it. I'll ask about the jamb. You can ask if I put the card up or only talked about putting it up.

WES: What stuck for me is going inside so a boy can leave. What isn't stuck is praying out loud at the Thursday table. I still pass. I'm not pretending the rock fixed my mouth. The rock is about my dad and my brother. My mouth is still my work.

JONATHON: What stuck for me is four Sunday calls in a row. What isn't stuck is speed. Dani timed my answers to Mom's texts at supper. About forty seconds. That's better than a day, and it's still a man managing a feeling. Lamp shopping is Saturday. Mom's birthday is Tuesday. I can do a store without turning it into a speech.

WES: Look around your house this week. Write one line about a father who stayed, or about the hole if he didn't. Put it where you will see it. Don't post it. Don't explain it to a group. Let it sit by the keys or on a mirror and do its small job.

JONATHON: If you asked a name and got one, put it in the phone correctly. If you froze, ask anyway before Tuesday. I asked late and Shane already knew. Late still counts. Frozen is the only version that does not.

[BOARD: One line is a whole stone.]

WES: I keep wanting Abilene to be a scene. It will be a family room and a dog if they let him in and a ballgame with the sound off. That's enough. I don't need a line from my brother that makes this a story. I need to be in the chair.

WES: The listener who has no stone because his father left still gets a line. Write the true thing. Not a halo. Not a prosecution. One sentence you could read in five years and still stand by. That's a memorial too.

JONATHON: I almost wrote a prosecution. I wrote we learned his name instead. That's about a grandfather and a girl, not about the man in Louisiana. I can keep those on different cards. That's how I stay sane this week.

WES: I keep wanting the season to resolve on a creek rock, and it won't. The assignment is the next honest sentence, said on time, without a speech attached. I've left too many good talks on a table because I wanted a better closing line than the one I had.

JONATHON: Honestly, I will try to add the speech anyway. Somebody should stop me. If you're sitting in a driveway doing that too, write the three sentences down before you dial, then read only those three. The fourth sentence is where I get cute and ruin it.

## SEGMENT · Sitting with it

WES: I want this back in your house, not in ours. Pick the one door that's yours. Not your brother's. Not a man on a screen. Yours. Put a day on it before supper, even if the day is tomorrow and the sentence is clumsy.

JONATHON: Mine is already on a calendar. That's the only reason I trust it. A mood isn't a plan, and I've used moods for years to sound wise while I stayed still. I don't want to sound wise. I want to be on time.

WES: If you fail the first try, the second try still counts. I've got to say that every time or I will use the miss as a way out and then tell a story about the miss like the story was the obedience.

JONATHON: I used the first miss as a way out for most of my twenties. I'm trying not to do that at thirty-seven. It's harder than any capacitor, and it's also more ordinary than I want to admit at this table.

WES: I've watched men collect stories like this and never change a Tuesday. Don't be that man this week. Change one Tuesday. Then tell one person you did, because a secret obedience turns back into a mood by Thursday.

JONATHON: I'm the man who collects stories. I've got a notes app full of them. The only ones that count are the ones that got a day and a time and a person who knows I said I would do it.

WES: I want the listener who thinks this does not apply because his house is quieter. Quiet isn't settled. You already know that if you have sat through a polite holiday and called it peace.

JONATHON: My house can be loud and still be a cease-fire. Volume isn't the test. The test is whether the true thing got said to the person who had to hear it, not to a mug at this table.

WES: I will check this on Friday, which is the only reason I trust myself. A public calendar is a kind of mercy for a man who likes his recliner and likes to call the recliner wisdom.

JONATHON: I will hold you to it, and I want somebody holding me. That's why I say the time out loud even when it makes me look late. Looking late is better than looking peaceful and being dug in.

WES: A steadfast man does the small thing while it's still small. That's the whole show, said again, because we forget it by Wednesday and start waiting to feel ready, and the ready never comes first.

JONATHON: I forget it by Tuesday afternoon. Ruby does not. She has a better memory for what I said I would do than any review I've ever run, and she does not care that I had a good reason.
