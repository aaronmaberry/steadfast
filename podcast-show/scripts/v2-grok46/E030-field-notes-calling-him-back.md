## SEGMENT 1 · The truck

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the receipt. You first. Wednesday. Seven-thirty. Porch light on.

JONATHON: Nine minutes with my thumb on the contact. I almost turned the truck around and called it wisdom. Then I hit it. Third ring. "Who died?" I said nobody. He said okay. Then, "Well, hey, bud." Same script. I asked the question anyway. "Dad, what was your dad like?"

WES: And then there was a silence long enough that you thought he had hung up.

JONATHON: A long silence, then, "Why do you want to know that?" Then he told me his father's name. I wrote it on the back of a receipt and I'm keeping it off this table. Ruby will be the first person I say it to.
WES: Ruby will be the first person you say that name to. [beat] Yeah. Keep going. The grandfather worked the rigs, two weeks on.
JONATHON: The grandfather worked the rigs, two weeks on and two off. When he was home he fished, every day, on the lake before light and back after dark. He mostly didn't take my dad. "I don't remember him saying ten words to me that weren't about fish."

WES: So the rods in the truck weren't a hobby. They were the one thing his father gave him, and he never turned around and gave that to you.

JONATHON: He took those rods when he left. He never took me fishing. No apology. No mention of leaving from either side. Then he said he would let me go. I said, "Not yet." My voice cracked on "yet." He said okay, and he waited.
WES: [laughs] You said not yet. He said okay, and he waited. All right. Then you told him Ruby asked if he knows about her.
JONATHON: I told him Ruby asked if he knows about her. He said, "She's the one in the flowers." Then, "She looks like your mama." He asked what I told her. I said he lives in Louisiana. He said, "Well. That's true."

[BOARD: Do not take the first exit.]

WES: How long did he stay once you wouldn't let him off the hook?

JONATHON: Twelve minutes. Twice any call before. At the end he said, "Well. Call me sometime, bud." I said, "I just did." He laughed. A real laugh. "Ha. You did." I sat there mad, not sad. If he could do twelve minutes, he could have done twenty-eight years.
WES: Yeah, and—twelve minutes he could have done in twenty-eight years. I'm with you. Then you went inside. Dani had the lamp on.
JONATHON: Inside, Dani had the lamp on. I said he laughed. She said, "Good." At two in the morning I made the other call, the one to God, about the rods. Then I slept.

WES: You went first. You didn't get a speech back. You got twelve minutes and a laugh and a name on a receipt. That's not nothing, and it's not twenty-eight years either. Both of those can be true in the same driveway. I've wanted a father conversation to pay me back in one sitting. It never has. A first call is a door, not a refund.

## SEGMENT 2 · Proud without a reason

WES: My box was a lunch call to my oldest. Thursday. I said, "I'm proud of you." He was chewing. He said, "For what?" I said, "Not for anything. Just am." He said, "Okay. Thanks, Dad." Then fifteen minutes on the noise in his truck. Probably a wheel bearing. I don't know if it landed. I've decided that's okay.

JONATHON: You said the sentence without a trophy attached, and he went to a wheel bearing, which is how men in your family say they heard you. I'm going to need you to not chase a bigger reaction than okay.

WES: I wanted a bigger one. I didn't get it. I'm leaving it. The other thing in my house is my wife's sister turns fifty in May, and the two of them are going to Fredericksburg for three days. First time in twenty-five years. Nightly calls from the bathroom with the door shut, laughing. I asked what they talk about. She said, "Not you."

JONATHON: Her list is on the fridge next to the truck paper. A winery. A peach stand if they're early. A quilt shop she has wanted since 2003. You're in charge of the house for three days, and you're already treating it like a deployment.

WES: I'm treating it like a deployment. She laminated nothing yet, and I'm already nervous. I will run the house for three days and I will probably call her about a grocery list she already wrote. Look around your house this week. Somebody has been waiting on a sentence that's not about a report card. Say it clumsy. Then let them talk about a wheel bearing.

[BOARD: Say it without a trophy.]

JONATHON: I keep wanting my dad's twelve minutes to count as the whole debt. It does not. It counts as a start. I can be glad he laughed and still be mad about the rods. Friday is a receipt, not a eulogy.

WES: We're about halfway, and I want that tension left open. You don't have to tidy a father in one call. You have to stop being the only one who never dials.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: Colossians three, verse thirteen. Paul writing to a church that's learning how to live in the same room. One verse today.

JONATHON: "Bear with each other and forgive one another if any of you has a grievance against someone. Forgive as the Lord forgave you."

[BOARD: Luke 15:20 (NIV) · "So he got up and went to his father."]

WES: Notice the order. Bear with. Forgive. As the Lord forgave you. It does not say wait until the other man writes a speech. It does not say the grievance was small. It says the standard is how you have been forgiven. Paul is writing to people who still have to sit in the same room after the hurt. He does not give them a feeling to wait on. He gives them a move.

JONATHON: Here's my problem with that. My father isn't sorry. He didn't mention the lamp. He didn't mention the driveway. He told me about fish and a name. If I forgive him, does that mean I'm saying it was fine?

WES: No. Forgiveness isn't a press release. It's handing the debt to God so it stops running your kitchen. You can forgive a man and still tell the truth about the rods. You can forgive and still not hand him a key to your house. Bearing with isn't pretending. If you wait for sorry before you obey this verse, you may wait your whole life and call it discernment.

## SEGMENT 4 · From the table

WES: We've got about six minutes, and I want them on the question men asked. Do I've got to forgive a dad who isn't sorry?

JONATHON: That's the one I would have written in, because I'm living it. He laughed. He didn't repent. I don't want to be the guy who uses a Bible verse to rush myself past anger I haven't finished.

WES: You don't have to finish the anger this week. You do have to decide whether the anger gets to drive. If it drives, you will sit on the next call the way you sat on the last twenty-eight years.
JONATHON: You'll sit on the next call the way you sat on the last twenty-eight years. Hold on. That's the fork, and I know which way I'd pick.
WES: Forgiveness, in this verse, isn't a feeling you wait to arrive. It's a move you make because you have been forgiven. I've used unfinished anger as a reason to stay quiet and called it being careful. It was just me protecting me.

JONATHON: So the two-call rule isn't "call him until you feel warm." First call is to the man, if he's safe. Second call is to God, about the thing the first call didn't settle. I did the second one at two in the morning about the rods. I didn't get a feeling. I got sleep.

WES: That's a good receipt. The man whose dad will never say sorry still has a second call. Pray the name. Tell God the lamp. Then get up and be a father in your own house. Your kids aren't waiting for your dad to become a better man. They're watching what you do with the weight.

JONATHON: If your dad is gone, the first call is to somebody who knew him. An uncle. A sister. Then the second call is still to God. If he's dangerous, skip the first call. Do the second. Get a counselor in the room. That's still going first. It's just going first in the right direction.

WES: Married men, tell your wife what the call did to you before you decide what it meant. Jonathon told Dani he laughed, and she said good, and that was enough for the night. Don't turn the kitchen into a courtroom at ten o'clock.

JONATHON: Single men, text one man after you hang up. Not the speech. "I called him. It was twelve minutes. I am mad." Somebody should know you did it, or you will talk yourself out of the fact that you did.

## SEGMENT 5 · The two-call rule

WES: Last few minutes on the tool. Write it on a card if you have to. Two calls.

JONATHON: Call one, if he's safe: one question about his life before you. Stay past the first goodbye. Don't give the speech. Call two: talk to God about whatever the first call left on the floor. The rods. The silence. The laugh that made you mad.

[BOARD: Two calls. One man. Then God.]

WES: Put a day on both. Not "sometime." Wednesday at seven-thirty. Then before you sleep. If you already made the first one and skipped the second, make the second tonight. If you made neither, you still have a driveway. Write the two times on a card and put it on the dash. A card is harder to talk yourself out of than a mood.

JONATHON: I'm not sending him a paragraph. I'm not explaining the show to him. I'm going to say the name to Ruby this week, and I'm going to let that be the next honest thing. If you need a next step smaller than another phone call, tell one child one true sentence about your father.

[BOARD: A name is a start, not a finish.]

WES: Look at your week. If you sat in the truck and didn't hit the button, that's not the end of the assignment. The little thing is still the button. Second try counts here too. The contact is still named Dad. You can still put your thumb on it.
