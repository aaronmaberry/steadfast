WES: This is Walk Steadfast. I'm Wes Hart.

WES: Today, one question. What happens when you ration a son's name, or ration proud, until the only word left is a nickname that once belonged to somebody else? If you have a boy, or you were one, pull up a chair. Before we're done I will tell you what I said on the porch steps, and he will tell you how many times he said bud in a week.

## SEGMENT 1 · Steve and a knee

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on a fish and a farm road, because both houses did a small grown thing. The bluegill photo is on your fridge. Tell that first.

JONATHON: Beside Ruby's sticky note. Your brother released the fish. On the way home she named it Steve. It looked like a Steve. Bedtime prayers now include, and help Steve, right after evening, Chris. I did not laugh. I wanted to. I let a seven-year-old name a released fish and then ask God to help it. That is a better theology than most of my Monday calls.

WES: Saturday my youngest drove me to buy feed. A funeral procession on the farm road, about twenty cars. He pulled onto the gravel, cap off, held on his knee, till the last car passed. Then he asked for a Coke. He was six when my dad died. He got the knee from me. I did not know I did the knee. I said your granddad used to do that. He said, I know. You do it. I sat there with a Coke I had not earned and tried not to cry in a boy's truck.

JONATHON: He learned a liturgy you did not know you were teaching. That is how sons work. They copy the thing you never announced. They also copy the nickname you did not notice. I have been copying a word since I was nine and handing it to my kids like it was mine.

WES: I have rationed proud. My dad said it once, hospital bed, I was forty-three, he looked at the ceiling. At my oldest's high school graduation I told my wife in the stands I was so proud of him. She said tell him. I shook his hand and said good job, son. I gave the word to the air beside him and a lesser word to his face.

[BOARD: Six minutes is a feast.]


## SEGMENT 2 · Porch and a phone

WES: Saturday I sat him on the porch steps. One step up, because of my knees. His name first. I watched you pull over today. I am proud of the man you are turning into. He said okay. Ten seconds later, thanks. He went inside. That is the whole scene. I wanted a longer one. I did not get it.

JONATHON: Sunday you called the oldest.

WES: Twenty-one. I said I am proud of you. Not of a thing you did. You. He said, are you sick. I said no. He said huh. Then thanks, Dad. Fifteen minutes on a breaker panel. I let the panel be the rest of the love. I did not tack on a speech. I have tacked on too many speeches and watched the word die inside them.

JONATHON: I typed good call, bud, to Austin and stopped. Bud is my father's word. Birthday and Christmas. Hey, bud. I think the last time he said Jonathon out loud I was nine, in the kitchen, with his keys. At the first Thanksgiving after he left, relatives said I was the man of the house now. They did not say my name. They said a job. I have been giving my boys a job-word ever since.

WES: Count it. I want the number on the table, because men believe numbers when they will not believe a feeling.

JONATHON: Friday I opened the notes app. Thirty-four. Mostly Theo. Come on, bud. Not now, bud. Theo always says Daddy, the whole word. He has never shortened me. I have shortened him to a word that once meant I was being left. Three days I tried to stop. Ruby asked why I keep saying our names. I sound like a teacher. At bath I said Theo, you are a really good explainer. You explained a whole thing to a goat one time. He said the goat did not listen. The tally is down to nine. Nine is still a lot. Nine is not thirty-four.

[BOARD: The knee is a liturgy.]


## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: Isaiah forty-three, verse one. God does not ration a name.

JONATHON: "Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have summoned you by name; you are mine."

WES: By name. Mine. Not bud. Not sport. My granddad called me Sport. I would have walked through a fire for that man, and I cannot remember him saying Wes. I remember the warmth and I remember the hole. Both can be true. A nickname can be love. A nickname can also be how a man avoids the weight of a real name. We're about halfway. I want you to know which one you are doing.

JONATHON: I thought bud was warmth. It was a ghost. I was keeping my father in my mouth and putting him on my son. Theo does not need a ghost. He needs Daddy, and he needs Theo, and he needs one true thing I have watched him do. The goat is a true thing. I had never said it.

WES: The rep at your work walked away from money. You said his name first on a Monday call. That belongs here too. Work is allowed to be a place you practice a name.

JONATHON: Thirty-one. He walked away from a big deal rather than give a dishonest discount. Nine people on the call. I said his name first. You walked away from money last week because the number was not honest. That is the kind of rep I would buy from. He said thanks, boss. I did not say bud. I said his name. It felt like I had taken off a hat I did not know I was wearing.

[BOARD: Ephesians 6:4 (NIV) · "Fathers, do not exasperate your children; instead, bring them up in the training and instruction of the Lord."]


## SEGMENT 4 · Pushback

WES: Push. The man who says names are just words. And the man whose father never said proud, so he will not either.

JONATHON: Names are how a boy finds himself in a room. If the only word he gets is a leftover, he will spend years trying to guess whether he is a person or a job. I guessed job. I do not want that for Theo. Words are not just words. Isaiah did not think so.

WES: I used my dad's silence as a reason to stay silent. That is a cheap inheritance. You can break it on a porch in twenty seconds and a boy can still say okay and go inside. The break still happened. Do not wait for a movie reaction. You will use the missing movie as an excuse to never speak.

JONATHON: The man who was called man of the house at nine thinks a name is a luxury. It is not. A name is how you give a boy his age back. You are not the man of the house. You are nine. Nobody said that to me. I am trying to say the opposite to my son by saying Theo like it is enough.

[BOARD: The floor teaches first.]


## SEGMENT 5 · This week's challenge

WES: Use his name this week. First. Then one true thing you have watched. If proud is true, say proud. Do not say it to the air beside him. Say it to his face. If he says okay and leaves, let him leave.

JONATHON: Delete the nickname if it is doing a job a name should do. If you have a tally, write it down. You cannot fight a ghost you will not count. I will keep counting. I want nine to become four. I will not get cute and aim at zero so I can fail and quit.

WES: Last few minutes. If you have no son, say a younger man's name. A nephew. A rep. A boy on a field. The assignment is not only biological. It is a mouth that stops hiding inside a leftover word.

JONATHON: I will not send my father a lecture about names. That is a different week. This week is Theo and Ruby and a fridge with Steve on it. If I make this about Louisiana I will skip the bath. I am not skipping the bath.

WES: I keep hearing I know, you do it. He has been watching the knee since he was six. I was watching the procession. He was watching me. That is always how it works. I want to watch my own mouth the way he watched my knee.

WES: I keep wanting this to resolve in one conversation, and it will not. The assignment is the next honest sentence, said on time, without a speech attached. I have left too many good talks on a table because I wanted a better closing line than the one I had.

WES: I want this back in your house, not in ours. Pick the one door that is yours. Not your brother's. Not a man on a screen. Yours. Put a day on it before supper, even if the day is tomorrow and the sentence is clumsy.

JONATHON: Mine is already on a calendar. That is the only reason I trust it. A mood is not a plan, and I have used moods for years to sound wise while I stayed still. I do not want to sound wise. I want to be on time.

WES: If you fail the first try, the second try still counts. I have to say that every time or I will use the miss as a way out and then tell a story about the miss like the story was the obedience.

WES: I have watched men collect stories like this and never change a Tuesday. Do not be that man this week. Change one Tuesday. Then tell one person you did, because a secret obedience turns back into a mood by Thursday.

JONATHON: I am the man who collects stories. I have a notes app full of them. The only ones that count are the ones that got a day and a time and a person who knows I said I would do it.

WES: I want the listener who thinks this does not apply because his house is quieter. Quiet is not settled. You already know that if you have sat through a polite holiday and called it peace.

JONATHON: My house can be loud and still be a cease-fire. Volume is not the test. The test is whether the true thing got said to the person who had to hear it, not to a mug at this table.

## SEGMENT · Sitting with it

WES: I will check this on Friday, which is the only reason I trust myself. A public calendar is a kind of mercy for a man who likes his recliner and likes to call the recliner wisdom.

JONATHON: I will hold you to it, and I want somebody holding me. That is why I say the time out loud even when it makes me look late. Looking late is better than looking peaceful and being dug in.

WES: A steadfast man does the small thing while it is still small. That is the whole show, said again, because we forget it by Wednesday and start waiting to feel ready, and the ready never comes first.

JONATHON: I forget it by Tuesday afternoon. Ruby does not. She has a better memory for what I said I would do than any review I have ever run, and she does not care that I had a good reason.

WES: If this feels like too many stories, pick one. One house. One name. One time. That is enough weight for seven days, and it is more than most of us will actually carry if we get proud of the list.

JONATHON: I will pick one. If I pick three I will do none, and I already know that about myself from sales and from home. One is how a man like me gets free of his own notes app.

WES: I have a habit of saving the true sentence for a better room. There is no better room. The kitchen is the room. The truck is the room. The porch after the dishes is the room. Say it there.

JONATHON: I have rehearsed in better rooms my whole adult life. The rehearsal is how I stay safe. Dani can hear the difference between a rehearsal and a sentence. She stops me when I start handing people a door before they are in the room.

WES: Some of you will try to make this spiritual so you do not have to be specific. Prayer is good. A day and a name is still required. Do not baptize delay and call it discernment.

WES: The man who says he will start after the busy season is lying to himself the same way I did in my forties. There is always another season. Do the small thing inside the busy, or you will never do it.

JONATHON: HVAC sales does not hand you a quiet month. If I wait for one I will still be waiting when Theo has a license. I have to put the true thing on a Tuesday that already has a meeting.

WES: I do not need you to feel ready. I need you to be on time. Ready is a feeling. On time is a clock. I have trusted the feeling too long, and it has made me late to people I love.

JONATHON: I wait for ready like it is a weather window. Then the window closes and I tell a funny story about almost doing it. I do not want another funny story. I want a clumsy sentence that landed.

WES: Look at the next seven days and circle one hour. Not a vibe. An hour. If you cannot find an hour, you are not too busy. You are hiding inside busy, and I have done that with a laminated list and called it love.

JONATHON: I hide inside a full calendar better than anyone I know. A full calendar can still be a man running from one phone call. I am trying to leave a hole in Thursday on purpose.

WES: If your people are used to you disappearing into a screen after supper, tell them you are going to be late to the recliner this week. Then be late to the recliner. That is a kind of going first nobody applauds.

JONATHON: Theo notices the recliner more than he notices my speeches. If I stay at the table ten extra minutes he treats it like a holiday. That is how small this has to be to matter.

WES: I will not give you a poem. I will give you a time. If you need a poem after you have been on time, fine. Most of us want the poem first so we can skip the time.

JONATHON: I want the poem first. I always have. Then I get cute and the time slips. If I write the time on a sticky note where Dani can see it, I am much less poetic and much more likely to go.

WES: I have left good men as scenery. A table. A shop. A porch. Scenery does not call you when you fall. A name does. Stop collecting scenery and ask for a name.

JONATHON: I have a whole album of scenery. Nice men I never asked anything. I can admire a life from a folding chair and still go home unbrothered. I am done admiring from the chair.

## SEGMENT · Sitting with it

WES: If you only obey when the story is pretty, you will obey twice a year. Pretty is a trap. Ordinary is the field. Do the ordinary thing while nobody is making it a lesson.

JONATHON: I wait for pretty. Then I miss the ordinary and write a paragraph about the miss. I want fewer paragraphs and one ordinary Thursday that actually happened.

WES: Do not confuse a good conversation at this table with a changed kitchen. This table is practice. The kitchen is the game. I will not let us go home thinking practice was the game.

JONATHON: I am very good at practice. I can talk a good kitchen. Ruby can tell when I have only talked. She does not say it. She just watches whether I put the phone down.
