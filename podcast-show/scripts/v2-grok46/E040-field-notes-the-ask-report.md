JONATHON: And he said no, thank you, before you could explain why he should say yes.

## SEGMENT 1 · No, thank you

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the no, because I promised we wouldn't hide it. Behind the fellowship hall. Van on ramps. I brought coffee, black, one sugar. I said the whole sentence I had practiced in the truck.
JONATHON: The whole sentence. I've watched you three years. That's the ask you wrote on a card.
WES: I've watched you three years. Would you let me buy you breakfast and ask you. He said no, thank you.

JONATHON: Just no? I need the rest, because I will turn a short no into a verdict on every older man in Texas if you let me.

WES: He said it nicer than that. I'm not a talker, Wes. You know that. I would be no good to you across a table. I almost explained. I didn't. I said okay. Thank you for thinking about it. I will keep bringing your coffee.

JONATHON: You almost explained. Yeah, and—I would have explained. What'd he say from under the van after you let it sit?

WES: From under the van he said, black. One sugar. Then he rolled halfway out. Wes. You're not getting small.

JONATHON: That's a no to eggs and a yes to something else. I hate how clean that is. I wanted you to get a diner so I would feel braver about a porch. You got a sentence I would have paid for, and you still don't have a Thursday breakfast.

WES: I will keep bringing the coffee. A no to the table isn't a no to the man. Some of you will get that. Some of you will get a harder no. Don't turn either one into a story about how asking is foolish. You asked. That's the check.

[BOARD: A no can still be kind.]


## SEGMENT 2 · The card and a rivet

WES: We're about halfway. How is the card. Sunday is still coming. I don't want you adding a door to it because I got a no.

JONATHON: The card still says how did you stay. I rehearsed the ask on Dani Wednesday night in the kitchen. I got to, you don't have to answer it, and she stopped me.
WES: She stopped you. You were giving him the door before he's even in the room.
JONATHON: She said I was giving him the door before he's even in the room. I cut it. Sunday plan: after the dishes, pour his coffee on the back porch, ask right then. No warm-up. It's his coffee. I'm pouring it. That counts in that house.

WES: Good. Don't tell him about my mechanic. That's a door. Ask your question. Then listen. James is going to help us with that in a minute.

JONATHON: Shane's garage was Wednesday. The Texas is three feet of gray. Ruby didn't touch anything until he showed her one rivet on the back railing and let her glue it. The first piece anyone but Shane has ever touched. She told Theo she built a battleship. I didn't correct her. I wanted to. I let the sentence live.

WES: Let it live. A child who glues one rivet is allowed to say she built a ship. A man who asks one question is allowed to say he started a walk. We ruin both by being exact in the wrong direction.

[BOARD: Do not hand him the door.]


## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: James one, verse nineteen. A verse for men who want to explain a no and for men who want to decorate an ask.

JONATHON: "My dear brothers and sisters, take note of this: Everyone should be quick to listen, slow to speak and slow to become angry,"

WES: Quick to listen. Slow to speak. I almost explained to a man on a creeper why he should want breakfast with me. That's not listening. That's a sales close. I'm glad I stopped. The sentence he gave me from under the van wouldn't have survived my explanation.

JONATHON: I almost explained to Dani's dad in a rehearsal why he didn't have to answer. That's not kindness. That's me trying to lose on purpose so I can't be rejected for real. I cut the door. I'm still scared. Scared and slow to speak can live in the same body.

[BOARD: Proverbs 18:13 (NIV) · "To answer before listening— that is folly and shame."]


## SEGMENT 4 · From the table

WES: What if the man I ask says no. Then you keep the small kindness. I keep bringing coffee. You still pour his cup. You don't punish a no by disappearing. You also don't chase it for three months and call that faithfulness. Ask. Receive. Stay decent.

JONATHON: My old no was year one in San Antonio. Dani pregnant with Ruby. I asked a gray-haired neighbor three doors down to lunch. He said this month is slammed. Catch me later. I never did. I decided it was the answer from everybody. I only met you because a customer dragged me to a breakfast, and it still took me three months to text you. He probably meant later. I made it forever.

WES: Later isn't a lifetime unless you write it that way. The Sunday-text dad at the table asked an elder how he waited through six silent years. The elder said he was sorry. He could not go back in those years. The text still went out Sunday. Nothing back. Unresolved. We're not going to tidy that for the show.

JONATHON: I needed that left messy. I want a porch with an answer. I may get a short I don't know. I may get a no. I won't turn either one into forever. Not this time. I've already done that once and called it wisdom.

WES: Last few minutes. Tool is the coffee ask, word for word. I've watched you three years. Would you let me buy you breakfast and ask you one question. Then stop. If he says no, thank you, you say okay. Then you keep the small thing you were already doing. Coffee. A ride. A Thursday hello. Don't make the ask the whole relationship.

JONATHON: Mine is shorter. How did you stay. Then I drink my own coffee and I don't fill the mockingbird. If I come back with a paragraph about almost, don't let me call it a report.

[BOARD: The floor teaches first.]

WES: My oldest asked his foreman how a man stays with one outfit thirty years. The foreman said ask me tomorrow. Next day he handed over a notepad page, both sides. I won't repeat it. It's his. I said nothing about the job. I just sat there proud and quiet, which is a new trick for me.

JONATHON: I want a notepad page from Dani's dad. I may get four words. I'm trying to decide ahead of time that four words are enough. If I decide after, I will call it a miss and start a new notes-app novel.

WES: I keep wanting the no under the van to resolve into a diner, and it won't. Black. One sugar. That's still a yes to the man. The assignment is the next honest ask, said on time, without a speech attached. I've left too many good talks on a table because I wanted a better closing line than the one I had.

JONATHON: Honestly, I will try to add the speech anyway. Somebody should stop me. If you're sitting in a driveway doing that too, write the three sentences down before you dial, then read only those three. The fourth sentence is where I get cute and ruin it.

## SEGMENT · Sitting with it

WES: I want this back in your house, not in ours. Pick the one door that's yours. Not your brother's. Not a man on a screen. Yours. Put a day on it before supper, even if the day is tomorrow and the sentence is clumsy.

JONATHON: Mine is already on a calendar. That's the only reason I trust it. A mood isn't a plan, and I've used moods for years to sound wise while I stayed still. I don't want to sound wise. I want to be on time.

WES: If you fail the first try, the second try still counts. I've got to say that every time or I will use the miss as a way out and then tell a story about the miss like the story was the obedience.

JONATHON: I used the first miss as a way out for most of my twenties. I'm trying not to do that at thirty-seven. It's harder than any capacitor, and it's also more ordinary than I want to admit at this table.

WES: I've watched men collect stories like this and never change a Tuesday. Don't be that man this week. Change one Tuesday. Then tell one person you did, because a secret obedience turns back into a mood by Thursday.

JONATHON: I'm the man who collects stories. I've got a notes app full of them. The only ones that count are the ones that got a day and a time and a person who knows I said I would do it.

WES: I want the listener who thinks this does not apply because his house is quieter. Quiet isn't settled. You already know that if you have sat through a polite holiday and called it peace.

JONATHON: My house can be loud and still be a cease-fire. Volume isn't the test. The test is whether the true thing got said to the person who had to hear it, not to a mug at this table.

WES: I will check this on Friday, which is the only reason I trust myself. A public calendar is a kind of mercy for a man who likes his recliner and likes to call the recliner wisdom.

JONATHON: I will hold you to it, and I want somebody holding me. That's why I say the time out loud even when it makes me look late. Looking late is better than looking peaceful and being dug in.

WES: A steadfast man does the small thing while it's still small. That's the whole show, said again, because we forget it by Wednesday and start waiting to feel ready, and the ready never comes first.

JONATHON: I forget it by Tuesday afternoon. Ruby does not. She has a better memory for what I said I would do than any review I've ever run, and she does not care that I had a good reason.

WES: If this feels like too many stories, pick one. One house. One name. One time. That's enough weight for seven days, and it's more than most of us will actually carry if we get proud of the list.

JONATHON: I will pick one. If I pick three I will do none, and I already know that about myself from sales and from home. One is how a man like me gets free of his own notes app.
