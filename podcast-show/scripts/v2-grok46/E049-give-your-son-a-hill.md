## SEGMENT 1 · You too, bud

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the text, because it landed and you did not answer it. Dani said well, there's your bud. Ruby is making Grandpa in Louisiana a card, markers, a fish on it, half done on the counter. You left it there. Tell me why you did not answer.

JONATHON: Because if I answer I will start a conversation I have not chosen. He called me a dad with the same word he used when he left. I can hold both. I will not tidy it so this table feels resolved. I sent two words. He sent three. That is enough for a Sunday. The card can wait. I am still mad. Not yet is still not yet.

WES: My daughter said you are the best dad, Daddy, the way she does every year. This year I said only thank you, baby. She did not notice. She went on about her tomatoes. Something is eating them. I did not argue myself out of a blessing. That was the whole win. I wanted a parade. I got tomatoes. I am taking tomatoes.

JONATHON: Saturday I called the nephew. Name first. The blessing. He was crying and angry. Why are you doing this to me on a Saturday. You cannot just say stuff like that, man. He hung up. Ten minutes later, my roommate says that was nice. Dont make it weird. I did not make it weird. I said okay. That is a blessing that did not feel like a movie. It still landed in a roommate's mouth.

[BOARD: Two words can still sting.]


## SEGMENT 2 · A bus and a slope

WES: I called the twenty-four-year-old on the bus home. I asked if I could say a thing. He asked if somebody told me to call him. I said the donuts. The bus. I hope you get a car, and I will help you pick a used one. He said I do not want a car. I like the bus. I read on the bus. He wants his grandma to make it to Christmas. He said there are some old men singing at her place on Thursdays now. They are not bad. They are missing a guy. I did nothing. He has ears. I had the wrong hill. I listened. That was the blessing after I got it wrong.

JONATHON: You were going to give him a car like a prize. He wanted a grandmother at Christmas and a melody. I keep handing people the hill I would want. That is not going first. That is colonizing. I needed to hear you get it wrong on the air. Thank you.

WES: From about twelve, winter Saturdays on my granddad's place. Dad on the chainsaw ahead. Me with loppers behind. He said each cedar drinks thirty gallons a day. I believed it. Every one you cut gives some back. You will figure it out. At fifteen we cleared the south slope. Next summer the creek ran into July. On the Fourth he walked me down to look. No speech. Water. Saturday I took my youngest to the older brother's place. The north slope has gone back to cedar. The brother had said bring him, at the barn.

JONATHON: Your wife said no on the air, or she would have if we let her. Tell the disagreement. I do not want a clean hill. I want the marriage in it.

WES: She said no, you are not. He is sixteen. He has never run one. I said Dad had me on one at fourteen. She said your dad also let you ride in the back of the truck on the interstate. The compromise was chaps, a helmet with a face screen, the brother beside him for the first hour. She still did not like it. She said I am saying it now so you remember I said it. Neither of us is proven right. A quarter acre cleared. The boy asked how long to do the whole thing. Brother said years. The boy said okay. Can we come back next Saturday. I will walk him to the creek in August without a speech.

[BOARD: The bus can be the hill.]


## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: First Thessalonians two, eleven and twelve. A father who does more than name a boy.

JONATHON: "For you know that we dealt with each of you as a father deals with his own children, encouraging, comforting and urging you to live lives worthy of God, who calls you into his kingdom and glory."

WES: Encouraging. Comforting. Urging. Three verbs. I have done one at a time and called it fathering. A hill needs all three. Urge without comfort is a drill sergeant. Comfort without a hill is a recliner. We're about halfway. I changed my mind on this. I used to say do not rescue him. I now say be there, and do not take over. Those are different. One abandons. One stays and keeps his hands off the saw.

JONATHON: Man of the house was a hill with nobody on it. A job I could not do at nine. Urged, not comforted. I have been afraid to give Theo any hill because I remember that one. Then I watched him with shoes and I saw a hill that fits a four-year-old.

[BOARD: 1 Corinthians 16:13 (NIV) · "Be on your guard; stand firm in the faith; be courageous; be strong."]


## SEGMENT 4 · Shoes and a crew

WES: Tell the shoes. I want the listener who thinks a hill has to be cedar to hear Velcro.

JONATHON: Thursday night. Wrong feet. Folded Velcro. A crunchy sock. Nine minutes. I had my keys in my hand. I put them in my pocket and sat on the floor. We were six minutes late. Nobody noticed. Friday he did it unasked in seven. Dani walked past and said nothing. That nothing was a blessing. If she had clapped he would have performed. He just did the hill again.

WES: Good. A hill can be shoes. A hill can be a slope. A hill can be a Saturday ramp for a widow. Do not despise the small one because you saw a chainsaw on a show.

JONATHON: My go-first is yes to the church men's Saturday repair work. Ramps. Rails. I had said let me check with Dani three times. I said yes. I am taking Ruby, and Theo with a box of screws. If I only watch I will still be a man who talks about hills. If I hand a screw I am on one.

WES: Last few minutes. If your son only wants games, you still give him a Saturday that is real. We will talk about the cord and the router Friday. Today I want a hill with dirt or Velcro on it. Do not skip dirt because a screen is louder.

JONATHON: I will not answer the Father's Day text this week. I will sit on the floor for shoes. I will go on Saturday with a box of screws. If I stack a reply to Louisiana on top of that I will drop the screws. I am not dropping the screws.

[BOARD: Stay on the floor longer.]


## SEGMENT 5 · This week's challenge

WES: Name one hill. Put a younger man on it. Be there. Do not take over. If you were wrong about the hill, listen, and stay anyway. My young man did not want a car. I still owe him a name and a Thursday hello.

JONATHON: If you have no son, take a nephew or a boy with a box of screws. If you have a four-year-old, the hill may be shoes. If you have a sixteen-year-old, it may be cedar and a helmet your wife still hates. Do not hide behind the perfect hill.

WES: I will bring him back next Saturday. I will not make August at the creek a speech. Water is the speech. I have finally learned that from a man who is dead and a brother who says years.

JONATHON: I will set a Saturday alarm and put screws in a coffee can. If Theo drops them, we will pick them up. That is also a hill. Picking up what you dropped in a lot.

WES: I can still smell the cedar. Green. Sticky. A boy asking about years. Years is a good answer. I wanted to say we will crush it in a month. Years is how a hill stays a hill instead of becoming a stunt.

JONATHON: I can still feel the crunchy sock. I wanted to do it for him at minute two. Minute nine was the work. I am trying to love minute nine. It feels like I am late to everything. I may just be on time for him.

WES: I keep wanting this to resolve in one conversation, and it will not. The assignment is the next honest sentence, said on time, without a speech attached. I have left too many good talks on a table because I wanted a better closing line than the one I had.

JONATHON: Honestly, I will try to add the speech anyway. Somebody should stop me. If you are sitting in a driveway doing that too, write the three sentences down before you dial, then read only those three. The fourth sentence is where I get cute and ruin it.

WES: I want this back in your house, not in ours. Pick the one door that is yours. Not your brother's. Not a man on a screen. Yours. Put a day on it before supper, even if the day is tomorrow and the sentence is clumsy.

JONATHON: Mine is already on a calendar. That is the only reason I trust it. A mood is not a plan, and I have used moods for years to sound wise while I stayed still. I do not want to sound wise. I want to be on time.

WES: If you fail the first try, the second try still counts. I have to say that every time or I will use the miss as a way out and then tell a story about the miss like the story was the obedience.

JONATHON: I used the first miss as a way out for most of my twenties. I am trying not to do that at thirty-seven. It is harder than any capacitor, and it is also more ordinary than I want to admit at this table.

## SEGMENT · Sitting with it

WES: I have watched men collect stories like this and never change a Tuesday. Do not be that man this week. Change one Tuesday. Then tell one person you did, because a secret obedience turns back into a mood by Thursday.

JONATHON: I am the man who collects stories. I have a notes app full of them. The only ones that count are the ones that got a day and a time and a person who knows I said I would do it.

WES: I want the listener who thinks this does not apply because his house is quieter. Quiet is not settled. You already know that if you have sat through a polite holiday and called it peace.

JONATHON: My house can be loud and still be a cease-fire. Volume is not the test. The test is whether the true thing got said to the person who had to hear it, not to a mug at this table.

WES: I will check this on Friday, which is the only reason I trust myself. A public calendar is a kind of mercy for a man who likes his recliner and likes to call the recliner wisdom.

JONATHON: I will hold you to it, and I want somebody holding me. That is why I say the time out loud even when it makes me look late. Looking late is better than looking peaceful and being dug in.

WES: A steadfast man does the small thing while it is still small. That is the whole show, said again, because we forget it by Wednesday and start waiting to feel ready, and the ready never comes first.

JONATHON: I forget it by Tuesday afternoon. Ruby does not. She has a better memory for what I said I would do than any review I have ever run, and she does not care that I had a good reason.

WES: If this feels like too many stories, pick one. One house. One name. One time. That is enough weight for seven days, and it is more than most of us will actually carry if we get proud of the list.

JONATHON: I will pick one. If I pick three I will do none, and I already know that about myself from sales and from home. One is how a man like me gets free of his own notes app.

WES: I have a habit of saving the true sentence for a better room. There is no better room. The kitchen is the room. The truck is the room. The porch after the dishes is the room. Say it there.

JONATHON: I have rehearsed in better rooms my whole adult life. The rehearsal is how I stay safe. Dani can hear the difference between a rehearsal and a sentence. She stops me when I start handing people a door before they are in the room.

WES: Some of you will try to make this spiritual so you do not have to be specific. Prayer is good. A day and a name is still required. Do not baptize delay and call it discernment.

JONATHON: I have baptized delay. I have called it being careful. Careful is sometimes fear with a pressed shirt. I am trying to tell those two apart before I waste another month.

WES: The man who says he will start after the busy season is lying to himself the same way I did in my forties. There is always another season. Do the small thing inside the busy, or you will never do it.

JONATHON: HVAC sales does not hand you a quiet month. If I wait for one I will still be waiting when Theo has a license. I have to put the true thing on a Tuesday that already has a meeting.

WES: I do not need you to feel ready. I need you to be on time. Ready is a feeling. On time is a clock. I have trusted the feeling too long, and it has made me late to people I love.

JONATHON: I wait for ready like it is a weather window. Then the window closes and I tell a funny story about almost doing it. I do not want another funny story. I want a clumsy sentence that landed.

WES: Look at the next seven days and circle one hour. Not a vibe. An hour. If you cannot find an hour, you are not too busy. You are hiding inside busy, and I have done that with a laminated list and called it love.

JONATHON: I hide inside a full calendar better than anyone I know. A full calendar can still be a man running from one phone call. I am trying to leave a hole in Thursday on purpose.

## SEGMENT · Sitting with it

WES: If your people are used to you disappearing into a screen after supper, tell them you are going to be late to the recliner this week. Then be late to the recliner. That is a kind of going first nobody applauds.

JONATHON: Theo notices the recliner more than he notices my speeches. If I stay at the table ten extra minutes he treats it like a holiday. That is how small this has to be to matter.

WES: I will not give you a poem. I will give you a time. If you need a poem after you have been on time, fine. Most of us want the poem first so we can skip the time.

JONATHON: I want the poem first. I always have. Then I get cute and the time slips. If I write the time on a sticky note where Dani can see it, I am much less poetic and much more likely to go.

WES: I have left good men as scenery. A table. A shop. A porch. Scenery does not call you when you fall. A name does. Stop collecting scenery and ask for a name.

JONATHON: I have a whole album of scenery. Nice men I never asked anything. I can admire a life from a folding chair and still go home unbrothered. I am done admiring from the chair.
