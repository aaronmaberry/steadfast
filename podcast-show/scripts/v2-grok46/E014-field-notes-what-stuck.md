## SEGMENT 1 · The hidden thing and the will

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the receipt we will not read out loud, and then the one we will. I did the hidden thing. I will not tell you what it was. My wife asked. I said out. That is the whole report, and I want it to stay boring.

JONATHON: I did mine. I got as far as naming the day at supper and Dani cut me off, which is how I know I was about to turn it into a plaque. She has heard this show enough to know when I am about to collect. I wrote back to my nephew instead, because he texted "did it. not telling." and then a minute later, "is that right". I wrote, "That's right." That's the single check. A twenty-two-year-old understood the assignment better than I did at thirty-six.

WES: That's a good text. I want that to be the culture of this table. One man. Not the room. Now the will, because that one we can say. I have watched men hide a finished thing because they want a bigger audience, and I have watched men hide an unfinished thing because they want to look further along. Both hides are the same hunger. The will is the finished kind. Say it. The hidden thing is the other kind. Do not say it. Knowing the difference is part of becoming a man.

JONATHON: Thursday morning, ten o'clock, we signed at the lawyer's office at church. Dani signed faster than I did. I sat there with a pen and I thought about the pie and the dashboard light and the three years I drove around it. Then it was done. She said, "I want tacos." We ate tacos at eleven in the truck. I have never been happier about a boring hour and a greasy bag.

WES: That's the duffel, picked up. How did the guardian line feel once it was ink?

JONATHON: Heavy, and then lighter. We already decided her parents for now, my sister as backup, look again in five years. Seeing it on paper made it real in a way the kitchen talk did not. I cried in the truck after the tacos, which is a sentence I did not expect to say. Dani handed me a napkin and did not make a speech. That's a good wife.

[BOARD: Ink is heavier than a promise, and then it is lighter.]

WES: I remember the yellow stickers. I remember not knowing who was on mine until I was fifty. You know. That's a different kind of man than the one who ate tacos at eleven. I signed where I was told and I called it done. You sat in the decision. You named the people. You cried in a truck. I would rather be the man in the truck than the man with the stickers. I am trying to become him late, which is still allowed. Late is not the same as never. Never is what I did with the words until a hospital bed. I do not want never on a fan, or on a brother, or on a Thursday text.

## SEGMENT 2 · What stuck

WES: We're about halfway, and I want a clean list. What stuck in your house, and what did not. You first.

JONATHON: The phone off the table at supper stuck. Most nights. Ruby enforces it with a basket she labeled PHONE JAIL. She is seven and she is the sheriff. If I reach, she looks at me like I reached for a drink I quit. The coffee made before Dani gets home stuck, decaf now, because she wrote it on the note and I finally listened. What did not stick is the ten minutes in the morning. I tried to give God ten minutes before the email. I did it nine times in three weeks. The other mornings I opened the leaderboard in my head even after I moved the email. I want that on the record so nobody thinks I became a monk.

WES: Asking before deciding stuck. I asked Mom what she wanted. I asked my wife about the diner. I am slower now, and it is better. Tuesday PT with Mom stuck. We are side by side on those little tables and she still counts my reps. What did not stick is the clicking ceiling fan in the youngest's room. It has been clicking since winter and I keep saying I'll get to it. I said that about a lot of things before. I am putting a date on this one. Saturday the twenty-seventh if I can get my son-in-law, and if not, March first. I am saying it here so I cannot turn it into weather.

JONATHON: So the fan is your dashboard light now. Not a mystery. A date. I'll text you Thursday and ask if you bought the part.

WES: Text me. I have been answering "K" when you ask "Did you do it?" and "K" is not an answer. I have answered "K" twice since January, which is me hiding inside a letter again. Next Thursday I will write a sentence.

[BOARD: K is not a sentence. Write a sentence.]

JONATHON: I have texted you "Did you do it?" every Thursday since the middle of January. I will keep doing it. That's my Thursday text, and I think every man listening needs one man who will ask that and wait for more than a letter.

WES: That's the tool. The Thursday text. One sentence. Did you do it. Then a real answer. Look around your house this week. Who is allowed to ask you that? If the answer is nobody, that is the next duffel.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: I want the Bible open for one sentence we already know, because a review is not a new verse. It is a receipt on the old one. Luke sixteen, verse ten.

JONATHON: "Whoever can be trusted with very little can also be trusted with much, and whoever is dishonest with very little will also be dishonest with much." That's still the test. The basket. The fan. The letter K. None of those is a retreat. All of them are little.

[BOARD: Matthew 25:21 (NIV) · "His master replied, 'Well done, good and faithful servant! You have been faithful with a few things; I will put you in charge of many things. Come and share your master's happiness!'"]

WES: We have talked about sentences and weights and going first and cheap prizes. The little thing is still how you know if any of it is real. A man can love a big idea and cheat a small Tuesday. I do not want us to be those men. I can feel myself wanting Season Two to be a fresh start so I can drop the fan and the Thursday sentence. That's how I got fourteen months of delay on other things in this house. A new season is not a reset button. It is more Tuesdays. The click will still be there in March if I do not get on a ladder.

JONATHON: I'll be real with you, I want a new idea because a new idea would let me restart. A review does not let you restart. It asks what you actually kept. I kept the basket. I dropped the morning ten. I signed the will. I still have not finished October with Dani. Started is still the honest word on that one.

WES: Started is allowed. Lying about started is not. If it is not finished, say so. Then put a date on the next inch.

## SEGMENT 4 · One thing to keep

WES: Last few minutes. Each of us keeps one thing. Not five. One. You first.

JONATHON: Supper with the phone in jail. That's the one. If I keep that, Ruby keeps seeing a dad who can be in a room. If I drop that, the rest is talk. I can lose the morning ten and still be a man at six-fifteen. I cannot lose the table and call this a life.

WES: I'm keeping the fan date, and I'm keeping the asking. I will not decide Mom's life in a hallway. I will not decide my son's job from articles. I will ask, and then I will do the small thing I said I would do. The fan is the picture. It clicks every night and everybody in that room hears that I have not gotten to it. That's enough sermon. My youngest has started timing the click. He thinks it is funny. It is not funny. It is a metronome of I'll get to it. I am done letting a ceiling preach in my house.

JONATHON: From the table, the question this week is, what do we keep doing from these first weeks? Not what sounded good. What stayed. If nothing stayed, pick one little thing and start it tonight. Do not wait for a new season to become a different man.

WES: If you live alone, your one thing might be the Thursday text. Send it to a brother. Ask him what he did. Then answer his. Two men, two sentences, every Thursday. That's a church that fits in a pocket. Do not wait until you have a table full of kids to need a brother. You need one now. The empty apartment is not a reason to skip the text. It is the reason.

[BOARD: Keep one small thing. Drop the rest of the speech.]

JONATHON: And if you have a nephew or a roommate who only texts, that still counts. Mine is not coming over. He is not sitting in this room. He is a text, and the text is enough if I answer it like a man.

WES: That's enough for a Friday. We are not finishing the year today. We are naming what we will still be doing in March. I want you to write your one thing on a card and put it where you will see it on Thursday. Not a paragraph. One line. Then send the picture to one man, or don't, if it would turn into a plaque. The card is for you. The text is for the brother. Those are different jobs.

JONATHON: I'll write "PHONE JAIL" on mine, which is already written, because Ruby beat me to the card. That's fine. She can be the sheriff. I just have to stay a deputy who actually shows up. If I start deputizing the whole house and then sneak my phone under the table, she will know, and God will know, and I will know, and knowing is not the same as keeping.
