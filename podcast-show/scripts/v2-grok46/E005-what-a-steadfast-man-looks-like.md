## SEGMENT 1 · The sentence

WES: Pull up a chair. Friday we said leave the back of the card blank. Today we fill it in. I'm going to say the sentence once. Just once. Then we take it apart, one piece at a time, and I want you to see each piece on a regular day. Because a sentence you can't picture on a Tuesday is just a slogan.

JONATHON: And we don't do slogans. I've sat through enough of those at work to last me until I retire, and none of them ever followed me home.

WES: [turns his mug a quarter turn] On a regular Tuesday, a steadfast man stops waiting, picks up the weight, goes first, and works for God's pay, not the room's.

JONATHON: [writing, then reading it back] Okay. Got it. Can I say what I like about it before we take it apart? It's all verbs. Stops, picks up, goes, works. There's no adjective in there. It doesn't say a man is strong or tough or tall.

WES: Thank goodness. Nobody's ever called me tall.

JONATHON: Theo says I'm medium. You'd be medium too. And that's the point, isn't it? You can't feel your way into this. You can only do it. A man who's not tall, not loud, not anything the internet says a man is supposed to be, can do every word of it by Thursday.

WES: And a man who's all of those things can fail every word of it. Easily. I've met him. Lots of times. There's four. We call them the four marks. And there's no fifth.

JONATHON: Why not five? Add "prays." Add "loves his wife." Add "grills." I can keep going if you let me, and some guy on my team would add a fifth just to have a poster.

WES: Prayer's in all four. Loving your wife is in all four. Grills isn't. No fifth.

## SEGMENT 2 · The first two

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the first two, because they show up in a house before they show up in a verse. Piece one. When it's yours to do, start now, before you feel like it. James four, seventeen, in plain words. If you know the good you ought to do and you sit on it, James doesn't call that a personality type.

JONATHON: And the picture used to be your gutter. Used to be. Because I want the man listening to hear that it didn't stay loose for another season.

WES: The gutter is done. Saturday. Eighty-one-year-old held the ladder. He supervised. What's left at my house is a ceiling fan in my youngest's room that clicks, and a call I still haven't made. Waiting doesn't feel like doing anything wrong. It feels like nothing. I didn't decide to leave the gutter. I just never decided to fix it.

JONATHON: It feels like peace. And it's not. I already know we're going to spend a whole Tuesday on that, because I've got my own version, and it's not a porch.

WES: Piece two. Carry what's yours, even when somebody else dropped it. Paul told the Galatians each one should carry their own load. Your load. Not everybody's. Not nobody's. Yours.

JONATHON: Okay, this one's mine. Can I take this one? Because "even when somebody else dropped it" is my whole life. My dad dropped a lot of weight when he drove off. A family. Two kids. A wife working two jobs. And for a long time I thought, that's his weight, he dropped it, why should I have to carry it?

WES: That's a fair question.

JONATHON: Yeah, and— it's a fair question I used for years. But here's what I figured out, slowly, mostly at breakfast with you. I don't carry his weight. I can't. That's his. He'll answer for that. But my kids are mine. My marriage is mine. How I treat my mom now, that's mine. And the stuff he left in me, the anger, the "win the room," that's in my house now, so whether I like it or not, it's mine to deal with.

WES: You didn't drop it. But it landed in your yard.

[BOARD: What sits in the grass still needs a hand.]

JONATHON: It landed in my yard. I can stand there and yell about who dropped it, or I can pick it up. It's in my yard either way. And if you've never had a dad drive off, the picture's smaller, but it's the same. The dishes you walked past. The bill you let your wife worry about. The thing at work that fell between two desks, and one of them's yours.

WES: That's it. It's yours, put a hand on it.

## SEGMENT 3 · The second two

WES: Piece three. Step out ahead of your people, scared or not. John ten. Jesus is talking about a shepherd, and the shepherd goes on ahead, and they follow him because they know his voice. A shepherd doesn't push from behind with a stick. He walks in front.

JONATHON: And the picture is the text. The scariest text I ever sent a man. Somebody had to go first. You weren't going to text me. You didn't have my number.

WES: You had to go get mine from the guy who invited you. You went first. That's this one. At home it looks like the first apology after a fight. The first one to bring up the hard thing. The first one to pray at the table.

JONATHON: Okay, so this is the one I'm worried about. I think guys misuse it. "Goes first" turns into "I'm in charge, I decide, I go first through the door, I get the remote." Guys hear "lead" and they hear "boss."

WES: That's a real worry. Going first means going first into the cost. Not first to the good seat. The shepherd goes first because the front is where the wolf is. If your going first never costs you anything, you're not leading. You're just cutting in line.

[BOARD: Lead toward the cost, not the chair.]

JONATHON: So not first to the remote. Into the cost. The first apology costs you. The hard conversation costs you. The prayer out loud costs you when you've never done it. I still think guys are going to misuse it, and I'm going to bring this up again when we get to that one on purpose.

WES: Some will. Guys misuse everything. I'm counting on you to bring it up. Piece four. Do it for the Lord's reward, not the room's applause. Paul told the Colossians, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters. Then he says you'll receive an inheritance from the Lord as a reward.

JONATHON: Okay, so I have to ask, because I grew up Easter-and-Christmas. "Pay." Does that mean I'm earning my way in? Because I've heard you say you can't earn it, and I need that said plain if we're going to put the word "pay" in a man's mouth.

WES: You can't. You don't work for God's pay to get in the family. You're already in the family. Look at the word he uses. Inheritance. You don't earn an inheritance. You get it because you're a son. A son works his father's land because it's his father's, and someday it's his. Not to get adopted. He's already adopted.

JONATHON: Okay. That helps. Because for me the opposite of God's pay is the room's pay. The leaderboard. The parents at coach-pitch saying "great job, Coach." That's the room. That's my pay. I've been working for it since I was twelve. And it's great for about an hour. Then it's gone. And you need it again tomorrow.

WES: That's the thing about the room's pay. It doesn't keep. God's pay keeps.

JONATHON: So the front of my card is "Win the room." And the back of my card ends with "not the room's." You planned this a little.

WES: [sips coffee] I planned it a little.

## SEGMENT 4 · Which one is hardest

WES: We're about halfway. Before we open the Bible, I want this on the table. Which one's the hardest? For most men. You've sat with me at enough breakfasts to have an opinion, and I've sat with hundreds of guys.

JONATHON: I think it's the pay one. And I know you're going to say waiting, so I'm going to say mine first so I don't just nod.

WES: Waiting. Not even close. Because every other one starts with it. You can't pick up the weight if you're waiting. You can't go first if you're waiting. Most men I've sat with aren't bad men. They're waiting men. Waiting for the kids to get older, waiting for the job to settle, waiting to feel ready.

JONATHON: See, I think the pay one is underneath the other three. Why do I wait? Because the thing I'm waiting on doesn't pay. The room won't clap for it. Nobody claps when you do bath. Nobody claps when you pick up a weight somebody else dropped. Going first into the cost, by definition, nobody's clapping. A man who's still working for the room will wait on everything the room doesn't see.

WES: [beat] Huh. That's a "I'm going to think about that on the drive home" huh. I'll say this. You might be right about you. A man who grew up winning rooms, the pay is probably the root. A man like me, who grew up not making trouble, I think the waiting's the root. I didn't need applause. I needed comfort.

JONATHON: So it depends on the guy. Which is why the challenge is going to be, find yours. And we're going to keep arguing about this. Fair warning. I don't fold just because you said "huh."

WES: Good. Don't.

## SEGMENT 5 · The Word

WES: Now. Where does a sentence like this come from? Because I don't want anybody thinking we made up four things that sounded good on a coffee mug. It starts with one line in Philippians. You read it.

JONATHON: "In your relationships with one another, have the same mindset as Christ Jesus:"

WES: And then Paul tells you what that mindset was. In the next few verses, he says Jesus was God, and he didn't hold on to that for his own advantage. He made himself nothing. He took the nature of a servant. He humbled himself and obeyed, all the way to a cross.

[BOARD: Philippians 2:4 (NIV) · "not looking to your own interests but each of you to the interests of the others."]

JONATHON: So where are the four in that? Because I don't want a mug. I want to see him.

WES: Look at him. He didn't wait for us to get better. He came. That's the first one. He carried what wasn't his, our sin, and he didn't put it down. That's the second. He went ahead of us, into the worst of it, first. That's the third. And he did it for the Father's joy, not the crowd's. The crowd was yelling "crucify." He wasn't working for the room.

[BOARD: Hebrews 12:2 (NIV) · "fixing our eyes on Jesus, the pioneer and perfecter of faith. For the joy set before him he endured the cross, scorning its shame, and sat down at the right hand of the throne of God."]

JONATHON: [quietly] He definitely wasn't winning the room. He lost the room completely. On purpose. For us.

WES: So the four marks aren't four rules a man adds to his life. They're what that one mindset looks like on a man. On a Tuesday. In a house where the gutter is finally hung, and the fan still clicks.

## SEGMENT 6 · Pushback

WES: Jonathon, you've been sitting on a sales-training objection the whole time. Go ahead and push.

JONATHON: It's a list. I've been to a lot of sales trainings. They all have a list. The five C's of closing. The four P's of something. And I've never once changed because of a list. Lists don't change people. So why would this one?

WES: You're right. A list never changed anybody. I'll agree with that all day. The sales list is a technique. It's for getting something. This is a description. It's what a man looks like when Jesus is changing him. So which is it for you this week, a list you can ignore, or a mirror you have to hold up?

JONATHON: So it's more like a mirror than a to-do list. You hold it up on a Tuesday night and you ask, did I wait on anything today? Did I pick up what was mine? Did I go first anywhere? And who was I working for? And most nights, you'll get one out of four.

WES: One out of four is a good night. Some nights you'll get zero. I get zero sometimes.

[BOARD: A mirror, not a technique.]

JONATHON: Second problem. These aren't just for men. Dani does all four of these better than I do. She goes first constantly. She picks up everybody's weight. She works nights for patients who'll never remember her name. Isn't this just "be a good Christian"?

WES: In one way, yes. These are Christlike, and that mindset is for everybody. Paul wrote Philippians to the whole church. Women and men. So yes, Dani's doing them, and we'd all do well to watch her. But here's why we say them to men, on purpose. Men tend to fail at them in a particular way. Go all the way back to the garden. When things went wrong, the man was standing right there and he didn't say a word. He waited. He let somebody else carry it. He didn't go first. We don't usually fail by being too much. We fail by being absent while standing in the room.

JONATHON: [quietly] I can do that with my kid on my lap. That's the recliner. That's 1604. That's the gutter before it got fixed. It's all the same story. It's just different furniture.

WES: Let me run it through two other chairs, then you take one. Twenty-two, first job, two roommates. Show up like it matters before anybody's watching. Your own money, your own mess, your own laundry. Going first might be the first guy in your friend group to say, "I think we should go to church." God's pay is, you're working for somebody besides the guys you're trying to impress.

JONATHON: I spent my twenties working for the guys I was trying to impress. They don't even text me now. Sixty-five, retired, wife passed two years ago. You take that one, because I will get it wrong if I guess.

WES: That man's waiting looks like the recliner, and nobody'd blame him. Picking up the weight might be his grandkids. Going first might be calling his grown son first, after years of the son always calling him. And God's pay matters most to him now, because the room's mostly gone. The people who used to clap aren't there. He's finding out who he was working for.

JONATHON: Divorced dad. Kids every other weekend. Stop waiting means don't wait for a better custody schedule to be a real dad. Use the weekend you've got. Pick up the weight means his part of what broke, and only his part. Going first is being decent at the handoff in the parking lot. And God's pay means you're not trying to be the fun parent so the kids like your house better. That's room pay too. Give me a second with that divorced-dad picture.

## SEGMENT 7 · What we actually did

WES: Last few minutes on what we actually did with it. I wrote the sentence on the back of my card and put it in my Bible. Then I did the one-out-of-four thing for five nights. Before bed, I'd look at the card and ask which one I'd done that day.

JONATHON: What was your score, and I want the ugly nights, not the Friday you already know how to tell.

WES: Monday, one. I picked up a weight. My youngest dented the truck, I didn't yell, I called the body shop myself instead of making him do it. Which, now that I say it out loud, maybe he should've called.

JONATHON: That might be the opposite of pick up the weight. That might be picking up his. I'll give you a half, and I'm being generous because you didn't yell.

WES: Tuesday, zero. Wednesday, one. Thursday, zero. Friday, two. Friday I went first. My wife and I had a disagreement about Christmas money that had been sitting there since Christmas. I brought it up. I didn't wait for her to. And then I picked up the part that was mine, which was that I'd said yes to a thing for my brother without asking her.

JONATHON: The brother you haven't called.

WES: Different brother. I've got two. I haven't called the other one either. What about you?

JONATHON: I put the card in my truck visor like I said. Monday I had a call with a customer, a big one. Their order was going to be three weeks late. Our warehouse messed up. And my whole instinct was to win the room. Make it sound fine. Blame the manufacturer. Promise something I couldn't promise. I had the visor down. I saw the card. "Not the room's." And I just told him the truth. Our warehouse messed up, it's three weeks, here's what I can actually do, here's what I can't.

WES: What'd he say?

JONATHON: He was mad. For about two minutes. Then he said, "Thanks for not jerking me around." And he didn't cancel. That feels like room pay, because he kept the order, and I need you to tell me if I'm cheating.

WES: The order's a nice bonus. God's pay was you being a man who told the truth when lying would've been easier. Nobody saw that but God and your visor.

JONATHON: My visor saw it. So here's the challenge, because I don't want a man walking out with four things and no next step. Write it on the back of your card. Then pick the one mark you're weakest in. Just one. Tell your one man which one.

WES: Married guys, ask your wife which one she sees you do best. Not worst. Best. Last time was rough on some of us. This week you get to hear something good. Then ask her which one she'd love to see more, if she wants to tell you.

JONATHON: Single guys, ask a friend the same two questions. Best one, and one he'd like to see more. And everybody, every night this week, do the one-out-of-four check before bed. Takes thirty seconds. I will forget two nights if I don't put the card where I actually am at ten o'clock.

WES: Tape the card where your eyes already go at ten o'clock. Then look at it.
