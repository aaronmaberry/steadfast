## SEGMENT 1 · Pen

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on a calendar, because Friday is the receipt. You told her. She wrote in pen. That's a woman putting it on a table.

JONATHON: My sideways thing is being the loud guy. Practice. The pipeline call. Supper. One thing I miss: about seven, steering his truck in the church parking lot on Sunday afternoons, in his lap, him on the pedals. She put her hand on my knee. I haven't told Ruby yet. Her day's in February. A reminder a week ahead: Dad calls next week.

[BOARD: Pen is how a date becomes a place.]

WES: I called my brother Sunday. A long quiet. Well. Then, I'll be at the barn by two. The youngest said, do I have to talk. No. Okay. I'll come. You still disagree. I'm bringing him because he asked to come, not because I drafted him.

## SEGMENT 2 · Five dates

WES: Wednesday at supper I told my wife how my dad said my name coming in from the barn, like a question. Wes? She told hers: he always asked about her garden first. How's the okra. That night I wrote five dates. Dad's day. Dad's birthday. Granddad's. Mr. Abernathy's. My mother-in-law's, because she goes quiet every April. On Abernathy's day I order his breakfast every year.

JONATHON: That's an anniversary calendar. Not a mood. A list. I needed to see five. I have been acting like grief is one day in February.

WES: Tool this week is the anniversary calendar. Write the dates. Tell one person what you do on them. A breakfast. A tailgate. A pen.

JONATHON: The 61-year-old rep, mother died in March at eighty-nine. A sister. He told me I'm just sad. Then the exit to her house. Then the chicken-fried steak in Uvalde. I'll text him on her birthday in November.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: We're about halfway, and I want one verse for the man who thinks grief has a deadline. Psalm thirty-four, verse eighteen.

JONATHON: "The Lord is close to the brokenhearted and saves those who are crushed in spirit."

[BOARD: Romans 12:15 (NIV) · "Rejoice with those who rejoice; mourn with those who mourn."]

WES: Close. Not finished. Brokenhearted is a pliers year and a mug in a cabinet. Crushed is a day after a birthday you refuse to name. He is close. You don't have to hurry up.

JONATHON: How long is it supposed to take. That's the table question. The psalm does not give a number. It gives a nearness. I wanted a number so I could be done. Nearness is better and harder.

WES: Context in two sentences. This psalm is a man who has been in trouble and is teaching others the taste of that nearness. It is not a schedule. It is a location. Close.

## SEGMENT 4 · What we're carrying

WES: Last few minutes. I'll be at the barn at two. I'll say one thing I miss. I'll let the boy be quiet. On Abernathy's day I'll order the eggs. That's the calendar. The fan got a Saturday in February. The back door is still waiting. I am not calling it the last thing. I am calling it the thing I still lift every time I go to the porch.

JONATHON: I'll keep the pen. I'll tell Ruby in February, not on a porch in October. I'll text the 61-year-old in November. I will not ask him if he's over it. I'll keep asking about the jamb too. You can keep not finishing it. Just don't pretend a quiet fan closed the list.

[BOARD: 1 Thessalonians 4:13 (NIV) · "Brothers and sisters, we do not want you to be uninformed about those who sleep in death, so that you do not grieve like the rest of mankind, who have no hope."]

WES: We grieve. Not like men with no hope. Hope is not a hurry. Hope is a table you keep setting.

JONATHON: Look around your house this week. Write the dates. Tell one person. Sit where a brother can find you. If you fail and go sideways, name the sideways and come back.
