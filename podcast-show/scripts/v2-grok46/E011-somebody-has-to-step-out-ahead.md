## SEGMENT 1 · The coffee and the talk

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on two kitchens. Mine first, then Sunday at yours.

JONATHON: Yours first, because I need the eleven days. I'm a same-day guy. If I do a good thing I want it noticed before lunch.

WES: After the fog years, I was looking for a first move that wasn't a speech. She had been making the coffee since before the kids could drive. I started getting up at five-fifteen and making it. I didn't announce it. I didn't leave a note. Day eleven she came in, tasted it, and said, "Why are you doing this?" I said, "Because I want to." She cried into the mug. I stood there like a man who had broken something. I hadn't. I had just finally moved. I want you to hear how ordinary that is. It was coffee. It wasn't a retreat. It wasn't a speech at church. It was a pot and a clock and a man who stopped letting her be the only one awake for the house.

JONATHON: She cried into the mug. Yeah, and— that's going first when it's your idea. Nobody asked. You saw it. I'll be real with you, Sunday wasn't that. Sunday I caught up. She had been out front for four months on the October fight, and I finally sat down. I'm not sure catching up counts as going first. I think going first is when you see it before anybody says it. The will was a little bit of that. Sunday wasn't. Sunday I was late to my own house. I need to know if late still counts, because if it doesn't I will use that as a reason to quit.

WES: Catching up still costs you. You still have to open your mouth. Late still counts if you actually do it. Quitting because you were late is just another wait. Tell Sunday.

JONATHON: After the kids were down I said, "I've been making you pay for my mom." I didn't dress it up. I didn't explain my childhood for twenty minutes first. She said, "Keep going." So I did. I told her I've been building every holiday around whether my mom will be okay, and I've been handing Dani the bill. I told her I was sorry for the October night we left on the floor. She cried a little. Then she said, "I need you to tell your mom we are hosting Easter. At our house. And I need you to tell her, not me." That was the part I wanted her to do. I wanted a buffer. She wouldn't be the buffer.

WES: So you went first twice. Once in the kitchen with her, and once toward your mom.

JONATHON: Thursday at seven I call my mom. I put it on the calendar next to the will signing. I already know what she'll text back. "Is everything ok??" With two question marks. That's how she hears a plan that didn't come from her. I've spent years managing her temperature by never being the one who changes the plan. Going first toward her is going to feel like kicking a hornet nest I've been feeding sugar. I still have to dial. What do I say if she asks if everything's okay? Because "yes" is a lie and "no" is a speech.

[BOARD: First is a direction, not a trophy.]

WES: You say, "Everything's okay, and I have something to tell you." Then you tell her. Look around your house this week. If you have been waiting for her to start the hard talk, that wait has a name. It's not patience. It's her carrying the opener again.

## SEGMENT 2 · The stop sign and Mom

WES: Saturday I was driving my sixteen-year-old out to my brother's place, one of the twelve. I had yelled at him about the rolling stop. In the truck I tapped his arm. He took one earbud out, which is the most you get. I said, "I yelled at you about the stop sign. The yelling was wrong. The stop sign wasn't."

JONATHON: How long until he said anything, because a sixteen-year-old can sit in a quiet truck like it's a career. I would've filled that quiet in the first mile. I always do that.

WES: Twenty minutes. Then he said, "Sorry about the stop sign." That was it. I didn't get a speech. I got a sentence. Going first with your kid is often that. You say the hard line, and you ride in the quiet, and you don't fill it with a lecture so you can feel done.

JONATHON: I would have filled it. I fill quiet the way I fill a pipeline. That's the sales habit I have to kill at home. If there's a gap I put words in it, and then I call those words leadership. They're not. They're me being uncomfortable. The earbud version is better. Say the true line and then let the road do the rest.

WES: Today at two I sit in Mom's doctor appointment with my older brother. Before I left the house I called her and asked what she wants. She said, "Nobody's asked me that." She wants to stay in her house. She'll take help cleaning every two weeks. She won't have a walker in the living room. She'll use it in the bathroom. The thing she cares about most is driving herself to church on Sunday. She'll give up the grocery store before she gives up church. I wrote it down while she talked, because I knew I would start improving it in the truck. I don't get to improve her life without her in the sentence.

JONATHON: You asked her before you and your brother decided for her. That's going first toward her, not around her. A lot of sons plan a mother's life in a parking lot and call it honor.

WES: My first thought, when he asked me to come, was I would listen to the doctor and then he and I would figure out what Mom needs. That's the old move. Two men, a plan, her as the project. I don't want that to be the day.

JONATHON: What if the doctor and your brother want something she doesn't? Do you go first toward her, or toward the plan that feels safer?

WES: Toward her. I can live with a brother being mad at me. I can't live with my mom finding out her life got decided in a hallway. I'll tell you Friday how the room went.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: We've got a little under fifteen minutes, and I want the Bible open, because the third mark has a picture. John ten, verse four. Jonathon.

JONATHON: "When he has brought out all his own, he goes on ahead of them, and his sheep follow him because they know his voice."

[BOARD: John 10:11 (NIV) · "I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep."]

WES: Jesus is talking about himself. The good shepherd. He does not drive the sheep from behind with a stick. He goes on ahead. They follow because they know his voice. Going first, for a Christian man, isn't a personality. It's copying the man who already walked out in front.

JONATHON: Okay, hold on. My wife has had faith since she was a kid. She sings alto. She knows where things are without the tabs. If going first means I have to be ahead of her in the Bible, I will be waiting until Theo is in college. So do I lead even if she is more mature in faith than I am?

WES: You lead as the man in the house, not as the scholar in the house. Going first can be, "I don't know how. Will you come with me?" That's still first. First is direction. It's not a test score. I've sat with a lot of men who are waiting to feel qualified. Qualification does not arrive. Obedience does, and it usually looks a little clumsy the first month. Your house does not need a polished man. It needs a man who will stand up and start.

JONATHON: That's the line I needed, because I've been using her maturity as a reason to stay in the doorway. If she's better at it, I let her start. Then I call that humility. It's not humility. It's me keeping my hands clean.

WES: Same thing I did with the coffee for twelve years. She was better at the morning. I let her stay better at it. Going first was me taking a turn I should have taken a long time ago.

[BOARD: Hebrews 10:24 (NIV) · "And let us consider how we may spur one another on toward love and good deeds,"]

## SEGMENT 4 · Pushback

WES: Jonathon, push. I know you've got more than the alto question.

JONATHON: Here's my problem with "go first." It can turn into a man making decisions and calling it leadership. I've watched guys pick the church, the budget, the holiday, and never ask. That's not a shepherd. That's a man who likes the sound of his own boots. So where's the line between going first and just running the room?

WES: The line is whether you asked, and whether you can be told no. A shepherd goes ahead and still knows the sheep. If your "first" can't survive her saying "not that," it wasn't first. It was control.

JONATHON: Second one. What if going first makes it worse? I tell my mom we're hosting Easter, and she hears "you are not the center." I can't control that hearing. So part of me wants to wait until it feels kinder.

WES: Kinder to who? If you wait until it feels kinder, you're waiting on a feeling again. You already picked a night. Thursday at seven. Say the sentence. Let her have her two question marks. Then stay on the phone.

JONATHON: Third, and this is the guy with no wife. Or the guy whose wife left. Going first at an empty table sounds like a poster.

WES: Then go first toward a brother, or toward your kids on your weekend, or toward the man at work nobody checks on. Going first isn't a marriage-only move. It's a direction. Somebody in your week is waiting to see if a man will move. I think about the apprentice on my oldest son's crew, the quiet one with a baby coming. Nobody asked him anything until a man put a phone in a truck and asked. That was first. It was also just lunch.

JONATHON: I can work with that. The single version isn't "wait until you have a kitchen." It's "pick a person and take the first step this week." I'll take that over a poster.

WES: There's an older man at our church, eighty-one, retired Air Force, sits behind me. He asked if he could start a Saturday morning at the church for single moms' cars. Oil, tires, the simple stuff. His idea. His ask. I said I'd make the coffee. That's going first at eighty-one. He didn't wait for a committee.

JONATHON: That's the mechanic. I want that man on the show one day, and I know you will never let me ask him on a Friday. Here's what gets me. He's eighty-one and he is still looking for a first move. I'm thirty-six and I still wait for Dani to write the script. If he can start a Saturday for women he barely knows, I can call my own mother without a buffer.

WES: You can ask him yourself. That's the whole point. And I want the listener to hear the age in that story. Going first isn't a young man's mark. It's a man's mark. Some of you're fifty-two like me and you still want a committee. Some of you're twenty-two and you think you have to wait until you have a title. The mechanic did not wait for a title. He asked. I said I'd make the coffee. That's the whole partnership. One man starts. The other man shows up.

## SEGMENT 5 · What we're actually doing

WES: We're about halfway through the last stretch. What are you actually doing?

JONATHON: Thursday at seven I call my mom. The sentence is, "We're hosting Easter lunch at our house, and I want you there, and I'm telling you myself." Then I shut up. Dani already said keep going. I don't get to outsource the second half. Saturday the thirteenth we've got a reservation, because she works Sunday night. I picked the place. I didn't ask her to plan her own Valentine's after a shift.

WES: Good. And the coffee?

JONATHON: I left it made this week with a note. We'll see if she writes back. I'm trying not to collect pay on it. That's harder than the note.

WES: I'm in the appointment at two. I will start with, "Mom, tell the doctor what you told me." Then I listen. If my brother bristles, I stay in the chair. I don't leave early to beat traffic this time. I already know the room will want a man with a plan. I want to be the man who makes space for her sentence before anybody improves it. That may be the first time I have gone first in that family without trying to win.

JONATHON: And the sixteen-year-old. Are you going first again this week, or was the earbud the whole thing?

WES: The earbud was the start. I want to say one true thing in the truck each Saturday that's not about the dent. Last week it was the yelling. This week it might just be, "I'm glad you're in the truck." That still counts as first.

JONATHON: I'm going to need that in writing, because I will usually wait for a bigger moment and miss the truck.

[BOARD: The first move can be one true sentence.]

WES: There's a man at the Thursday table who is divorced. His kids are with him this week. I told him, help them make a valentine for their mom. Buy the paper. Sit with them. That one costs him something, and they will remember who went first.

JONATHON: That's a hard one, and it's the right kind of hard. Going first isn't winning the old fight. It's doing the next right thing in front of the kids.

## SEGMENT 6 · This week's challenge

WES: Last few minutes on the challenge. One first move this week. Not five. One. If you pick five you will do none of them and then tell yourself the assignment was too big. Pick the one that makes your stomach tight. That's usually the right one.

JONATHON: Married men, go first toward her on something she has been carrying the opener for. A talk, a plan, an apology, a reservation. Don't make her start it again. And don't turn it into a presentation. One sentence, then listen longer than you want to. That's the whole move.

WES: If you live alone, go first toward one man or toward your mother or toward the person at work nobody checks on. A text is a start. A phone call is better. Don't wait until you feel like a leader. Feelings don't go first. You do.

JONATHON: Dads, go first toward your kid with one sentence that's not a correction. In the car is fine. In the doorway is fine. Don't wait for a clean moment that never arrives. I've been saving good words for a better night, and the better night is never on the calendar. Ruby does not need a speech. She needs me to go first on a regular Tuesday while the watch is beeping.

WES: And if your first move is toward God, say one sentence out loud at the table this week. You don't have to know more than she does. You have to start. Clumsy and out loud still counts as first. Quiet and later just counts as waiting again, and we already did that year.
