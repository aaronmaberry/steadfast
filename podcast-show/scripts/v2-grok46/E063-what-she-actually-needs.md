## SEGMENT 1 · The kitchen alone

WES: Pull up a chair. Let's take the next few minutes on a Thursday night I handled like my father. She was standing at the counter. The friend had said, "We'll know more in a few weeks." A scan in about two weeks. I said, "I'll let you be," and I sat on the porch two hours. She went to bed. I thought I had been kind.

JONATHON: Kind to yourself. You got quiet and a ballgame in your head. She got a kitchen and a clock. Wait, back up. Did you ask her what she wanted, or did you guess the barn?

WES: [beat] I guessed the barn. Morning she said, "I sat in that kitchen by myself last night." Twenty-seven years of door-shutting. When I'm sick I want a dark room, a ballgame, the door shut. When she's sick she wants chicken and rice from the little place by the shop, the television on low, and somebody on the end of the bed talking about nothing. The neighbors. The dog down the road.

JONATHON: You have been giving her your medicine. That's not love. That's projection with a polite face. How long have you known those two lists and still handed her yours?

WES: My dad went to the barn when my mother was sick. I inherited the barn. I called it giving her space. It was me hiding in a habit I never asked her about.

[BOARD: Your medicine is not her meal.]

JONATHON: I gave Dani a pep talk Thursday in the kitchen. I clapped once. She said, "Please stop. Now I've got two things to be scared of. Not getting in, and letting you down." Can I tell you the ugly part? I clapped because I was scared, not because she needed a coach.

WES: That's the tell. Your medicine wearing a smile.

## SEGMENT 2 · The living room

WES: Saturday she made a casserole for the friend. I said, "What can I carry?" She said, "Drive me over there. I don't want to walk in by myself." An hour in the living room. The friend's husband stood in the kitchen doorway the whole hour. On the way home she held my hand in the truck. She has not done that in a while.

JONATHON: The doorway man is me. I hover in the wrong room and call it support. She wanted you in the living room, not the casserole speech. Did you want to carry dishes and leave?

WES: I would have carried dishes and left. That's the help that would have worked on me. She needed a driver and a body in a chair. I've got to keep asking, because I will drift back to the porch.

JONATHON: Saturday morning she said, "Take the kids out of the house. Three hours. I want to sing in my own living room once before Thursday." Batting cages. Breakfast tacos. The park. From the driveway with the windows down I heard the last eight bars. She stopped when she heard the car. I still have not looked up the song.

WES: So you gave her the room she asked for, and you almost stole the last eight bars by coming home early. Did you walk in?

JONATHON: I wanted to walk in like a proud husband. I sat on the bumper instead.

WES: [laughs] The bumper. That's the chair this week.

JONATHON: I wanted to clap. I sat in the driveway and let the engine tick. That's the closest I've come to believing her answer.

## SEGMENT 3 · Curtains and a rabbit

WES: Your college coach grabbed your jersey and yelled from six inches before every start. That worked on you. It does not work on Dani. How long did it take you to retire the jersey?

JONATHON: I wore a sleep watch four days. Forty-one. I put it in the drawer. What she wanted was blackout curtains. She asked about three years ago. I put them up Monday. Twenty minutes. She slept till two. She texted one word. "Dark."

WES: Three years of a score, and the need was a piece of fabric.

JONATHON: Ruby shoved her rabbit at Theo when he was crying. He threw it. He wanted his other sock. She gave him the comfort that would have worked on her. I train every rep at work to do it their way, and I've never done it with Dani. Does that make me a hypocrite in my own kitchen?

WES: It makes you a man who finally saw the sock.

[BOARD: A sock is not a rabbit.]

WES: That's the picture. We hand them the rabbit because the rabbit worked on us. They wanted the sock. Then we get offended they didn't thank us for the rabbit.

JONATHON: The audition is Thursday. She said, "Don't text me. Not good luck, not nothing. Have supper made, and the kids fed, and don't ask me how it went until I tell you." That's a sock. I wanted to send a heart. That's a rabbit. Are you going to tell me a heart is still a text?

WES: A heart is a text. She said no text. Believe her.

## SEGMENT 4 · The Word

WES: Under ten. I want the need verse where we can see it. First Corinthians ten, verse twenty-four. You want to read it?

JONATHON: "No one should seek their own good, but the good of others."

[BOARD: Philippians 2:4 (NIV) · "not looking to your own interests but each of you to the interests of the others."]

WES: Paul is in a chapter about food and freedom and whether you make your brother stumble. The line is bigger than meat. Seek her good, not the good that would have soothed you. The porch soothed me. The kitchen needed a body.

JONATHON: "The good of others" isn't a guess. It's a question. I guessed a pep talk. She needed three hours and a dark room. My guess was about my nerves. Is this Paul on food rights, or Paul on sitting in the kitchen when she asked?

WES: Both. Context in two sentences. Corinth was full of men who could defend their rights. Paul tells them to lay the rights down for somebody else's good. A diagnosis isn't the time to defend your barn habit.

[BOARD: Ephesians 4:32 (NIV) · "Be kind and compassionate to one another, forgiving each other, just as in Christ God forgave you."]

JONATHON: Kind isn't a vibe. Kind is curtains. Kind is spaghetti on Thursday. Kind isn't a clap in a kitchen.

## SEGMENT 5 · Pushback

WES: Halfway. I want you to argue with me about her need. A man says, she won't tell me what she needs. Go ahead and push.

JONATHON: Ask anyway. Then believe the first answer. If she says quiet, don't come sit on the end of the bed "just for a minute." If she says company, don't go to the porch and call it respect. I don't totally buy that one ask is enough. Fine is sometimes a test. What do you do with fine?

WES: Ask a smaller one. "Do you want company or quiet?" Then do the one she picks. A man says, I checked once, she said she was fine, and I clocked out.

JONATHON: I've asked and then done the other because I thought I knew. That's fair, but— a man says he's not a mind reader, and I want to shake his hand and then take the candle away.

WES: Good. Stop pretending you're. Ask. Repeat it back. Do it. That's the whole tool. I thought mind reading was romance. It was laziness with a candle.

JONATHON: Last few minutes on the ask, while the night is still calm. No text Thursday. Supper made. Kids in pajamas. No asking how it went until she tells me. I will sit on my hands if I've got to. Can you live with not knowing how it went until she says?

WES: I will ask, "Do you want company or quiet?" and I will believe the answer. I will drive her to her friend's again Saturday. I will ask my mother at Tuesday PT whether she wanted my dad in the house or the barn.

## SEGMENT 6 · The challenge

WES: This week's challenge. Ask her, "Do you want company or quiet?" Believe the answer. Do it once this week when it costs you the thing that would have worked on you.

JONATHON: If you're dating, ask what would actually help, then don't upgrade it. If you're single, ask a friend what he needs and don't give him your medicine. If you're divorced, ask your kids' mom what would help a handoff, then do only that. What are you picking, because if you pick the barn and the bed you'll do the barn?

WES: The question. Out loud. Then Saturday's drive. Look at the calm night you keep wasting. The rabbit is probably already in your hand. Put it down. Find the sock.

JONATHON: And if you fail and give the pep talk, stop when she says please stop. Don't finish the speech. I finished speeches for years after she had already left the room.

WES: I sat on a porch while she sat in a kitchen. I can't get that Thursday back. I can get Saturday. I can get the question. I can get the drive.

JONATHON: I clapped in a kitchen. I can't get that back. I can get Thursday night with spaghetti and no phone and no heart on a screen.

## SEGMENT 7 · Mother and the barn

WES: I asked my mother at Tuesday PT, after the leg presses. Wait— I haven't asked yet. That's next Tuesday. I'm telling you the question so I don't get to skip it. "When you were sick, did you want Dad in the house or in the barn?" I thought I knew. I thought she would say the house, and then I could feel sad about him.

JONATHON: And if she says the barn, your whole inheritance changes. That's why you waited so long to ask. Are you actually going to ask, or are you going to tidy it into a theory on the drive home?

WES: If she says the barn, then my dad wasn't hiding. He was obeying a woman I never interviewed. Don't hold me to a speech about men. Hold me to asking her.

JONATHON: [laughs] Fine. Ask her. Then ask your wife. Don't let your mother answer for the woman in your kitchen.

WES: "Ask her, not me. I'm not her." I can already hear that. I'll still ask, because I've been using my dad as a map for a country he may not have lived in.

JONATHON: Ask. Write it down. Don't turn it into a theory about men. Theories are how we avoid the living room.

## SEGMENT 8 · The scan week

WES: The friend's scan is coming. My wife asked me to drive so she can sit in the back. I will go in. I will sit. I won't make jokes to the nurses. I made a joke to a man at a parking gate last year and called it lightening the mood. It was me being unable to sit in a hard room.

JONATHON: When Dani talks about a loss on her unit I crack a joke. She doesn't need a joke. She needs me on the kitchen floor where she can see me. I learned that after I asked three questions in a row and she said please stop. No, hold on. You're going to make a joke in that waiting room. I know you. What's the fence?

WES: Sit. Nod. No jokes. So the good of others includes your mouth. Sometimes the good is silence. Sometimes it's nothing-talk on the end of a bed. You don't get to pick from a menu in your head. You ask.

JONATHON: Company or quiet. I'm going to write those four words on a card in the truck. If I feel a clap coming I will look at the card.

WES: Write it. Then do Saturday. Drive her. Sit down. Hold her hand if she reaches. Don't reach first if she asked for quiet.

JONATHON: And don't narrate the hand later as proof you're getting better. That's another letter.

## SEGMENT 9 · Thursday night

WES: Stay on his Thursday. No text. Supper. Pajamas. No asking. That will feel like neglect if your medicine is encouragement. It isn't neglect. It's obedience to a woman who knows her own nerves.

JONATHON: I already typed "Thinking about you" at lunch in my head. I will text you instead. "Is a heart a text." You can say K. I need a man who will tell me K. Will you actually say K, or will you write a paragraph?

WES: K. A heart is a text. She said no text. Believe her. Ruby can set a glass of water with ice. That's enough of a welcome. You stay away from the window when the car sits in the driveway.

JONATHON: She sits in driveways to finish things. I've gone to windows my whole life. That's the barn in reverse. Hovering. I'll stay in the kitchen and stir a pot.

WES: Stir the pot. Feed the kids. Go to bed when she's ready to talk, or don't, if she isn't. The good of others isn't a speech at ten-forty.

JONATHON: If she says she sang it the way she sings it in the car, I won't upgrade that sentence. I will put my hand on her back. That's the sock.

## SEGMENT 10 · Men who fix feelings

JONATHON: Single men, this is still yours. When a friend is in it, ask what would help, then do only that. Don't build a ministry on his Tuesday.

JONATHON: Older men, if you've been giving the rabbit for forty years, you can still ask tonight. She may be tired of rabbits. She may still tell you the sock if you look like you won't argue.

## SEGMENT 11 · The doorway hour

WES: I keep seeing the friend's husband in the kitchen doorway. An hour. He didn't sit. He didn't leave. He occupied the frame like a man who wanted to help and didn't know the chair was the help.

JONATHON: I've been that frame. At births. At holidays. At my own table. Present in outline. Missing in the room. A doorway isn't company. A doorway is a barn you can see from the couch. Can you sit without improving the dog down the road into a lesson?

WES: I will sit. In the living room. In the waiting room. On the end of the bed if she says so. I will talk about the dog down the road if that's the medicine. I won't improve the conversation into meaning.

JONATHON: Nothing-talk is harder for me than a pep talk. Pep talks make me feel useful. Nothing-talk makes me feel small. Small is probably the good of others.

WES: I've got one more minute. Ask her while the night is still calm. Ask the question this week when it costs you the porch. Then stay in the answer even if your chest wants the barn.

JONATHON: I'll stay in the kitchen Thursday. No window. No heart. Spaghetti. A glass with ice. If I fail, I'll come back and put the phone in the other room, not write her a letter about the failure.

## SEGMENT 12 · Chicken and rice

WES: I want the little place by the shop on the table, because I've mocked that order in my head. Chicken and rice. Television low. Nothing-talk. I thought a grown woman should want something more spiritual when she hurts. That thought is pride. Her body asked for a bowl and a voice about a dog.

JONATHON: I've mocked Dani's oven. When a baby doesn't make it she comes home and scrubs the oven until it shines. I used to ask three questions. She said please stop. Now I put coffee on the counter and I sit on the kitchen floor where she can see me. That's chicken and rice in another font. Hold on. Are you going to upgrade the bowl to a steak because a bowl looks too small?

WES: I will buy the bowl this week if she wants it. I won't upgrade it to a steak. I won't add a devotion. If she wants the dog down the road, we will talk about the dog down the road.

JONATHON: Nothing-talk isn't empty. It's how some women come back into the room. I kept trying to make the room mean something. She needed the room to be a room.

WES: Look at the calm night you keep wasting. What have you been upgrading because the real ask looked too small to be love?

JONATHON: I've been upgrading Tuesdays into a program. She asked for okay out loud. I'm trying to retire the program and keep the sentence.

## SEGMENT 13 · What we're actually doing

WES: Here's what I'm actually doing. Company or quiet, asked out loud, believed. A drive on Saturday. A question for my mother at PT. No jokes to nurses. A chair, not a doorway.

JONATHON: Here's what I'm actually doing. No text Thursday. Supper. Pajamas. A glass with ice. Hand on her back if she wants a hand. No song lookup. No clap. Will you text me K if I start typing a heart?

JONATHON: And if you're the man, let a brother ask you. I needed you to say K when I wanted to send a heart. That's a kind of weight I can't pick up alone.

WES: A minute. Ask tonight, on a calm night, before the scan. Ask tonight. Not after the next crisis. Tonight, on a calm night, so the question has a practice rep before the scan.

JONATHON: I'll ask Dani a practice one that isn't about the audition. Company or quiet after a night shift. Then I'll do the one she picks, even if I want the other.
