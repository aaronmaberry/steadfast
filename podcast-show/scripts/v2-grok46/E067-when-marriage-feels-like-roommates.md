JONATHON: And I'll tell you the forty-second handoff that has become our weekday marriage.

## SEGMENT 1 · Who is this

WES: Pull up a chair. Let's take the next few minutes on a fridge, because that's where this started, and it didn't look spiritual.

JONATHON: Dani's unit is short. Two nurses out, one on maternity leave. She's working a fourth night every week through Labor Day. Weekday handoff in the driveway at seven-fifteen, windows down, about forty seconds. She sleeps till two. She leaves at six-thirty. Wednesday the whiteboard said Ruby's shoes, Theo's dentist, love you. I wrote, "Who is this."

WES: Wait, back up. You wrote that as a joke. Was it a joke?

JONATHON: [beat] No. It wasn't. I laughed so I wouldn't have to feel it.

WES: Yeah. That's how a fridge tells on you. Kids read the marker before they read our mouths. Ruby already knew, and she asked the question anyway, which is the report card. Did you answer her, or did you laugh again?

JONATHON: I laughed. It was a cry in marker. We've got become a company that loves each other in theory and manages children in practice. Ruby heard the theory getting thin.

[BOARD: A fridge can tell the truth a porch will not.]

WES: Kids read whiteboards. They read pats on the shoulder. They read whether anyone laughs at a dog.

---

## SEGMENT 2 · Bored with you

WES: Your back step. Her night off. What did she actually say once the water bill died?

JONATHON: Kids down by eight-thirty. I started with the water bill. Five minutes of logistics, then quiet. She said, "I miss being bored with you." Saturdays before kids we went to the hardware store for an hour and drove past houses we couldn't afford. Now every minute we're together has a job.

WES: That's the roommate sentence. I heard a version about seven years ago after a ballgame. My wife asked, "When's the last time we talked about anything that wasn't on the calendar?" I didn't have an answer. Do you?

JONATHON: I noticed she still pulls her feet up on a step. She still talks to the neighbor's dog first. She has tired nurse hands. Those aren't logistics. Those are a woman I used to study.

WES: Study is the job we retired when the calendar got loud.

---

## SEGMENT 3 · The ride

WES: Every Saturday I said, "Ride with me," out the farm road, about twenty minutes. She has ridden most Saturdays since, seven years. When she can't come, she says she's sorry. I honestly can't say whether the ride or the quieter house ended our relay year. I know we still have a road.

JONATHON: I don't have a farm road. I've got a back step. Same job. A place with no whiteboard. Can I tell you the sales version, because I only had one lens this week?

WES: Go.

JONATHON: I had been treating Dani like an account going transactional. In sales that's the week before you lose it. You still get orders. You have already lost the room.

[BOARD: Orders can keep coming after the room is gone.]

WES: What I like about her, if you need a list because you have forgotten you have one: she reads the last page first. She talks to dogs before people. She knows every hymn by number. She backs a trailer better than I do. My granddad's back forty went to mesquite in about six years after he sold the cows. My older brother bought it from the estate and spent three years clearing it. That's marriage after a roommate season. You can clear it. It takes years, not a date night coupon.

JONATHON: I want a coupon. That's the tell.

---

## SEGMENT 4 · The Word

WES: I've got a little under ten minutes, and I want the Bible open. Ecclesiastes nine, verse nine. You want to read it?

JONATHON: "Enjoy life with your wife, whom you love, all the days of this meaningless life that God has given you under the sun—all your meaningless days. For this is your lot in life and in your toilsome labor under the sun."

[BOARD: Ephesians 4:32 (NIV) · "Be kind and compassionate to one another, forgiving each other, just as in Christ God forgave you."]

WES: Enjoy is a command, not a mood. Under the sun means the Labor Day stretch, the fourth night, the dentist at three-fifteen. The lot includes toilsome labor. You don't wait for a vacation to enjoy her. You enjoy her in the toil.

JONATHON: I've been waiting for the unit to be fully staffed to be a husband. That's a lobby. The verse does not give me a staffing clause. So which is it, a verse about a vacation, or a verse about a fourth night?

WES: Both. Context in two sentences. The preacher has looked at work and death and said, while you're here, enjoy the wife you love. That's not thin romance. That's rebellion against a whiteboard that thinks it owns you.

---

## SEGMENT 5 · Pushback

WES: We're about halfway, and I want the push. A man says, a date night will fix this. Go ahead and push.

JONATHON: A date night that's another event on the calendar won't. We tried every-other-Saturday like client dinners after Ruby was born. Dani's first line was the rash on Ruby's arm. The calendar came with us in the car. I don't totally buy that a ride fixes a fridge. Does yours?

WES: A man says, we don't have fifteen minutes.

JONATHON: You had forty seconds in a driveway. You have a whiteboard. You have a step. The minutes are there. They're wearing jobs. Take one job off one minute.

WES: A man says, she doesn't want to ride.

JONATHON: Then find her step. Don't make the ride the test. Some women talk in a car. Some talk in the dark. Some talk while they dry a pan. I've been asking in the wrong room and then saying she never talks. That's fair, but— what are you actually doing Saturday?

WES: Last few minutes on what we're actually doing. Saturday on the ride I will ask, "What are you looking forward to? This year." I won't fill the quiet.

JONATHON: I'm writing one non-logistics line on the whiteboard every day, and the back step again on her next night off. If I write a joke, it still has to be about her, not about the dentist.

---

## SEGMENT 6 · The challenge

WES: This week's challenge. Take one minute that has a job and give it no job. A ride. A step. A couch. Ask one question that isn't on the calendar. Write what she says.

JONATHON: If you're dating, notice whether every hour has a plan. If you're single, notice whether every friendship has a task. If you're divorced, one non-logistics sentence with your kids' mom is still a kind of clearing. What are you picking, ride or fridge, because if you pick three you'll do the dentist?

WES: Ride. One question. Look around your house this week. The roommate season is usually a fridge. Read the fridge. Then write one line that could not go in a manager's meeting.

JONATHON: And if a child asks if you like each other, don't lie and don't give a speech. Like each other somewhere she can see. A step. A laugh at a dog. A pat that isn't a handoff.

---

## SEGMENT 7 · Mesquite

WES: Stay on the back forty. Six years of mesquite after the cows left. Three years of clearing. Nobody got a weekend miracle. If your house has gone to brush, don't buy a coupon. Buy a saw and a Saturday.

JONATHON: I want a coupon. A restaurant. A sitter. Then I want the old marriage to be waiting at the table. The old marriage is under brush. I've got to cut. No, hold on. If I try to clear forty acres on a Friday night I will quit and blame her. What's the small cut?

WES: Cut small. A ride. A line. A step. Don't ask the duck to be a paragraph. Receive the duck. That's enjoy.

JONATHON: I'll cut a line a day. If she draws a duck, that's a tree down. Receive the duck. That's harder for me than a speech about intimacy. A duck is small. Small is how brush actually moves.

---

## SEGMENT 8 · Fourth night

WES: A fourth night is a season, not a verdict. Seasons end. Roommate can become a habit that outlives the season. That's the danger. The unit will staff. The whiteboard may stay in charge if you let it.

JONATHON: I'm naming the season out loud so I don't marry it. Through Labor Day. Then we revisit. If I don't name an end I will still be a manager in October. Are you going to let me store enjoy for September?

WES: Name the end. Then enjoy her before the end. The verse does not say enjoy her after the unit is staffed.

JONATHON: I've been storing enjoy for September. That's a lobby. I'm spending a little now on a step.

WES: Spend it now. A laugh at a dog is spend. A line that isn't shoes is spend.

JONATHON: I'll spend. If I fail and write a dentist line, I'll write a second line about her feet on the step. Two lines. One of them has to be a wife.

---

## SEGMENT 9 · Sales lens

WES: You used a sales word. Transactional. I'm allowing it this once because it named a real death. Don't make a career out of talking about your marriage like a territory.

JONATHON: Fair. It's the only lens I had for a week. An account going transactional still sends orders. That's what fooled me. The orders were baths and dentists. The room was gone. How do you win a room without turning it into a meeting?

WES: Then win the room, not the order. A ride is a room. A step is a room. A forty-second driveway is an order. You need both. You have been living on orders. No agenda. One question. Then quiet. I will practice that on the farm road.

JONATHON: I'll practice it on the step. If I start a water bill I will stop and say I miss being bored with you too.

---

## SEGMENT 10 · What I like

WES: Men forget they like their wives. Not love as a duty. Like. I like that she reads the last page first. I've mocked that. I'm going to say it on the ride without a joke that undercuts it.

JONATHON: I like her tired hands. I've treated them as a problem to solve with lotion and a speech. I can like them. Liking is enjoy. Can you sit in a like without turning it into a compliment as payment?

WES: Say one like this week out loud. Not a compliment as payment. A like. Then shut up.

JONATHON: I'll say the feet on the step. I won't add a lesson. If I add a lesson I've written a manager note.

WES: I've got one more minute. One minute with no job. One question. One line. Clear a little brush. That's the lot under the sun this week.

JONATHON: And if Ruby asks again, I want to have a laugh she can point to, not a speech about how much we love each other in theory.

---

## SEGMENT 11 · Pancake later

WES: Theo is five now. You have talked about a pancake morning when he turned five. Don't let the roommate season steal that too. A pancake is a room. Put it on a Saturday that isn't a job. Have you written it, or have you slid it?

JONATHON: I've been sliding it. The unit is short. The whiteboard is loud. If I slide a pancake I've told him the company comes first. I'll pick a Saturday in September and I'll write pancake where a dentist would go.

WES: Write it. Then keep it. If you move it three times you have taught the fridge who wins.

JONATHON: I'll keep it. Dani can sleep. I can burn a pancake and eat that one myself. He can have the good one. That's a first pick I understand now.

WES: Good. A five-year-old can read a fridge too. Give him a word that isn't shoes.

JONATHON: Pancake. I'll write it in marker. If I write it I've got to heat a pan. That's enjoy with butter on it.

## SEGMENT 12 · The forty seconds

WES: I want the driveway handoff named as a marriage, because you have been calling it logistics. Forty seconds is a marriage if that's what you have. Roll the window down. Look at her. Say one thing that isn't a time. Then let her sleep. I've given my wife a weather report in a doorway and called it connection. What are you going to say that isn't a time?

JONATHON: I'll say one thing that isn't a time. I like your laugh. I want you to sleep. I'm glad you're my person. If I say dentist I will say the other line too. Two lines. One of them has to be a wife.

---

## SEGMENT 13 · Hardware store

WES: Before kids you had an hour that produced nothing. That's enjoy. I've mocked hours that produce nothing. I called them wasted. Ecclesiastes would call them the lot. I was the whiteboard wearing boots.

JONATHON: I want an hour that produces nothing this month. Not a project store run. A walk past houses we still can't afford. If we talk about money I will stop and say I miss this. That's the room. Will you let me have a wasted hour, or are you going to ask me for a receipt?

WES: Have the hour. Receipts are how managers stay managers.

## SEGMENT 14 · Hymns by number

WES: I like that she knows hymns by number. I've used that as a joke at church. I'm going to say it on the ride as a like, no joke under it. Liking is how I obey enjoy when my mood is a calendar.

JONATHON: I like that she talks to a dog first. I've rushed her past the dog. I'll let the dog have the first minute. If I rush the dog I've told her the whiteboard is hungrier than she's. [laughs] Are you actually going to skip the joke this time?

WES: I'm going to skip the joke. That's the death this week.

## SEGMENT 15 · Relay year

WES: Seven years ago the house was a relay. Daughter a senior. Boys in sports. After a game she asked when we last talked about anything that wasn't on the calendar. I didn't have an answer. The ride started because I needed an answer more than I needed a faster Saturday.

JONATHON: I don't have an answer for Ruby yet. That's why the line on the fridge matters. If she can point to a line that isn't shoes, she has an answer without a speech. Can I ask you something hard?

WES: Go.

JONATHON: Are you still riding to get an answer, or are you riding because you like her?

WES: Both. If I only ride to get an answer I've made the road a meeting.

---

## SEGMENT 16 · Clearing

WES: My brother cleared mesquite three years. He didn't post about it. That's the work. If you need a post you're still in the roommate office. Clear a little. Don't announce the acreage.

JONATHON: I won't announce the acreage. I'll write a line. I'll sit a step. I'll keep a pancake. If I announce it Ruby will think we're performing like each other. I want her to trip over it, not clap for it.

## SEGMENT 17 · What I'm actually doing

WES: Saturday I ask what she's looking forward to this year. I will let a mile of quiet happen. I won't mention a printout I've been carrying. Hearing is still the job when the house feels like an office.

JONATHON: I'll write a line every day through Labor Day. I'll take the step on her night off. I'll put pancake on a Saturday in September. If I fail Wednesday I will write two lines Thursday, and one of them will be about her feet. Hold on. Are you going to slide the printout if she says finish something?

WES: I won't slide it. Hearing is the job.

## SEGMENT 18 · Wes on the calendar

WES: I've been the manager in my own house. I ran people. I ran a clock. I ran a cooler to a beach she didn't ask for. Roommate isn't a young man's disease. It's what happens when a calendar gets a louder voice than a woman.

JONATHON: I'm thirty-seven and I already have the disease. The fourth night didn't invent it. It revealed it. That's a mercy if I cut brush now.

---

## SEGMENT 19 · Quiet mile

WES: A mile of quiet will make me want to teach. I won't teach. I will watch the fence line and let her find a sentence. If she says she wants to finish something I won't pull a printout. I will say tell me more.

JONATHON: Tell me more is the only upgrade I want. I've upgraded her sentences into projects for years. I'm tired of being a project manager at my own step. That's fair, but— HVAC sales will still have a fourth-quarter number in September. If I wait for a quiet month I'll still be waiting when Theo has a license. Do I put the step on a night that already has a shift?

WES: Put the step on a night that already has a shift. Or you never will.

---

## SEGMENT 20 · Labor Day stretch

WES: Through Labor Day is a name. Names help. After Labor Day we ask again whether the fourth night is still required. If I don't put a date on a season I will still be eating standing up in November and calling it normal.

JONATHON: I'll put the date on the whiteboard. Season ends. Then we date the pancake. Then we see whether the whiteboard will give the house back.

## SEGMENT 21 · What I want you to do

WES: Look around your house this week. Read the fridge like a child would. Then write one line a child would know meant like. I've got about a minute and I want it on you. One minute with no job. Tonight if you can. Tomorrow if you must.

JONATHON: Tonight if I can. The step if she's home. The driveway window if she's not. One line either way. I won't wait for a fully staffed unit to become a man who likes his wife out loud.

WES: Pick the minute. Take the job off it. Ask the question. Write what she says. Then don't turn it into a meeting before Monday. I've wasted good answers by turning them into meetings. I'm not doing that on the ride.
