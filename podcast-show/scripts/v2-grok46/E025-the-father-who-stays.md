## SEGMENT 1 · The like

JONATHON: Easter lunch at our house. My mom is fifty-nine. She brought the spiral ham plus deviled eggs nobody asked for, and she asked at the door, "Is this okay?" Dani hugged her on the porch before she could put the pan down. Ruby and Theo hunted eggs in the backyard. Theo found eleven, all in the same bush, because Ruby hid them there for him.

WES: Same bush. Ruby hiding eggs for him. Then you told your mom about the friend request, in the kitchen?

JONATHON: In the kitchen I told my mom about the friend request. She said, "He sent me one too. Two years ago. I said yes." Then, "He posts a lot of fish." Then, "I'm not going to tell you what to do. He's your father." I wanted her to tell me. She wouldn't. That's a gift I didn't want and still needed.

WES: Then 9:40.

JONATHON: Kids down. Dani left the lamp on. I sat in the truck in the driveway so I wouldn't perform for a kitchen. I accepted. No message. Twelve minutes later he liked the photo Dani had posted that afternoon. Ruby and Theo in Easter clothes in the bluebonnets by the mailbox.

WES: He liked the photo. A thumb you haven't heard from in a real sentence.

JONATHON: I sat there and I watched a thumb I haven't heard from in a real sentence. I didn't write. I went inside. Dani said, "Well?" I said, "Yes. He liked the picture." She said, "Okay." We didn't make it a meeting. I slept anyway. I woke up wanting a paragraph from him. I didn't get one. I still have to tell Ruby this week. I know the sentence. I haven't said it. Friday I will tell you if I did.

WES: That's a walk and a leftover hide. Both can be true. You walked at 9:40. The girl is still waiting in the car. Don't let a like become the ending. A like is a fish. A girl is a person. Did you want him to write first so you wouldn't have to tell Ruby?

## SEGMENT 2 · Glad you're mine

WES: Mine was the truck, Easter afternoon. My youngest is sixteen. He said, "Am I almost done with the Saturdays?" I said four more. Then I said, "I want to tell you something that's got nothing to do with the truck. I'm glad you're mine. That's all. Not because of anything."

JONATHON: "I'm glad you're mine." No because. What'd he do with a sentence that had no score?

WES: He said, "Okay." A long pause. "Can I drive home?" I let him. I held the handle above the door the whole way. I didn't add a because. I've added because to every kind word for fifty years. I wanted one without a score.

JONATHON: That's the well done that's not a prize. I need that next to a like, because I will turn a like into a score. Did he like it enough. Did I pick right. Your sentence has no points. I tried it at bath. I told Theo, "I'm glad you're mine." He said, "I know." Four years old and he already knew. I almost added because you're funny, because you throw over the fence, because. I stopped. I want to be the kind of man who can say it without a receipt.

WES: My dad said he was proud of me when I was forty-three, in a hospital bed. I'm not retelling that whole night. I'm saying I don't want my boy to wait until a handle is the only thing I can hold. I said it in a truck that smelled like grass. That's enough setting. I almost ruined it by asking if he heard me. He said okay. Okay is enough.

JONATHON: Okay is enough. If you'd asked again you would have been collecting. Yeah, and—you've collected enough. You held the handle and you let him drive.

WES: I've collected enough. I held the handle and I let him drive and I didn't add a lecture about the Saturdays. Four more. He knows. He does not need a speech on Easter afternoon. He needed a sentence without a score. I finally had one. I want another one for my wife tonight, and I want to leave it alone after I say it. Leaving it alone is the stay. Talking it to death is the old me.

JONATHON: Theo at church, after the reading about the tomb, said, "Because he came back?" I said yes. He nodded like that settled the universe. I want a faith that nods. I also want a father who stays, and I can't make my dad into that with a button. I can stay. That's the only half of this I own.

[BOARD: Leave the receipt off the kind words.]

WES: Whose well done do you actually work for? I still hear my dad. You have said yours isn't your dad.

JONATHON: Mine is a first sales manager in Beaumont. Same tie every Monday. "Not bad, kid." He's retired in Galveston. I still write emails to my VP in that man's voice. Not bad, kid, is a thin well done, and it ran me for years. I want God's well done more than a tie. I don't have it in my bones yet. I've got a verse, and I've got a bath, and I've got a like I must not worship.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: We're about halfway, and I want the Bible open, because a voice from heaven already said the sentence men wait decades to hear. Matthew three, verse seventeen.

JONATHON: I've wanted that voice from a man who posts fish. I won't get it that way. I might get a like. I might get a call in April. I might get nothing. The verse isn't about my dad. So which is it? Work for a thumb, or work for a voice that already spoke?

WES: The voice. Not the thumb. "This is my Son, whom I love; with him I am well pleased." Before Jesus had preached a sermon. Before a cross. Before a resume. Loved. Pleased. A Father who stays, and who speaks, and who does not wait for a trophy. If you only work for a hospital bed or a tie or a like, you will be hungry on a Monday. Work for this voice. Then say a smaller version in a truck.

JONATHON: I've worked for a tie. I know the hunger on a Monday. I still write emails like I'm trying to hear not bad, kid.

WES: I've worked for a hospital bed most of my life without admitting it. I waited until a man could not sit up to hear four words, and then I talked about those four words as if talking were the same as living them, and I still catch myself earning them again in a kitchen. Earning is a hole. This verse fills it if I will let it. I don't let it most Tuesdays. I let a title, or a porch, or a brother call, tell me if I'm pleased enough. That's a thin god. I'm tired of thin gods. I want this voice in the truck more than I want a handle to hold.

[BOARD: Romans 5:8 (NIV) · "But God demonstrates his own love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us."]

JONATHON: Well pleased before the work. I've built a life on pleased after the work. Cancun. Leaderboard. A kneel in the dirt I hoped the bleachers saw. This verse ruins my pay system. Good. Ruin it.

WES: Ruin it. Then go say glad you're mine to someone who already knows and still needs to hear it. Then, if you owe a girl a sentence about a grandfather in Louisiana, say it this week. Don't wait for a better like. I keep waiting for a better moment the way I waited for a better title. The moment is a Tuesday with leftover ham in the fridge and a boy who already said okay. Take the moment. If you wait for a clean feeling you will get another Easter and another like and another year of far away. I've enough years of far away on my side of the ledger. I don't want more on his.

[BOARD: Luke 15:20 (NIV) · "So he got up and went to his father. But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion for him; he ran to his son, threw his arms around him and kissed him."]

## SEGMENT 4 · Pushback

WES: Keep pushing. Go ahead.

JONATHON: Yeah, here's my problem. A like isn't the same as a father who stays. If I sit here glowing about a button, a man whose dad is gone or cruel will think we just sold him a fairy tale. Paint.

WES: Paint. A like is small. God isn't small. The chair can still be empty and the tomb can still be empty. Both true. We're not baptizing a profile picture. We're saying you can stay even if he does not. You can speak even if he likes and does not write. You can be well pleased with a boy who has not earned a truck yet.

JONATHON: I don't totally buy a clean sentence yet. "I'm glad you're mine" can become a new plaque. I can say it and still miss bath. I can say it and still hand Ruby to Dani. Is that staying, or is that a commercial?

WES: Then say it and stay. The stay is the pay. The sentence without the stay is a commercial. I said it and I let him drive. That's a stay I can measure in miles and a white knuckle on a handle.

JONATHON: That's fair, but— I still have October with Dani. We started. We haven't finished. Easter didn't finish it. A ham didn't finish it. I asked her last night for a night this week. She said Wednesday. I put it on the fridge. If I erase it I will have used Easter as weather again. Will you actually pick up at seven, or is leftover ham going to finish your night?

WES: Seven o'clock. Ham doesn't get the night. Keep Wednesday. I call my older brother tonight at seven. We will tell you Friday. I'm not using a like or a tomb as a reason to skip a barn date I already set. I've used holy days as cover before. I left Thanksgiving early and called it traffic.

JONATHON: You left Thanksgiving early and called it traffic. Don't leave Easter in a glow and call it peace. That's a cease-fire with a ham on it.

WES: I can leave Easter in a glow and call it peace. It would be a cease-fire again. I'm done selling myself cease-fires. Seven o'clock. I will sit in the truck so I don't perform for a kitchen. I will say the house and the will. I won't say I'm a better brother now because I remembered a verse. Verses don't make me better. They make me unable to hide with a straight face. I want that more than I want to look like a man who had a nice Easter.

JONATHON: She will ask if you liked it. I will tell her you ate it and you called him. That's the report she actually wants. She does not listen to us. She listens for whether a man followed through.

WES: A woman I've never met sent food across a city because her son said my name like I had been useful. That could puff me up. I won't let it. Useful is a cousin of the title.

JONATHON: Useful is a cousin of the title. Don't let the ham finish the thing you owe your brother. Two different tables. Both yours.

WES: I will say thank you through you, and I will pick up the phone at seven, and I will remember that being useful to a younger man does not finish the thing I owe the older one. Two different tables. Both mine. I've sat at the easy one and skipped the hard one most of my adult life. Not tonight. Tonight is the hard one, and the ham can wait until I've told you Friday whether I stayed on the line.

JONATHON: I tell Ruby this week. I've got the sentence on a card. I don't have Dani all the way with me on the wording. We will fight that a little. I will still say it. I owe her more than far away.

WES: I will say glad you're mine again this week, to my wife, without a because. She has heard a lot of becauses. She has earned a clean one.
