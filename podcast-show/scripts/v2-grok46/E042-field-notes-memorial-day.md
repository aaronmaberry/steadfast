## SEGMENT 1 · Deputy and pancakes

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the living, then the dead, because I do not want this Friday to be only granite. I called from the truck. He said who died. I said nobody. I am calling about the dog. He said, Wes. I told you at Christmas. Twice. I had said that is a good name for a dog both times. Twenty-two minutes. He asked why I called. I said no reason. He said okay. That night a video. Deputy caught on the couch. Deputy says hi.

JONATHON: You finally asked. He had already told you. That is embarrassing and also exactly how men work. I called Austin from my office. Voicemail. I was calling about the pancakes. How did the pancakes go. He called back in forty minutes. Who calls about pancakes. He burned the first batch. Smoke alarm. His daughter, ten, took the spatula and made the second batch. His son, nine, quiet since March, ate six and asked for chocolate chips. So now I need chocolate chips. I said get the mini ones. They melt better.

WES: That is a brother Thursday. You did not fix a marriage. You talked about batter. I think the dog call makes Abilene easier on Saturday. I am not going to make it a scene. I am going to sit in a family room.

JONATHON: I missed my second name. Shane. I did not call him. That is a half. I can tell you about pancakes and still have a half. I do not want credit for one live wire while another one sits dark. I called Shane later in the week and I will tell that Tuesday if it belongs. Today I am leaving the half on the table.

[BOARD: Ask the dog's name twice.]


## SEGMENT 2 · Chris and a pencil

WES: We're about halfway. Tell the plate. I want the listener who thinks honor is a graphic to hear a name.

JONATHON: Shane has a gum-sized brass plate on the model's bridge for Quartermaster Third Class Christen Christensen. Chris. Helmsman. Killed when a German shell hit the bridge at Cherbourg, June twenty-fifth, nineteen forty-four. He was twenty-one. The only man killed in action in the Texas's service. Shane's grandfather was aboard that day, in a gun mount. Everybody on that ship got to come home but one. Shane says evening, Chris, every time he sits down to work. Ruby says it too.

WES: You left a tribute.

JONATHON: Wednesday. Ruby and I left a line on Chris's memorial page. Thank you for steering. I grew up fifteen minutes from the Texas at San Jacinto. Fourth-grade field trip. I remember the gift shop and a cannon pencil. I did not remember a twenty-one-year-old with a name. I had the souvenir. I did not have the man. That is most of my Memorial Days until this week.

WES: Most of mine too. We have granite on the courthouse lawn. First World War and the second on the front. Korea on the side. Vietnam on the back. I have never read the back. Monday I will, out loud, before the town is up. Phone flashlight if I have to. Names, not a feeling.

[BOARD: Mini chips melt better.]


## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: John fifteen, verse thirteen. A verse we put on graphics and then do not read a name.

JONATHON: "Greater love has no one than this: to lay down one’s life for one’s friends."

WES: Greater love is specific. It has a date. It has a ship. It has a twenty-one-year-old. It is not a mood you put on a shirt. If you want to honor men you never met, start with their names. Then keep a Thursday for the living so you are not only loyal to the dead.

JONATHON: I can love a story about Chris and still not call Shane. That is the tell. The dead are safer. They do not say who calls about pancakes. The living do. I want both. A plate and a phone call. If I only do the plate I am still hiding.

[BOARD: Joshua 4:21 (NIV) · "He said to the Israelites, “In the future when your descendants ask their parents, ‘What do these stones mean?’"]


## SEGMENT 4 · Reading the names

WES: Last few minutes. Tool is reading the names. Go to the stone in your town. Read the side you have never walked around. Out loud. If there is no stone, pick one name from a memorial page and say it at supper. Do not post a grave you were not asked to post. The crew we will meet later in the summer does not do pictures. Learn that early.

JONATHON: Ruby already says evening, Chris. That is her version of the tool. I will not turn it into a content plan. I will say the name with her and I will call the living man I skipped. Half is not the report I want to keep giving.

WES: The mechanic said something Wednesday while I brought coffee and asked nothing. I go out to Fort Sam Monday morning. Early. Not clearly an invitation. I will be at the gate of Fort Sam Houston National Cemetery with two coffees at six-thirty Monday. I will tell you Tuesday what I was allowed to see.

JONATHON: My oldest-son version is your oldest. He called a trade-school friend. The friend asked if he was dying. They talked forty minutes about trucks. The friend is getting married. He did not know. That is a Memorial Day week I can live with. The living, called about nothing, before the stone.

WES: Look around your house this week. A flag is fine. A name is better. A Thursday call is the test. Do not let a holiday do your brotherhood for you and then go quiet until November.

JONATHON: I will get the mini chips to a man who asked for them. That is not a strategy. That is breakfast. If I cannot do breakfast I should stop talking about brothers on a holiday.

[BOARD: Say evening to the missing.]

WES: I have walked past that granite for years the way I walk past a man in a truck. Front only. The back is where the later wars are. I do not want to be a front-only man. Monday I walk around.

WES: I keep wanting this to resolve in one conversation, and it will not. The assignment is the next honest sentence, said on time, without a speech attached. I have left too many good talks on a table because I wanted a better closing line than the one I had.

WES: I want this back in your house, not in ours. Pick the one door that is yours. Not your brother's. Not a man on a screen. Yours. Put a day on it before supper, even if the day is tomorrow and the sentence is clumsy.

WES: If you fail the first try, the second try still counts. I have to say that every time or I will use the miss as a way out and then tell a story about the miss like the story was the obedience.

JONATHON: I used the first miss as a way out for most of my twenties. I am trying not to do that at thirty-seven. It is harder than any capacitor, and it is also more ordinary than I want to admit at this table.

## SEGMENT · Sitting with it

WES: I have watched men collect stories like this and never change a Tuesday. Do not be that man this week. Change one Tuesday. Then tell one person you did, because a secret obedience turns back into a mood by Thursday.

JONATHON: I am the man who collects stories. I have a notes app full of them. The only ones that count are the ones that got a day and a time and a person who knows I said I would do it.

WES: I want the listener who thinks this does not apply because his house is quieter. Quiet is not settled. You already know that if you have sat through a polite holiday and called it peace.

JONATHON: My house can be loud and still be a cease-fire. Volume is not the test. The test is whether the true thing got said to the person who had to hear it, not to a mug at this table.

WES: I will check this on Friday, which is the only reason I trust myself. A public calendar is a kind of mercy for a man who likes his recliner and likes to call the recliner wisdom.

JONATHON: I will hold you to it, and I want somebody holding me. That is why I say the time out loud even when it makes me look late. Looking late is better than looking peaceful and being dug in.

WES: A steadfast man does the small thing while it is still small. That is the whole show, said again, because we forget it by Wednesday and start waiting to feel ready, and the ready never comes first.

JONATHON: I forget it by Tuesday afternoon. Ruby does not. She has a better memory for what I said I would do than any review I have ever run, and she does not care that I had a good reason.

WES: If this feels like too many stories, pick one. One house. One name. One time. That is enough weight for seven days, and it is more than most of us will actually carry if we get proud of the list.

JONATHON: I will pick one. If I pick three I will do none, and I already know that about myself from sales and from home. One is how a man like me gets free of his own notes app.

WES: I have a habit of saving the true sentence for a better room. There is no better room. The kitchen is the room. The truck is the room. The porch after the dishes is the room. Say it there.

JONATHON: I have rehearsed in better rooms my whole adult life. The rehearsal is how I stay safe. Dani can hear the difference between a rehearsal and a sentence. She stops me when I start handing people a door before they are in the room.

WES: Some of you will try to make this spiritual so you do not have to be specific. Prayer is good. A day and a name is still required. Do not baptize delay and call it discernment.

WES: The man who says he will start after the busy season is lying to himself the same way I did in my forties. There is always another season. Do the small thing inside the busy, or you will never do it.

JONATHON: HVAC sales does not hand you a quiet month. If I wait for one I will still be waiting when Theo has a license. I have to put the true thing on a Tuesday that already has a meeting.
