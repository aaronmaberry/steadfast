## SEGMENT 1 · The green lamp

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the lamp, because it is a small grown thing and I do not want to skip it on the way to a famous breakfast. You first. After church. San Antonio.

JONATHON: Mom drove in Saturday for the birthday weekend. Dani took the kids to the park. Mom walked the store like she was hunting a ghost of the old lamp and then picked a green glass banker's lamp. She said she has been reading at that table in bad light for a long time. She would like to see the page. Neither of us mentioned the old one. She said it is my birthday. I will buy my own lamp.

WES: A woman buying her own light is a kind of leaving you should bless. You used to be the capacitor guy. This time you let her hold the coupon and the cart.

JONATHON: She had a coupon since April. My sister told her. She texted a photo later. Lamp on, book, reading glasses, no words. It is my lock screen now. Ruby's birthday card is Grandma reading with three arms on purpose. One for the book, one for the glasses, one for the lamp. It is on Mom's fridge next to the first painting. Mom turned fifty-nine on Tuesday.

WES: Good. That is honor that is not a repair. I want the listener who only knows how to be useful to sit with a photo that has no task in it. You did not fix a lamp. You watched a woman buy one.

JONATHON: I almost offered to pay. I put my hands in my pockets instead. That felt like I was failing her. It was probably the first right thing I have done in a store with her in years.

[BOARD: Bad light is not a personality.]


## SEGMENT 2 · The barn and a receipt

WES: Saturday I pulled into the barn at five fifty-five. He said well. A bucket. An hour. A water trough. He did not talk to Dad while I was there. At the end he said, same time next week. I said yeah. That is eight words if you are generous. I am going to keep showing up for the eight words.

JONATHON: You used to wait for him to come to you. Now you are the one on the gravel at six. I keep thinking about how long a number can live in a pocket before it dies. You carried one for nineteen days once.

WES: Spring of two thousand eleven. Mr. Abernathy asked me a question at a men's breakfast and left. No invitation. I got his number off the sign-in sheet at the church office, wrote it on a gas receipt, and carried it nineteen days. My daughter was nine. She said, Daddy, you are not listening. I sent the text from the porch. Mr. Abernathy, this is Wes Hart from the men's breakfast. You asked me what I am waiting on, and I do not know, and I would like you to ask me again.

JONATHON: That is the scariest text I ever heard a man send, and I have sent a few. What did he do.

WES: He called back inside a minute. Thursday. Six o'clock. The diner by the highway. I will ask you again. My daughter said, who was that. A man I am going to have breakfast with. Why. I do not know yet. First Thursday I said I still do not know. He said, good. Now we know where we are starting. We found a spiral notebook later with the receipts. My first line is dated the morning of the breakfast, before the text.

JONATHON: You wrote it before you asked. That is a man who wanted it, not a man who got chosen. I have been waiting to be picked by older men since I was nineteen. The sales manager at the dealership told me never let them see you hurt. He was divorced three times. I had been living that sentence since I was nine. I thought that was a mentor. It was a warning I treated like a map.

WES: Mr. Abernathy died about five years ago, eighty-eight. Later in the notebook he wrote, a man has to want it enough to ask. Otherwise it is my breakfast, not yours. I tapped the table at my oldest last year and asked him what he is waiting on. I did not wait for him to come find me. I learned that late.

[BOARD: The floor teaches first.]


## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: Proverbs thirteen, verse twenty. A proverb about company, not about networking.

JONATHON: "Walk with the wise and become wise, for a companion of fools suffers harm."

WES: You become who you walk with. If you walk with nobody, you still become someone. You become the man who calls isolation wisdom. If you walk with a fool who sounds strong, you become his student. The ask is how you choose the walk. It will not happen to you while you wait to be recruited.

JONATHON: I have walked with the leaderboard for years. I have walked with a manager who called me champ and paid a coach to keep me sharp for numbers. That is a kind of company. It is not the same as a man who will ask me again on a Thursday. I want the second kind, and I have been too proud to request it.

WES: We're about halfway. Pride looks like patience. I called it not wanting to bother him. I bothered him. He was glad. Most men ahead are not as busy as your fear needs them to be. Some will say no. We will talk about that Friday. Today I want you to ask.

[BOARD: Proverbs 27:17 (NIV) · "As iron sharpens iron, so one person sharpens another."]


## SEGMENT 4 · The men ahead

WES: My man ahead this week is the car-care mechanic. Eighty-one. Retired Air Force. Black coffee, one sugar. He works on the church van Wednesday mornings. I am going to ask him, how do you get old without getting small. Wednesday. If he says no, I will find out with everybody else.

JONATHON: Mine is Dani's dad. Seventy-four. New Braunfels. Married fifty-one years, same house. He drove Dani to every shift for a year when her car died. He fixed Theo's bike chain at Easter. He holds her mom's chair. The golf years are real. I have known him since before the wedding. About three hundred meals. I have never asked a real question. How did you stay. That is the question. Sunday after the dishes. Dani takes her mom and the kids outside.

JONATHON: Dani said she thought I would never ask him anything. I said it does not count if I tell you. She already knew the question. She still made me keep it. I have a card in my wallet with those four words so I do not get cute on the porch.

WES: Good. Do not add a speech about the golf years. Ask how he stayed. Then shut up. The mechanic may say no. Dani's dad may give you a short answer. Short is still an answer. You cannot get a short answer from a man you never ask.

JONATHON: The nephew asked me a real thing and I gave him a real answer. About forty minutes. Dani timed that too. I can do forty minutes for a twenty-two-year-old on a phone and freeze in front of a seventy-four-year-old at a table. That is the tell. I am not actually afraid of questions. I am afraid of older men seeing me.

[BOARD: Wanting it is the invitation.]


## SEGMENT 5 · Pushback

WES: Push. The man who says he has no one ahead. And the man who says asking is needy.

JONATHON: If you have no one ahead, you still have a room. A shop. A table. A diner. You may have to get a number off a sign-in sheet like Wes did. That is not needy. That is how adults start a walk. Needy is waiting ten years for someone to notice you are drowning and then being angry they did not.

WES: The man who had a cruel father thinks we are telling him to go find a replacement dad and hand over his keys. We are not. We are telling him to walk with someone wise. That can be coffee once a month. It can be a question on a creeper under a van. It does not have to be adoption.

JONATHON: I do not need Dani's dad to become my dad. I need one true answer from a man who stayed. If he says I do not know, that is still more than I have. I have built a whole story about staying without ever asking a stayer. That story is mostly movies.

WES: I have built stories too. I told myself Mr. Abernathy would call if he meant it. He had already asked. The rest was mine. Some of you are carrying a receipt right now. Nineteen days is already late. Send the text from the porch. Then go inside and listen to your kid, because she can tell when you are not in the room.


## SEGMENT 6 · This week's challenge

WES: Name one man ahead. Ask him one real question this week. Write the question down so you do not add a door to it. If you do not have a name, get one the way I did. A sheet. A receipt. A porch.

JONATHON: Do not wait to be chosen. I have waited through three hundred meals. That is not patience. That is a museum of brisket. Sunday I ask. If I freeze, I will read the card. How did you stay. Then I will pour the coffee and stop talking.

WES: Last few minutes. If he says no, you still asked. Friday we will sit with a no, because I may get one under a van. Do not use my possible no as your excuse to skip the ask. Get your own no. It is cleaner than a fantasy.

JONATHON: I have used other men's nos as cover. Not this week. I have a porch and a card and a woman who will know if I come home with a story about almost. Almost is not the report.

WES: I still remember the diner by the highway. Formica. His hat on the extra chair. He did not impress me. He asked me again. That was the whole gift. I keep trying to make mentorship a program. It was eggs and a question.

WES: I keep wanting this to resolve in one conversation, and it will not. The assignment is the next honest sentence, said on time, without a speech attached. I have left too many good talks on a table because I wanted a better closing line than the one I had.

JONATHON: Honestly, I will try to add the speech anyway. Somebody should stop me. If you are sitting in a driveway doing that too, write the three sentences down before you dial, then read only those three. The fourth sentence is where I get cute and ruin it.

WES: I want this back in your house, not in ours. Pick the one door that is yours. Not your brother's. Not a man on a screen. Yours. Put a day on it before supper, even if the day is tomorrow and the sentence is clumsy.

JONATHON: Mine is already on a calendar. That is the only reason I trust it. A mood is not a plan, and I have used moods for years to sound wise while I stayed still. I do not want to sound wise. I want to be on time.

WES: If you fail the first try, the second try still counts. I have to say that every time or I will use the miss as a way out and then tell a story about the miss like the story was the obedience.

JONATHON: I used the first miss as a way out for most of my twenties. I am trying not to do that at thirty-seven. It is harder than any capacitor, and it is also more ordinary than I want to admit at this table.

WES: I have watched men collect stories like this and never change a Tuesday. Do not be that man this week. Change one Tuesday. Then tell one person you did, because a secret obedience turns back into a mood by Thursday.

JONATHON: I am the man who collects stories. I have a notes app full of them. The only ones that count are the ones that got a day and a time and a person who knows I said I would do it.

## SEGMENT · Sitting with it

WES: I want the listener who thinks this does not apply because his house is quieter. Quiet is not settled. You already know that if you have sat through a polite holiday and called it peace.

JONATHON: My house can be loud and still be a cease-fire. Volume is not the test. The test is whether the true thing got said to the person who had to hear it, not to a mug at this table.

WES: I will check this on Friday, which is the only reason I trust myself. A public calendar is a kind of mercy for a man who likes his recliner and likes to call the recliner wisdom.

JONATHON: I will hold you to it, and I want somebody holding me. That is why I say the time out loud even when it makes me look late. Looking late is better than looking peaceful and being dug in.

WES: A steadfast man does the small thing while it is still small. That is the whole show, said again, because we forget it by Wednesday and start waiting to feel ready, and the ready never comes first.

JONATHON: I forget it by Tuesday afternoon. Ruby does not. She has a better memory for what I said I would do than any review I have ever run, and she does not care that I had a good reason.

WES: If this feels like too many stories, pick one. One house. One name. One time. That is enough weight for seven days, and it is more than most of us will actually carry if we get proud of the list.

WES: I have a habit of saving the true sentence for a better room. There is no better room. The kitchen is the room. The truck is the room. The porch after the dishes is the room. Say it there.

JONATHON: I have rehearsed in better rooms my whole adult life. The rehearsal is how I stay safe. Dani can hear the difference between a rehearsal and a sentence. She stops me when I start handing people a door before they are in the room.

WES: Some of you will try to make this spiritual so you do not have to be specific. Prayer is good. A day and a name is still required. Do not baptize delay and call it discernment.

JONATHON: I have baptized delay. I have called it being careful. Careful is sometimes fear with a pressed shirt. I am trying to tell those two apart before I waste another month.

WES: The man who says he will start after the busy season is lying to himself the same way I did in my forties. There is always another season. Do the small thing inside the busy, or you will never do it.

JONATHON: HVAC sales does not hand you a quiet month. If I wait for one I will still be waiting when Theo has a license. I have to put the true thing on a Tuesday that already has a meeting.

WES: I do not need you to feel ready. I need you to be on time. Ready is a feeling. On time is a clock. I have trusted the feeling too long, and it has made me late to people I love.

JONATHON: I wait for ready like it is a weather window. Then the window closes and I tell a funny story about almost doing it. I do not want another funny story. I want a clumsy sentence that landed.
