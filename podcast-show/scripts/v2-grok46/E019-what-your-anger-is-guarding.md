## SEGMENT 1 · The horn

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the ugly version, not the cleaned-up one where I was just tired. Rumors of layoffs. A man cut me off. I hit the wheel. The horn went and I held it. I can still hear it. She was in the booster. She did not say a word to me. She told her mother that night. I found out later. I apologized to a kitchen, not to a seven-year-old, because I told myself she had not really seen it.

JONATHON: Kids see everything. They just wait to decide what it meant. What did she think it meant, this week, sixteen years later?

WES: She is twenty-five now. She said, "I remember. I thought it was because I was kicking your seat." She had been kicking the seat. She had built a whole theology around her shoes. I told her it was the layoff rumor and a man in a truck and a sentence I was living, pay the bills and don't make trouble. I was scared I could not pay the bills. The horn was guarding a paycheck I was not sure I could keep. She was quiet. Then she said, "I wish you'd said that then." I said I wish I had too. I do not get 2009 back. I get a Tuesday where I tell the truth about a horn.

JONATHON: I'll be real with you, I would have built a joke around the horn and never named the paycheck. That's how I keep anger looking like personality. Big feelings. Short fuse. Italian blood, or sales, or Monday. Anything except fear.

WES: Years later Mr. Abernathy tapped the table twice, the way he did, and asked, "What's it guarding?" He did not ask why I was angry. He asked what the anger was standing in front of. I said nothing the first time. The second time I said the old sentence. Pay the bills and don't make trouble. He nodded like a man who had already known. Then he asked if I had told my girl. I had not. I have now. Late. Late is still allowed.

[BOARD: The heat is usually covering a smaller word.]

WES: If you have a horn in you, I want you to hear that the girl in the booster will write a story if you do not. She will put herself in the middle of it because children do that. Your job is to go get the story and replace it with the true one, even sixteen years later. Especially sixteen years later.

## SEGMENT 2 · The shoulder

WES: Your Saturday. I want the snap, not the apology first.

JONATHON: Second practice. Saturday morning, before car-care. Shoulder already sore from the first week. A dad offered to throw batting practice. I said, "I've got it," in front of everybody, and I kept throwing in pain. That afternoon I snapped at Ruby about a juice box that was not even leaking. What it was guarding: never let them see you hurt. I learned that young. I have used it at work, in a marriage, and now on a field of seven-year-olds. I apologized to the dad. He throws BP now. I told Dani it hurts. First time I have said that out loud in years. She said, "Then go to a doctor." I have not gone. So I am still guarding a little. I wanted that on the record before anybody thinks a sorry fixed a shoulder.

WES: Never let them see you hurt is a boy's sentence that will run a man's house. It looks like toughness. It is fear of being the kind of man who needs help. You snapped at a juice box because you would not hand a bucket of balls to another dad.

JONATHON: That's the math. I hate the math. I also texted the contractor, because he used to throw a hard hat when a job went sideways. His crew called it hat weather. He has not thrown one in eight years. He is scared he will throw one at a student when he starts teaching. I text him every Monday of this first stretch, "What's it guarding?" He writes back one word most weeks. Pride. Money. His dad. Last Monday he wrote, "looking weak in front of boys who already think I'm old." That's a grown man. I told him I threw BP on a bad shoulder so twelve kids would think I was fine. He wrote, "dumb. go to a doctor." Two men, same sentence.

WES: Go to the doctor. I am not your mother. I am the man who watched you limp a little when you sat down. The shoulder is a date. Put it on the calendar the way we put a fan on a Saturday. Unnamed pain becomes weather. Weather becomes a juice box. A juice box becomes a girl who thinks she kicked the seat.

[BOARD: The carton is rarely the real subject.]

JONATHON: My mom's anger was the cold kind. Silent for a day. The house went quiet and you did the inventory of every word you had said since breakfast. Dani told me in October, "You sound like your mom when she's mad at the toaster." She meant the ice, not the volume. I have a hot version and a cold version. The hot one snaps. The cold one cleans the kitchen like a punishment. Both of them are guarding the same thing. If I go still and clean, I do not have to say I am hurt.

## SEGMENT 3 · The ledge and the lot

WES: Two more pictures, because I want men to see this is not only a horn and a shoulder. Ruby's birthday party at a climbing gym. You told me a line I have been carrying.

JONATHON: The instructor said, "It doesn't take a ledge. It takes a bump." The wall looks impossible until you notice the little holds. Anger is like that. Men wait for a cliff, a blow-up, a story big enough to confess. Most of us live on bumps. A horn. A juice box. A hard hat. A silent kitchen. If you only repent of cliffs you will never repent of the way you drive.

WES: A man at my Thursday table has been divorced six years. Every custody exchange in a fast-food parking lot he sat in the truck angry. What it was guarding: he was scared his kids like the stepdad more. He started getting out and talking to the stepdad about the Astros for five minutes. He said, "We're not friends. We're Astros people." His daughter says it is her favorite part of the exchange. He did not become a soft man. He named the fear and he got out of the truck. That's a bump. That's also a grown-up.

JONATHON: I need that, because I will wait for a season of anger management and miss the parking lot. Five minutes about the Astros is more sanctified than a speech about my childhood. I can talk about my dad leaving for an hour and still snap at a juice box. The parking lot is the test. I keep wanting a class with a workbook so I can postpone the five minutes. A class would let me stay the expert. Five minutes would make me a man in a lot with another man I do not like, talking about a team, so a little girl can see two adults in the same air. That is a smaller thing than a program and a harder thing than a program.

WES: The parking lot is the test. So is the booster seat. So is the kitchen after the toaster. If you want a program you can hide in a program. If you want a different Tuesday you pick a bump. I have hidden in books about anger and still hit a wheel. The book was not the problem. The night I would not name the paycheck was the problem. I want you bored with your own insight and busy with one bump. Insight without a lot is just a new way to stay right.

## SEGMENT 4 · The Word

WES: We're about halfway, and I want the Bible open, because Paul already told a church not to make a campsite out of anger. Ephesians four, twenty-six and twenty-seven.

JONATHON: I have used the first half as a permission slip. Be angry. I skip the rest.

WES: He says, "Do not let the sun go down while you are still angry, and do not give the devil a foothold." Anger is allowed. A night of it as a house guest is not. The sun going down is a clock. A foothold is what happens when the clock is ignored. You do not have to finish the whole war before dark. You have to stop feeding it overnight. Name it. Say the fear. Put the phone down. Ask the question. Then sleep. Morning can finish what night should not carry.

[BOARD: Proverbs 16:32 (NIV) · "Better a patient person than a warrior, one with self-control than one who takes a city."]

JONATHON: Here's my problem with the sunset clock. Dani works nights. The sun goes down and she is at the hospital. If I wait for a perfect conversation I will wait until Thursday, and Thursday I will have a better speech and a colder house. So I write it on a card and I say it in the morning when she walks in, even if I am clumsy. Clumsy before noon is better than a polished paragraph after a night of rehearsing.

WES: Clumsy before noon is the whole Christian life for some of us. I told my daughter this week. I told my wife the night of the horn in 2009, badly. I did not tell the girl. The sun went down on that one for sixteen years. I do not recommend my timeline.

JONATHON: And if the anger is righteous? A man whose ex left bald tires on a car the kids ride in. We saw that Saturday at car-care. Four used tires by Wednesday. That heat was guarding those kids, not his pride. I need us to say some anger is a smoke alarm. I stood there holding a coffee and I could feel my own cheap heat trying to join his good heat, like I wanted a villain so I could stay the hero. His heat bought tires. Mine wanted a speech. That's how I know which kind I am carrying. The kind that buys tires can stay long enough to finish the work. The kind that wants a speech needs the sun to go down on it.

WES: Some anger is a smoke alarm. You still do not get to sleep with the alarm screaming. You fix the smoke. Bald tires get tires. A layoff rumor gets a conversation, not a horn. A sore shoulder gets a doctor, not a juice box. Righteous heat still has a clock. If it lives overnight it starts lying about what it is.

[BOARD: Proverbs 29:11 (NIV) · "Fools give full vent to their rage, but the wise bring calm in the end."]

## SEGMENT 5 · Pushback

WES: Keep pushing.

JONATHON: What about the man whose dad hit him? If I sit here talking about a juice box, he is going to think we just baptized his father's fists as a personality. Draw the line in paint.

WES: Draw it. A father's fists are sin. They are not a shape you inherit like a nose. You do not confess his hands. You confess yours. If your anger is starting to look like his, that is the part that's yours, and it is why we are here. If you are in danger of putting hands on somebody, you leave the room and you call a brother and you get help today. This table is not a substitute for that.

JONATHON: Second. I go cold, not loud. People at work think I am the calm one. Dani is the only one who knows the toaster silence. If I only repent of volume I will never repent of ice.

WES: Then repent of ice. Cold is still a night the sun went down on. The foothold does not care about decibels. It cares about the story you are feeding. Never let them see you hurt is a quiet story. It still runs a house.

JONATHON: Third. Naming the fear can become a new performance. I can say, "What's it guarding?" like a man who already did the work, and still not call the doctor. The sentence is not the lift if the date is missing.

WES: Then put a date on it. Shoulder. Horn. Parking lot. Doctor. Conversation. Write it where a seven-year-old can see it if you have one. Ruby already has a basket. She can have a card. I told my wife I would ask her tonight what she sees my anger guarding this month. I will not explain first. I said the job last time and she said my mother. I am ready to be wrong again. I have been ready to be right for fifty-two years. Being wrong in a kitchen is a newer skill and I am still clumsy at it. Clumsy is fine. Hidden is not. If she names a thing I do not like I will write it on the same card as the horn and I will not add a footnote until morning. Morning is allowed to kill footnotes. Night is not allowed to grow them.

## SEGMENT 6 · This week's challenge

WES: Last few minutes. Name one anger from this week, even a small one. Write the sentence: it was guarding ______. Then tell one person, with no because. If a person was in the blast, tell them the true story so they do not write themselves into the middle. If you live alone, tell a brother, or tell God out loud in the truck and then text one man the sentence. Friday we will give you a twenty-minute rule so you do not turn this into a speech.

JONATHON: Mine is the shoulder. It was guarding never let them see you hurt. I told the dad. I told Dani. I have not told a doctor. So my contrary hour is an appointment, not another paragraph. I texted my nephew the question because he snaps at his roommates when he is lonely. He wrote, "guarding: looking like the kid who got left." I told him that was a real sentence and he should say it to the roommate he trusts, not to the group chat. He wrote, "ok. not the chat." That's a win.

WES: Mine is the horn I already told and the short voice I still get at PT. I will tell you more Friday. Tonight I ask my wife the question. I want her sentence, not mine. If she says the bills again I will not be shocked. If she says something else I will sit a night before I try the case. The girl in the booster already waited sixteen years. I can wait one night.

JONATHON: And if you cannot think of an anger, ask the person who lives with you what she has been walking around. She already has a list. Do not pick a nicer one from 2009 if she names a juice box from Saturday. The first one that makes your stomach tight is the assignment.

WES: Look around your house this week. If there is a booster, a juice box, a hard hat, a silent kitchen, that is enough. You do not need a cliff. You need a bump, a sentence, and a clock that still has daylight. I keep a list on the fridge now, under the PT times, of the things I have called weather. A horn. A title. A fan that finally got a Saturday. Anger wants to join that list and call itself climate. It is not climate. It is a Tuesday with a name. Write the name. Tell one person. Then stop feeding it after dark. If you cannot sleep, you already handed it over, and that still counts more than a speech you will write at two in the morning to win a case nobody asked you to try.
