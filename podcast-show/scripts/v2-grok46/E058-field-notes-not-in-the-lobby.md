## SEGMENT 1 · Check the box

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the texts, because Friday is the receipt. You go first. I already used the hook.

JONATHON: I texted him Saturday night. Not "Anybody special?" I wrote, "What's hard at work right now?" Twenty minutes later he called, then kept it in texts because he was on a job. Second-year apprentice electrician. A journeyman calls him College because he did one semester. He'd been thinking about quitting and told no one. I didn't give a speech. I asked what the journeyman is like on a good day.

WES: And what did he say when you didn't rush in to fix his pride?

JONATHON: "He's the best electrician I've ever seen. He just hates college kids." I wrote back, "You went one semester." He sent a laugh. Not quitting this week. That's the whole report on the work ask, and it is more of a man than two years of oranges.

WES: Then Theo's morning. The real ask.

JONATHON: I texted it the next day. "When Theo turns twelve I want you to be one of the men in the room. You'll be thirty. It's a real thing, not a show thing." He wrote back, "Is that a real thing or a show thing?" I said real. He said, "I'll be thirty." Then, "Okay. Yeah." Three words, and I sat in the driveway longer than I want to admit.

[BOARD: A real ask treats him like he can keep it.]

WES: Wednesday he noticed you skipped the girls question. You still haven't answered.

JONATHON: I haven't, because if I explain it I'll turn it back into a speech. I'm going to write, "Yeah. I asked a real one instead." Then stop. He can sit with that.

WES: My box. I sent the text. "Heard you can sing. Merle's lucky either way." Wednesday afternoon he wrote, "thanks wes." Nothing on Thursdays. Merle said nothing at church. I'm leaving it there. I will not chase a paragraph.

JONATHON: That's harder for you than the long text, because you like a clean loop.

WES: I like a clean loop. He said don't fix it. So I didn't. I sat with "thanks wes" the way I sat with two waters in a truck. It is not a testimony. It is just a man answering without giving me his whole week.

JONATHON: His roommate came up in a later text. Vegan. Dani had made a separate pan of beans one Sunday and the roommate never forgot it. I'm not turning that into a cameo. The roommate is his. The text is mine. I'm staying in my lane.

WES: Stay in the lane. Look around your house this week. If you've got a younger man on your phone, notice whether you are asking him to perform a life or tell you the truth.

## SEGMENT 2 · The boot

WES: Then I tried the attic Saturday, because Oklahoma is still on the guest room ceiling. Two hours. A hundred and twenty degrees. A wasp nest. No leak I could find. So I called a man from church who runs sound every Sunday and has never been asked to do anything else.

JONATHON: The tomato man. Nine kinds. A brother in Lubbock he doesn't talk to. I need you to say you asked him for help, not for a project.

WES: I asked him to look. Thursday after work he was there ten minutes and said, "It's the boot on your vent pipe. It's cracked." Then he sat at the kitchen table an hour. My wife got seeds and instructions. He's coming back Saturday with a new boot. I buy it. He shows me how.

JONATHON: So the assigned thing for a married man this week was admitting he couldn't see the leak, and letting a single man teach him in his own attic.

WES: That's exactly it. He's forty-four. Never married. Fifteen years on the board. Roofed houses in his twenties. I have treated him like furniture in the booth. This week he was the man who knew.

[BOARD: Ask the man who already knows the room.]

WES: I was twenty-three once and dated a girl most of a year so I wouldn't come home to an empty room. I ended it by phone and said God told me to focus on Him, then skipped church three months. That was the lobby wearing a Bible cover. I'm telling you so I don't get holy about a twenty-four-year-old who actually does his grandmother's nails.

JONATHON: That's the confession I needed you to make, because Tuesday can sound like married men lecturing. You were the empty-room guy. I was the pregame guy. Neither of us gets to talk down. I dated the way I sold, and I called it a season, and it was just me refusing to be a person without an audience.

WES: At twenty-two I ate off a TV tray and watched a ballgame every night and called that independence. It was a lobby with a remote. I don't want to forget that while I'm handing out assignments.

JONATHON: So the sound man teaching you the boot is the picture. A single man with a name, not a project. You buy the part. He shows you. That's honor, not charity.

WES: That's honor. I'm going to say his tomatoes out loud so I remember he has a life I have never asked about. Nine kinds. I asked about one. I have fourteen more questions if I ever become a man who asks. Starting with the brother in Lubbock, and why they don't talk, and whether he wants to.

JONATHON: Don't start with Lubbock. Start with the boot. Then ask if he wants to come eat, not fix anything. A meal without a job is how a lobby becomes a house.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: We're about halfway, and I want one verse for the man who thinks he has nothing in his hands. First Peter four, verse ten.

JONATHON: "Each of you should use whatever gift you have received to serve others, as faithful stewards of God’s grace in its various forms."

[BOARD: 1 Corinthians 7:20 (NIV) · "Each person should remain in the situation they were in when God called them."]

WES: Peter is writing to ordinary people, not a stage. The gift is already received. Past tense. You use it. You don't wait to feel titled. A cracked boot is a gift if you know how to see it. A semester you didn't finish is not the whole name.

JONATHON: "Various forms." That's the part that gets me. Nails. Sound. A Tuesday text. A melody from the back row. None of those look like a calling on a flyer, and all of them are grace in a form.

WES: If you needed a prettier gift than the one in your hands, you would still be in the lobby. Use the one you have. A semester you didn't finish is not the whole name. A cracked boot is not small if a ceiling is staining Oklahoma.

JONATHON: I'll be real with you. I wanted Peter to say "gift" and mean a stage. He means a towel. He means a text. He means a man who can see a vent pipe. That's why this verse is harder than the conference, and it's why I keep trying to upgrade the gift before I use it.

WES: Context in two sentences. Peter is talking about the end being near, and therefore serving, not performing. The various forms are how grace shows up in a kitchen and a warehouse and a booth. If the form in your hand looks ordinary, that is not a clue you missed your calling. That is the calling.

## SEGMENT 4 · From the table

WES: Last few minutes on a question men ask. Should I stop dating to focus on God? I'm labeling it honestly. Not a letter we invented. A younger man at church asked it after Tuesday.

JONATHON: I did a version of that at twenty-three in my head. If I stop chasing, God will send her. Then I chased anyway, and called the chasing a season.

WES: If dating has become the lobby, stop. If dating is how you hide from your grandmother and your church, stop. If you can date like a believer in the situation you're in, you don't owe anybody a monk year. The test is Tuesday. Are you using the gift, or are you using her to feel like your life started? I used a girl to avoid an empty room and then blamed God for the phone call. I don't get to forget that when a younger man asks this.

JONATHON: And if you're the married man advising him, don't make him your unfinished twenties. Ask what he did this week. Then shut up. I wanted to send him a paragraph about quitting and calling. I sent two sentences. That's the most Christian thing I've done for him in a month.

WES: Two sentences is a lot if they're the right ones. Most of us send twenty and still ask about a girl anyway.

[BOARD: Colossians 3:23 (NIV) · "Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters,"]

WES: Tool this week is a single man's Tuesday rhythm. Work you can stand on. One meal that isn't gas-station silence. One older man you text. One thing for somebody who can't pay you back. Four lines. That's a life. That's not a holding pattern.

JONATHON: Mine this week: answer his Wednesday text without a speech. Then ask the sound man his brother's name, because I don't know it and I've sat behind him for a year.

WES: Mine: buy the boot. Let him teach me. Don't turn it into a testimony before Saturday is over. If I tell this like a win before he comes back, I stole it.

JONATHON: Single guys, write the four lines Sunday night. Work you can stand on. One meal that isn't the gas station with the phone. One older man, even if the text is awkward. One person who can't pay you back. That's how you get out of the lobby without a ring.

WES: Divorced men, the four lines still work. One of them is your kids, even on the weeks they aren't with you. Widowed men, Merle already wrote the card. You can write a smaller one.

JONATHON: Married men, your four lines this week include quitting one lobby question. Write the question you keep asking. Then write the real one. Send the real one. Delete the other. If you can't think of a real one, ask what is hard at work. That one opened a door I had been knocking on with oranges for two years.

WES: And if he doesn't answer, you still asked. That's assigned. That's not a failed campaign, and it still counts.

[BOARD: Four lines is a life, not a lobby.]
