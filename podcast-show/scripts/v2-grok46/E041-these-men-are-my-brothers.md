## SEGMENT 1 · The porch and the umbrella

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on Sunday, because you actually asked. Brisket. Dishes. A porch. Do not clean it up.

JONATHON: I did all the dishes. First time in ten years of Sunday lunches. Dani's mom asked if something was wrong. Dani took her mom and the kids to the tomatoes. On the back porch I had his coffee and the card. How did you stay. Half a cup. A mockingbird. Then, I do not know, son. We did not think about it like that. We just did not leave.

WES: That is an answer. I know you hate it. Stay with why you hate it before you make it a slogan.

JONATHON: I asked about the golf years. He said those were not good years. Her mother should have thrown me out. She did not. Then he said help me with this umbrella, the base is full of sand and my back is no good. Mid-carry, complaining about the man who sold it, he said Dani's mom's brother called him every Thursday night for about three years in those years. The Rangers. The weather. I said, to check on you. He said he does not know. He never asked why. The uncle died in two thousand nine. He said that's better, and went back to the golf.

WES: You wanted a method. You got an uncle who dialed. That is the whole brotherhood lesson for Memorial Day week, and it is going to bother you because it is not a framework.

JONATHON: I am mad all week that it is an answer. I wanted a map I could sell to a twenty-nine-year-old. I got a dead uncle and a man who never asked why the phone rang. I am going to be the uncle anyway. I do not need to understand it to copy the Thursday.

[BOARD: The floor teaches first.]


## SEGMENT 2 · Barn, dog, kitchen

WES: Second Saturday at the barn. His thermos had two cups. A gate latch. About nine words. Hand me that. I am going to Abilene Saturday because the little brother's father-in-law is in hospice now. My go-first this week is smaller. Thursday I call the little brother about nothing, and I learn the dog's name. Forty videos. I have never asked.

JONATHON: You have watched a dog for years and never asked its name. That is the same sin as Harper's dad, just with fur. I have a version at work. I will get to him. Finish the kitchen first, because I can tell you are not done being ashamed of it.

WES: The band director at the Thursday table. Retired. Sixty-one then. He brings the good coffee because he says our pot is an insult to the bean. A couple of years ago he missed three Thursdays. Nobody called. Fourth Thursday he told us his wife had breast cancer, third round of chemo. He said he kept waiting for one of us to call. I asked what he wanted. Could you come to my kitchen. Six weeks of Thursdays at his house. She came down in a headscarf and a robe and said we were too loud.

JONATHON: She is two years clear this spring and still tells you you are too loud. That is a living woman who was alone while a table of men practiced being faithful in theory. I hate that story. I need it.

WES: Table practice since: if a man misses, whoever notices calls, voice, by Friday noon. About thirty calls. Twice it was not nothing. One man had slept in his truck in his own driveway for a week. He is back in the house. Somebody seeing him was the whole medicine. We were late. We are less late now.

[BOARD: A role is not a man.]


## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: Hebrews three, verse thirteen. A daily word, not a holiday word.

JONATHON: "But encourage one another daily, as long as it is called “Today,” so that none of you may be hardened by sin’s deceitfulness."

WES: Daily. Today. Not when you feel a swell of loyalty. Not when a flag is up. Encourage is not a compliment. It is a call. It is a kitchen. It is a Thursday night about the Rangers while a man is failing at being a husband. The uncle did Hebrews without a study Bible. We had a study Bible and we did not call.

JONATHON: I have encouraged men on Monday calls when their numbers were good. That is not this verse. This verse is for the week his video stays dark and you walk past a truck at lunch. I walked past once. I am still ashamed of the once.

WES: We're about halfway. Tell the truck. Memorial Day week is a good week to tell the truth about ordinary Thursdays, because the graves will get the speeches either way.

[BOARD: John 15:13 (NIV) · "Greater love has no one than this: to lay down one’s life for one’s friends."]


## SEGMENT 4 · Pancakes and a truck

WES: The Austin manager. You have been beside him on a leaderboard for eight years. Tell it with permission and without his name.

JONATHON: Fifty-three. His numbers fell in March and April. He kept his screen dark on Monday calls. I told Dani that Austin was having a bad year. She asked if he was okay. I did not like that question. Quarterly meeting in Austin, catered fajitas. He sat in his running truck at lunch. I walked past once. Then I came back with two plates. Ten silent minutes. He said his wife moved out in March. Kids with her weekdays. Saturdays with him. I make them pancakes Saturday morning. That is the whole thing I am holding onto. Do not tell anybody at work.

WES: And you have texted him since.

JONATHON: Two Thursdays. Did you eat. He answers K. You said that is your move. His brother is in Oregon. The couple friends went with his wife. My go-first is Thursday, voice, and I ask about the pancakes. Not about the marriage. The pancakes. If I ask about the marriage I am a coworker hunting a story. If I ask about the pancakes I am a brother.

WES: Good. I lost a title in two thousand nineteen. The men I would have sworn would call did not. Two men called. One was a man from shipping I had talked to about five times, about bass. He said he heard they moved my desk. You okay. A year later I bought him lunch and tried to thank him. It came out sideways, something about bass. That is still one of the kindest calls of my life.

JONATHON: I have talked to you from a Target parking lot with Theo screaming about a toy shark. That counted. The nephew has a group chat with nine guys. I have a leaderboard. I am trying to become the man who walks back to the truck the first time, not the second.

[BOARD: Today is still a name.]


## SEGMENT 5 · Pushback

WES: Push. The man who says brothers are a luxury on a holiday week. And the man who only shows up for funerals.

JONATHON: A holiday week is when we give speeches about men we never called. If you have a flag and no Thursday, you have a costume. I hear the guy with two jobs. Then do the small version. One voice call. Did you eat. How is the dog. That is not a retreat. That is a minute in a parking lot.

WES: The funeral-only brother thinks presence at a grave makes him loyal. It makes him punctual for death. I have been that man. I could show up for a service and miss three Thursdays. The kitchen was the test. We failed it. We changed the rule. You can change a rule without a speech.

JONATHON: I want to be the uncle. I do not need a theology of uncles. I need a night on the calendar. Rangers. Weather. I can do that. I have done harder things for work. That is the embarrassment. I have energy for a leaderboard and a thin battery for a man in a truck.


## SEGMENT 6 · This week's challenge

WES: Call one man about nothing. Voice, not a text, unless text is the only way he will answer. If a man has gone missing from your table, call by Friday noon. Ask about the small thing he is holding. Pancakes. A dog. A desk they moved.

JONATHON: I will call Austin and ask about the pancakes. I will not ask about the marriage. I will not tell anybody at work. If he says K, that is still a live wire. I will not need a paragraph from him to count it.

WES: Last few minutes. I will call my little brother and ask the dog's name. I will not make it a speech about Abilene. Abilene is Saturday. Thursday is the dog. Do not stack them so you can fail both and feel deep.

JONATHON: I am not stacking the uncle plan with the Austin call. Austin is this Thursday. The uncle is a habit I have to start, not a project I announce. If I announce it I will want credit before I have dialed.

WES: I keep seeing that headscarf in the kitchen doorway. We were loud. She was kind. We should have been there on week one, not week four. A rule after a miss is still a mercy. Do not wait for a better origin story for your rule.

WES: I keep wanting this to resolve in one conversation, and it will not. The assignment is the next honest sentence, said on time, without a speech attached. I have left too many good talks on a table because I wanted a better closing line than the one I had.

WES: I want this back in your house, not in ours. Pick the one door that is yours. Not your brother's. Not a man on a screen. Yours. Put a day on it before supper, even if the day is tomorrow and the sentence is clumsy.

WES: If you fail the first try, the second try still counts. I have to say that every time or I will use the miss as a way out and then tell a story about the miss like the story was the obedience.

WES: I have watched men collect stories like this and never change a Tuesday. Do not be that man this week. Change one Tuesday. Then tell one person you did, because a secret obedience turns back into a mood by Thursday.

WES: I want the listener who thinks this does not apply because his house is quieter. Quiet is not settled. You already know that if you have sat through a polite holiday and called it peace.

WES: I will check this on Friday, which is the only reason I trust myself. A public calendar is a kind of mercy for a man who likes his recliner and likes to call the recliner wisdom.

JONATHON: I will hold you to it, and I want somebody holding me. That is why I say the time out loud even when it makes me look late. Looking late is better than looking peaceful and being dug in.

## SEGMENT · Sitting with it

WES: A steadfast man does the small thing while it is still small. That is the whole show, said again, because we forget it by Wednesday and start waiting to feel ready, and the ready never comes first.

JONATHON: I forget it by Tuesday afternoon. Ruby does not. She has a better memory for what I said I would do than any review I have ever run, and she does not care that I had a good reason.

WES: If this feels like too many stories, pick one. One house. One name. One time. That is enough weight for seven days, and it is more than most of us will actually carry if we get proud of the list.

JONATHON: I will pick one. If I pick three I will do none, and I already know that about myself from sales and from home. One is how a man like me gets free of his own notes app.

WES: I have a habit of saving the true sentence for a better room. There is no better room. The kitchen is the room. The truck is the room. The porch after the dishes is the room. Say it there.

JONATHON: I have rehearsed in better rooms my whole adult life. The rehearsal is how I stay safe. Dani can hear the difference between a rehearsal and a sentence. She stops me when I start handing people a door before they are in the room.

WES: Some of you will try to make this spiritual so you do not have to be specific. Prayer is good. A day and a name is still required. Do not baptize delay and call it discernment.

JONATHON: I have baptized delay. I have called it being careful. Careful is sometimes fear with a pressed shirt. I am trying to tell those two apart before I waste another month.

WES: The man who says he will start after the busy season is lying to himself the same way I did in my forties. There is always another season. Do the small thing inside the busy, or you will never do it.

JONATHON: HVAC sales does not hand you a quiet month. If I wait for one I will still be waiting when Theo has a license. I have to put the true thing on a Tuesday that already has a meeting.

WES: I do not need you to feel ready. I need you to be on time. Ready is a feeling. On time is a clock. I have trusted the feeling too long, and it has made me late to people I love.

JONATHON: I wait for ready like it is a weather window. Then the window closes and I tell a funny story about almost doing it. I do not want another funny story. I want a clumsy sentence that landed.

WES: Look at the next seven days and circle one hour. Not a vibe. An hour. If you cannot find an hour, you are not too busy. You are hiding inside busy, and I have done that with a laminated list and called it love.

JONATHON: I hide inside a full calendar better than anyone I know. A full calendar can still be a man running from one phone call. I am trying to leave a hole in Thursday on purpose.

WES: If your people are used to you disappearing into a screen after supper, tell them you are going to be late to the recliner this week. Then be late to the recliner. That is a kind of going first nobody applauds.

JONATHON: Theo notices the recliner more than he notices my speeches. If I stay at the table ten extra minutes he treats it like a holiday. That is how small this has to be to matter.

WES: I will not give you a poem. I will give you a time. If you need a poem after you have been on time, fine. Most of us want the poem first so we can skip the time.

JONATHON: I want the poem first. I always have. Then I get cute and the time slips. If I write the time on a sticky note where Dani can see it, I am much less poetic and much more likely to go.

WES: I have left good men as scenery. A table. A shop. A porch. Scenery does not call you when you fall. A name does. Stop collecting scenery and ask for a name.

WES: If you only obey when the story is pretty, you will obey twice a year. Pretty is a trap. Ordinary is the field. Do the ordinary thing while nobody is making it a lesson.

JONATHON: I wait for pretty. Then I miss the ordinary and write a paragraph about the miss. I want fewer paragraphs and one ordinary Thursday that actually happened.

## SEGMENT · Sitting with it

WES: Do not confuse a good conversation at this table with a changed kitchen. This table is practice. The kitchen is the game. I will not let us go home thinking practice was the game.

JONATHON: I am very good at practice. I can talk a good kitchen. Ruby can tell when I have only talked. She does not say it. She just watches whether I put the phone down.
