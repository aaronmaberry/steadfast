## SEGMENT 1 · The attic

WES: Pull up a chair. Let's take the next few minutes on an attic, because that's where this started, and it did not look like a calling on a flyer.

JONATHON: Annual ride-along, usually June, with the senior tech. Sixty. Thirty-one years in attics. Last call, an 84-year-old widow on the east side. She had sat in her running car. A hundred and thirty-eight in the attic on his belt thermometer. About an hour and a half. Capacitor and a burnt wire, fixed, not replaced. He said, "Ma'am, that'll hold you a few years. When it goes, call me first." She brought tea and held his face.

WES: You thanked him for not selling her a unit.

JONATHON: He said she didn't need one. That's the whole religion of a Thursday. I have a rule since I took the region four years ago. If it can be fixed, fix it. Don't upsell a repair. I lost numbers the first year. My boss asked twice. I kept the rule.

[BOARD: She did not need a unit.]

WES: That's handiwork. Not a slot. A woman in a car and a man who can see a wire.

---

## SEGMENT 2 · Warranties and foil

WES: You were twenty-three once.

JONATHON: I sold extended warranties at an electronics store. I quit mid-Saturday with no plan. I lived with my mother four months. I thought work was a trick you play on people. Then I became a man who can hold a flashlight and keep his mouth shut while a sixty-year-old does the real thing.

WES: Your mom worked every Labor Day. Two jobs. Holiday shifts paid better. A list on the counter and a sandwich with your name on the foil. At eleven you hated Labor Day.

JONATHON: I was the good work. That's a heavy sentence for a boy. I'm thirty-seven. I can say it without making her a villain. She kept us. The foil had my name. That's a kind of handiwork too.

WES: Theo is five. Don't let a loud season steal a pancake Saturday. Write it where a dentist would go. Then heat the pan.

---

## SEGMENT 3 · Cedar

WES: My granddad built cedar chests. One per grandchild. Measured to the foot of the bed. Hand-planed. Dovetailed. No nails. Winter shed, woodstove, farm report. Mine was built two years early, for the bed my older brother was moving out of. It's at the foot of my bed now. Winter quilts. Initials inside the lid. His and mine. The year.

JONATHON: That's a work that outlives a slot. Nobody clapped in a shed.

WES: Labor Day growing up was hay. In the field by seven. Dinner on the tailgate at noon. I have almost thirty years in the same kind of job. I am honestly unsure whether I sleepwalked through it. On the ride my wife said, "You've never once come home and said you hated it." Few at work know I pray.

JONATHON: The best man I work beside this year is the one who will not sell a widow air she doesn't need. Merle is seventy-one. He already knows this verse whether he can quote it or not.

[BOARD: A lid can hold a name longer than a title.]

WES: The best man I worked beside hated the job and still brought an extra lunch for a new kid for two years. Hate did not get the last word. The lunch did.

---

## SEGMENT 4 · The Word

WES: I've got a little under ten minutes, and I want the Bible open. Ephesians two, verse ten.

JONATHON: "For we are God’s handiwork, created in Christ Jesus to do good works, which God prepared in advance for us to do."

[BOARD: Colossians 3:23 (NIV) · "Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters,"]

WES: Handiwork. Not a slot. Prepared in advance. The attic was already on the calendar in heaven before it was on a truck. A capacitor is not small if a woman is in a car.

JONATHON: I wanted handiwork to mean a stage. It means a flashlight. It means foil with a name. It means a chest at the foot of a bed.

WES: Context in two sentences. Paul has just said saved by grace, not works. Then immediately, you were made for works. Not to get in. Because you are in. That's the split that keeps a man from selling a unit she doesn't need so he can feel saved by a number.

[BOARD: Galatians 6:9 (NIV) · "Let us not become weary in doing good, for at the proper time we will reap a harvest if we do not give up."]

JONATHON: Weary is 138 degrees. Good is a wire. Harvest may be tea and a face in your hands. That's enough pay.

---

## SEGMENT 5 · Pushback

WES: We're about halfway, and I want the push. A man says, my job is just a job.

JONATHON: Then do it as handiwork anyway. The verse does not require a feeling. It requires a prepared good. A flashlight is a good. A lunch is a good. A honest no on an upsell is a good.

WES: A man says, I hate my job.

JONATHON: Hate it and still bring the lunch. Then, if you can, look for the prepared good beside it. I hated warranties. The hate was information. Quitting with no plan was a boy. I needed both truths.

WES: A man says, nobody sees it.

JONATHON: God prepared it. That's a see. Virgil's notebook is a see. A widow's tea is a see. If you need a stage you will miss the attic.

WES: Last few minutes on what we're actually doing. I will ask my youngest to show me a weld bead he's proud of and I will say what I see. I will tell one man at work something true. The friend. The Tuesdays.

JONATHON: I'll ride with a tech every month and hold the flashlight. Labor Day Monday, tacos at the shop at six. I'll shake each man's hand by name. If I have to look a name up, I'll look it up. That's not cute. That's honor.

## SEGMENT 6 · The challenge

WES: This week's challenge. Do one prepared good that is not on your performance review. Say one true sentence at work. Ask one younger man to show you something he made and tell him what you see.

JONATHON: If you're between jobs, the prepared good is still in your hands. A grandmother. A flashlight you borrow. A name you shake. If you're retired, you are not off the verse. Handiwork does not have a pension date.

WES: Look around your house this week. The work you were made for may be a lid, a lunch, a wire. Don't wait to feel called. The car is already running in a driveway.

JONATHON: And if you sell, keep the rule. If it can be fixed, fix it. Numbers can wait. A face in your hands cannot.

## SEGMENT 7 · Flashlight

WES: Holding a flashlight is a kind of leadership men skip because it looks like a helper. Helper is often the prepared good. I have skipped helper so I could stay the man with the clipboard.

JONATHON: I'll hold the flashlight monthly. If I talk too much in an attic I will shut up. The sixty-year-old has thirty-one years. I have a mouth. The attic does not need my mouth.

## SEGMENT 8 · Names at six
