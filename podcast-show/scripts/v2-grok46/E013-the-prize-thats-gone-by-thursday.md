**Arc.** *Hook:* Jonathon opens a Monday leaderboard before anything else, and by Thursday the prize has slid. *Tension:* cheap prizes, public prayer, a plaque from the 1604 years. Scripture raises the pay. *Turn:* Mr. Abernathy's hidden list. *Resolution:* move the email, decline the mic, help the rep and tell no one.

---

## OPEN (word for word)

JONATHON: Every Monday at eight I open an email before I open anything else. Regional leaderboard. I have done that for eleven years.

WES: And by Thursday the top spot has usually slid, and you still gave the morning to a list that will not remember your name.

JONATHON: That's the prize. It is gone by Thursday, and I still treat it like it can love me back.

WES: This is Walk Steadfast. I'm Wes Hart.

JONATHON: And I'm Jonathon.

Before we're done I'll tell you about a list a man kept on the back of diner receipts, and he will tell you about a fall he cannot remember with his kids.

## SEGMENT 1 · The Monday email

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on cheap prizes. Yours first, because you already named the inbox. I want the listener to see his own Monday while you talk. Not your job. His. The thing he opens before he has been a man in his own house.

JONATHON: The email lands at eight. Rank, dollars, a little arrow. For eleven years I have let that arrow tell me who I am before I have told my kids good morning. Some Mondays I am kind in the kitchen. Some Mondays I am already in a fight with a spreadsheet I did not write. By Thursday somebody else has the top spot. The prize does not even stay the week. I have snapped at Ruby over toast because a number in Indianapolis moved. She did not know that. She just knew Dad was already gone before he left the house. That's who pays for a cheap prize. Not the regional manager. Your seven-year-old.

WES: So you are running hard for a ribbon they take down before Sunday.

JONATHON: That's it. And I know it's dumb. I still open it. I moved it this week. Ten o'clock. I have to sit through breakfast and the first two calls without a number on my forehead. I already reached for it at eight anyway. My hand went to the phone like a man reaching for a drink he quit. That's how I know it was a prize.

WES: I have a cheap prize too, and it wears a nicer suit. I like being the man the church calls on to pray in public. "Wes is a rock." I have said yes so many times I started needing the yes. This week they asked me to pray from the front on Sunday. I said no. I asked my son-in-law to do it. Twenty-seven, works for the county, first time praying in front of that room. His voice shook. I sat there wanting somebody to notice that I had given it away. That's the cheap prize trying to get paid twice.

JONATHON: You wanted credit for not taking credit. I know that move. I spent an hour Thursday after work helping the rep I reviewed, going through his call list. I told nobody at the office. Then I almost told you in the parking lot so I would still get a little clap. I shut up. It was harder than the hour.

[BOARD: A prize that needs a clap is already cheap.]

WES: Look around your house this week. What prize do you reach for before you have been a husband? A number. A name from the front. A notification. If it cannot love you back on Thursday, it is not the pay.

---

## SEGMENT 2 · The fall he cannot remember

WES: You said you would tell about a fall. Tell it.

JONATHON: Fall of 2023 I was number one in the country three weeks running. There is a plaque in my office. Those were the 1604 years. I was taking the long way home so I would miss bath. I cannot remember that fall with my kids. I can remember the plaque. I can remember the email. I cannot remember putting Ruby to bed. That's the receipt. The prize ate the days and left me a piece of wood. I used to tell that story like it was a hustle story. It is a grief story. I traded a season of my daughter's life for a ranking that reset every Monday, and I still have the wood on the wall because some part of me wants the room to know I was good. That's the ache. I was good at the wrong prize.

WES: [quietly] Yeah. That's the fog in a different shirt.

JONATHON: Then last spring Theo had croup. Three years old. Two in the morning, steamy bathroom, I held him for an hour. No plaque. No email. I remember every minute of that hour. He fell asleep on my chest and I sat on the toilet lid and I thought, this is the job. I still opened the leaderboard that Monday like the hour had not happened.

WES: So you know the real pay, and you still clock in for the fake one. That's most of us. We have held a sick kid and we have still wanted the room to clap.

JONATHON: Valentine's happened. You asked. It was good. That's all you get. I am not turning a decent dinner into content. We sat down. She was tired. I did not check the email. That's the win I actually want, and it will never show up on a regional list.

WES: I'll take "it was good." I do not need the menu. I needed to know you went and you stayed. I have turned decent nights into speeches so I could feel like a man who is doing well. She does not need the speech. She needs you in the chair with the phone down. That's the pay. The rest is marketing.

JONATHON: Marketing is the right word. I market myself to me. That's the sick part. The leaderboard is not even my boss talking. It's me selling me a version of me that looks tall. Dani does not want tall. She wants me home. I keep buying tall anyway because tall is easier to measure than home.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: We've got a little under fifteen minutes, and I want the Bible open, because Paul already named the pay. Colossians three, twenty-three and twenty-four. I'll read it.

WES: "Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters, since you know that you will receive an inheritance from the Lord as a reward. It is the Lord Christ you are serving."

[BOARD: Hebrews 12:2 (NIV) · "fixing our eyes on Jesus, the pioneer and perfecter of faith. For the joy set before him he endured the cross, scorning its shame, and sat down at the right hand of the throne of God."]

WES: Context in two lines. Paul is talking to people with regular jobs, some of them under hard bosses. He does not say work so the room will notice. He says work as if the Lord is the one signing the check. The reward is an inheritance. Not a plaque. Not a Monday arrow.

JONATHON: Okay, hold on. Inheritance can sound like earning heaven. I need us to say that out loud, because I have a version of me that will turn "work for God's pay" into another leaderboard with better branding.

WES: Fair. Ephesians two. "For it is by grace you have been saved, through faith—and this is not from yourselves, it is the gift of God— not by works, so that no one can boast." The inheritance is not a wage you earn. It is a gift you then work from. Cheap prizes make you earn your name every Monday. God's pay gives you a name and then puts a shovel in your hand.

JONATHON: So the leaderboard is me trying to earn a name I already have if I'm in Christ, and I still refresh it like the name might expire. That's wild. That's also exactly me.

WES: Same. I wanted the church to say "rock" so I would not have to remember I am a man who used to eat in a recliner. The cheap prize is usually covering an old ache. If you follow the prize back far enough you will find the thing you are afraid you are not. I was afraid I was still the fog. So I collected "rock" like insurance. Insurance is not faith. Faith does the hidden thing and lets God keep the books.

[BOARD: Matthew 6:4 (NIV) · "so that your giving may be in secret. Then your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you."]

## SEGMENT 4 · The receipts

WES: Mr. Abernathy kept the diner receipts. After he died his daughter found them. On the back of each one, names. Thirty-some men. He prayed for those men by name for years and told no one. I did not know. I ate with him for six years and I did not know. That's the pay that lasts. A list nobody clapped for. I have been thinking about that all week, because I want to be the man who is known for being a rock, and he was the man who was known by God for a list in his pocket. If you stacked his life next to a leaderboard, he would lose every Monday. I do not want to lose the way he won. I want the pocket list. I want the quiet names. I want to die and have somebody find out I loved men I never used for credit.

JONATHON: That's better than every plaque in my building. No email. No arrow. Just a man and a pen and a God who saw it.

WES: I keep a short list now, on the back of my index card. I will not say who is on it. If I say who is on it I am already collecting. The contractor told me he's going to teach welding at the high school at night. He's sixty. He said, "I've made money for forty years. I'd like to do something for a while that nobody pays me much for." That's a man changing prizes on purpose.

JONATHON: I want to be him at sixty, and I am afraid I will still be opening an email at eight. So I moved it to ten. That's my first inch. It is not a new career. It is a different clock.

WES: An inch is how this mark works. You do not have to quit your job. You have to stop letting the job be the name. I have sat with men who quit jobs thinking the prize would die, and they just took the prize to the next desk. The desk is not the problem. The hunger is the problem. Move the email. Give the invitation away. Help a man and tell nobody. Those are inches. Inches are how a hunger starves.

JONATHON: Here's my pushback. Some prizes are just how sales works. If I ignore the board I miss my family. If I ignore it too much I miss my quota and then I really miss my family. So is "work for God's pay" a reason to get soft at work? Because I do not think God asked me to be bad at HVAC.

WES: Be good at HVAC. Paul said work with all your heart. He did not say work for the arrow. You can be excellent and still refuse to let excellence be your god. The test is Thursday afternoon, not Monday morning. If the number slides and you take it out on Ruby, the prize owns you. If the number slides and you still run bath, you are working for a different check.

JONATHON: That's a clean test. I'll run it this week. If I get short at bath because of a Thursday slide, the email goes later still.

[BOARD: Excellence is not the same thing as worship.]

## SEGMENT 5 · What we're actually doing

WES: We're about halfway through the last stretch. What did you actually do?

JONATHON: Moved the email. Helped the rep and told nobody. Held the dinner and did not turn it into a story. I also caught myself wanting someone to notice the car-care coffee I said I'd make. That's the same disease. I have not even made the coffee yet and I already wanted a thank-you.

WES: I declined the invitation to pray from the front. I asked my son-in-law. After church my daughter squeezed my arm and said nothing, which is her version of a lot. I wanted a sentence. I did not get one. I sat with that. Then I wrote a name on the back of the card and I stopped. I also caught myself hoping somebody in the row would notice I had given it away. That's the cheap prize trying to get paid in the pew. I had to tell it to sit down.

JONATHON: The hidden thing this week is going to be hidden. I am not telling you what I did that I will not tell the room. Dani almost got it out of me and she said, "Don't. You'll ruin it." She is right. If I say it here it becomes another plaque.

WES: Same. My wife asked where I was Wednesday afternoon. I said, "Out." She asked if I was buying her a boat. I said no. She said, "Then I don't care." That's a good marriage line, and it also kept me from collecting.

JONATHON: So Friday we report whether we hid something on purpose. That's a weird receipt, and I think it's the right one for this mark.

WES: There's a man I want you to hear. The contractor again. He sat in his truck an hour, then went inside and asked his wife what roof she wanted. She said metal. She had wanted metal for six years. They have a metal roof now. He did not post it. He told me because I asked about pay. That's the prize that lasts. She walks under a roof he finally heard. He also said he'd been running his house like a job site for thirty years, and she once told him she was not on his crew. Teaching welding is his way of working with somebody who is allowed to talk back. I want that sentence in this room. If your house feels like a crew, your prize has already come inside.

JONATHON: That's me when I bring the coach voice home. I have already done it. Theo called me on it. I will get to that when we talk shapes. For this week it is enough to say I have used my family as a place to keep winning after the email closed. That's not leadership. That's leftover hunger.

## SEGMENT 6 · This week's challenge

WES: Last few minutes on the challenge. Do one thing this week that nobody will clap for. Then do not tell the room. You can tell one man. You cannot tell the internet. I am being strict about that on purpose, because we will turn a hidden thing into a story the first chance we get. If you need a witness, pick one man who will not clap. Pick a man who will just nod and ask you next Thursday. That's a brother. That's not an audience.

JONATHON: Married men, ask your wife, "What do I do that I want credit for?" Then do not defend the answer. Write it down. That's your cheap prize.

WES: If you live alone, pick one hidden thing. A call to your mother. A meal for a neighbor. An hour on a brother's problem. Do it, and let God be the only audience. Nobody at your apartment is going to clap, which is why this mark is actually easier for you if you will take the gift. The empty room is not a curse this week. It is a chance to work with no audience at all.

JONATHON: Dads, do one hour with your kid that will never go on a plaque. Bath. Catch. Homework. Stay in the room when your phone wants the arrow. I held Theo in a steamy bathroom and I still reached for a ranking the next morning. Do the hour anyway. Then do not use the hour as proof that you are good. The hour is the pay. Proof is the old prize trying to sneak back in through a better story.

WES: And if your cheap prize is the front of the church, give that invitation away once. Not forever. Once. See what your heart does when nobody says rock.

WES: Friday we talk about what stuck from these first weeks. Until then: one hidden thing. One man, not the room. Do not turn Friday into a reveal. Keep the quiet, and let Thursday's little arrow stay in the inbox where it belongs.
