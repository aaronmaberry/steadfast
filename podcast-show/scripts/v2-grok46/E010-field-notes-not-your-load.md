## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: We're about halfway, and I want the Bible open, because the question after weight is always the same. What if I'm carrying stuff that isn't mine? I watch for three signs. Quiet resentment that never goes anywhere. Exhaustion with no progress for anybody. And control, carrying it because you're scared of how somebody else would handle it. My son's job is the third one. First Peter five, verse seven.

JONATHON: "Cast all your anxiety on him because he cares for you." That's the whole verse, and it's not a suggestion you get to postpone.

[BOARD: Psalm 55:22 (NIV) · "Cast your cares on the Lord and he will sustain you; he will never let the righteous be shaken."]

WES: Peter is writing to people who were carrying a lot. Some of them were being pushed out of jobs and families because of their faith. He doesn't say stop worrying. He says throw it on him. Cast it. Like a heavy bag off your shoulder. The reason isn't "because you should." It's that he cares. I want you to hear the order. You don't get lighter first and then throw it. You throw it, and then you find out he was already holding the other end. A lot of us have been treating worry like a job we were hired to do. Peter is firing us.

JONATHON: So the stuff that isn't mine, and isn't anybody's I can hand it to, goes up. My mom's Christmas afternoon goes up. Your son's job, part of it you can hand back. Part of it's you being scared for your kid. That part can't go to him.

WES: That part goes to God. The sentence I've been working on for my son is, "That's your call. I trust you. And if you want to talk it through, I'm here." Then I don't send him any articles. The articles are me taking it back.

JONATHON: No articles. Wait, are you actually going to delete that draft, or am I going to get a "helpful link" by Sunday?

WES: I'm deleting the draft. If I send it, you can screenshot that too.

JONATHON: [laughs] I will. That's the only reason I trust you on this one.

## SEGMENT 4 · The two-column list

WES: Last few minutes on a tool you can use tonight. I'm drawing a line down a legal pad. Left side, "Mine." Right side, "Not mine."

JONATHON: Very high-tech. What's the rule, because if you leave this fuzzy I will write three things and call it done?

WES: Sit down for ten minutes. Use the chicken if you have to. Write everything you're carrying. Everything on your mind, on your calendar, keeping you up at two in the morning. Put each one in a column. If you can't tell, run the test from Tuesday. If I carry it, does somebody else get lighter? Yes, left. No, probably right. Don't cheat and put the hard ones in the middle. There is no middle. The middle is how the dashboard light stays on.

JONATHON: Then a name on the right, and one next step on the left. I did a version on the whiteboard. The family one, which belongs to Theo now. I erased a dinosaur and wrote two columns. On the right, "Mom's Christmas." Name next to it, Mom, and God. On the left, "Call Mom back," with a little phone. Theo added a dinosaur on top of the phone.

WES: On the right, write whose it is. Some of those names will be God's, because nobody else on earth can carry it. Then say the sentence to that person, or pray it by name, and leave it there. On the left, one next step. Not the whole thing. Birthdays in my phone. That was one step.

[BOARD: Write the owner. Then write the next inch.]

JONATHON: Do it with your one man if you can. Read him the right side, because those are the things you'll try to pick back up next week, and he'll notice. If you live alone, nobody notices what you're carrying unless you write it down and tell somebody.

WES: Look around your house this week. If you're a divorced dad, half your right column is stuff that happens at her house. Write her name on it, and then pray it. You can't carry that from across town. And if you live alone, this list is built for you. Nobody is going to notice the pile unless you write it down and tell one man. I would rather you have an ugly page than a quiet apartment that looks fine.
