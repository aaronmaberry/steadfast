"Who were you doing it for?" Then, if it was for the kids, you already got paid. They played ball. I coached one more season. The best of the four. A boy's first caught pop fly.

## SEGMENT 5 · Pushback and challenge

WES: A man says, if nobody notices I'll quit. Then you were doing it for the plaque. Ask Abernathy's question. Who were you doing it for. If it was for the kids, you already got paid.

JONATHON: A man says, my wife should notice. Maybe. Ask her what would help her notice without a sigh. Then do that or put the sigh down. I have sighed for a gold star. It made the kitchen smaller.

WES: This week's challenge. Write one thank-you that cannot be liked. A letter. A sentence. Send it. Don't wait for a reply. And do one unseen thing you refuse to report unless asked.

JONATHON: I'm sending the short text Tuesday. You're writing a letter to the eighth-grade coach, the science teacher who drove the bus to away games. Handwritten. That's a seed.

WES: I found him through the school. Retired. Same town. I'll write the bus in the rain, eleven at night, waiting till every boy had a ride. My son is twenty-one and still talks about it. I will not need an answer.

JONATHON: If you're single, do an unseen good for somebody who cannot advance you. If you're between jobs, the verse still holds. Labor is not only a title. Stand firm is a library morning and a lunch you already promised.
