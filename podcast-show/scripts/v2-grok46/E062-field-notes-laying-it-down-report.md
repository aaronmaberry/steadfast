## SEGMENT 1 · The door

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the kitchen, because Friday is the receipt. Tuesday I came in the kitchen door. She was at the sink. She said, "What's wrong?" I said, "Nothing. I just came in." Twenty years of walking past her to the dresser had taught her that if I stop in the kitchen, something is broken. The back door, kitchen out to the porch, still sticks. I didn't stop to plane it. I came in to her. Those are two different doors, and I'm only working on one of them this week.

JONATHON: That's a report card you wrote with your feet. I get the same look when I sit down at the table before I open a laptop. I'll take the kitchen door. I'll still ask about the jammed one.

WES: [beat] The jammed one. Yeah. Two different doors.

JONATHON: You don't get to retire a delay by walking through a different hinge.

WES: Wednesday she told me her sister's knee replacement is in September. She will drive up and stay a week. I will have the house, the youngest, and supper. I didn't announce that I would handle it. I just said, "Go." Announcing it would have been another letter.

JONATHON: That's the unnamed thing wearing a calendar. You laid down a week and you didn't turn it into a speech. I would have written a paragraph about being the kind of man who can run a house.

WES: Thursday I forgot. I got halfway down the hall. I came back. She handed me a knife and a bowl of okra. She didn't say good job. She handed me work. That's how you know it landed.

[BOARD: Coming back counts if you actually come back.]

JONATHON: Coming back is the whole religion of a Thursday. I can talk about a door for twenty minutes and still walk past it when I'm tired.

WES: Wednesday night I did the kitchen and I gave her the porch. She sat out there half an hour alone. I stayed inside. I didn't follow her out to narrate the gift. If I follow her I've written another letter.

JONATHON: I would have followed her. I would have asked if she was okay. I would have turned her half hour into my moment. You stayed in the house. That's a death I haven't learned yet.

## SEGMENT 2 · Cattle and pancakes

WES: My dad fed cattle every morning of his life. Christmas. The morning the granddad died. The morning I was born. Nobody wrote him a letter. He just went. I've been wanting a letter for things he would have called Tuesday.

JONATHON: I wanted a letter for a calendar change. Dani didn't throw a parade about seven-thirty. I still moved the call. Notice isn't the wage, and I still want the wage.

WES: Your box. Yes or no. Don't dress it up.

JONATHON: Every day but Wednesday. Wednesday I picked me. I knew it while I was doing it. Dani didn't notice. I didn't tell her. Thursday I did the regular one. So the report isn't clean. It's six yes and one no.

WES: That's an honest Friday. A clean Friday would have been a speech.

JONATHON: Then I started seeing her first picks. She parks in the far spot at church so I can unload the kids at the door. She takes the cold side of the bed. She eats the burnt pancake. I've been eating the good pancake for years and calling myself a man who lays things down.

[BOARD: The burnt pancake was already a cross.]

WES: Look around your house this week. Somebody has been laying things down in a room you walk through. You may have called it her personality.

JONATHON: I called it Dani being Dani. That's how a man stays comfortable while he writes speeches about dying.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: We're about halfway, and I want one verse for the man who uses freedom to keep the good pancake. Galatians five, verse thirteen.

JONATHON: "You, my brothers and sisters, were called to be free. But do not use your freedom to indulge the flesh; rather, serve one another humbly in love."

[BOARD: Ephesians 5:28 (NIV) · "In this same way, husbands ought to love their wives as their own bodies. He who loves his wife loves himself."]

WES: Paul isn't canceling freedom. He's telling you what to do with it. Serve. Humbly. In love. A kitchen door is humble. A speech about a kitchen door isn't.

JONATHON: "Indulge the flesh" for me is the good pancake and the near parking spot and the Wednesday I picked me. I can call those small. They're how I stay on the throne.

WES: Context in two sentences. Galatians is about freedom from the law, and then immediately about not turning freedom into a snack. If your freedom always lands you on the warm side of the bed, you have not read the second half of the verse.

JONATHON: I'll be real with you. I wanted this verse to sound like a mission trip. It sounds like okra. That's why it's harder than the conference.

## SEGMENT 4 · Oregon and the song

WES: Austin is back from Oregon. He said, "River was cold. Kids didn't care." Then he called about the moved Tuesday call. He said, "I'm going to text you something. Don't read it to me." I haven't opened it on air and I won't.

JONATHON: That's a brother who knows I will turn a text into a speech. I needed that fence. Dani's audition is next week. Her report. Not mine. I'm still asking once a week about the song. I'm not driving by the store.

WES: Last few minutes on the man who did the thing and wants credit. Don't take the credit. Take the okra. If she hands you work, that's the thank you.

JONATHON: And if you failed Wednesday, say so. Don't hide it in a joke. I hid Wednesday for about an hour. Then I told the truth here, which is cheaper than telling Dani, and I still have to tell Dani.

WES: Tell her. Not as a confession speech. As a sentence. "Wednesday I picked me." Then do Thursday. I forgot the door and came back. Coming back is allowed. Staying down the hall is the old life.

JONATHON: Tool this week is the first-pick swap. Take the cold side. Take the far spot. Eat the burnt one. Don't announce it. If you announce it you have written a letter on a pancake.

## SEGMENT 6 · The week she leaves

WES: I want the September week on the table, because a man will hear kitchen door and think the test is a Thursday. The test is a week alone with a sixteen-year-old and a stove. I haven't run that house without her in a long time. I won't call her every hour to prove I'm fine.

JONATHON: I would call every hour. I would send pictures of supper. I would turn her sister's recovery into my report card. Don't do that. Let her be a sister. You be the house.

WES: That's the unnamed thing wearing seven days. I'll cook. I'll drive the boy. I'll keep the television off at supper. I won't send her a speech about how well I'm doing it.

JONATHON: Send her one sentence at night. "We're good. Love you." Then stop. If you write a paragraph you have written another letter. I know, because I write paragraphs when I'm scared I'm not enough.

WES: Look around your house this week. If a week like that's coming, practice the door now. Don't wait for September to become a man in your own kitchen.
