## SEGMENT · The shoebox and the pie

WES: I need the shoebox on the record a little longer. Mom kept every card that wasn't from a store. I wrote one the year I got married. Three lines. Grateful spelled wrong. She didn't correct it. She put it in the box. I had forgotten the spelling until she said it on the phone like it was a joke we share. It's not a joke. It's a record that I once used a word I could not spell and she treated it as enough.

JONATHON: I keep rewriting the sister note because I want her to cry and I want her not to be mad that I'm late. That's two notes. I can only send one. The one I think I'm sending is the one in the morning call. He could call at one because she taught him to say the hard thing out loud. If she asks who wrote it, I will say I did, even if Dani fixed two words.

WES: Merle does not need us to sing. He needs two more men who will drive forty minutes and take a note. I can't be one of those men. I can tell you to be one if you can match pitch even a little. If you can't, you can still tell your people one story about a woman who is gone or old or hard. That's the same family of obedience.

JONATHON: Saturday I will burn nothing if I can help it. Store crust. Sister's rule. Dani beside me not helping. If Mom says store crust in one bite, I won't defend the pie. I will ask her to teach Ruby the next Saturday. That's the leftover honor. The pie is for once. The Saturday is for a while.

JONATHON: Mine is already on a calendar. That's the only reason I trust it. A mood isn't a plan, and I've used moods for years to sound wise while I stayed still. I don't want to sound wise. I want to be on time.
