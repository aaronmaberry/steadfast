## SEGMENT 1 · How do you stop

WES: Let's take the next few minutes on the text, because a young man went first and he gave you permission to tell it without his name.

JONATHON: He is twenty-two. My sister's son. He checks in by text. Thursday, 1:14 a.m., "how do you stop." Porn since he was thirteen. Worse when he is lonely. Better since he started texting me on Sundays instead of disappearing. Then a bad week. I called at 1:15 and I listened for an hour. I said, "I've been there. Longer than you'd guess." That's all I say at this table. Dani knows the rest. The plan: he told the roommate he trusts the whole truth on Friday. I text him Thursdays. He is on day five. I told him he will probably slip. We will see. He said I could tell it if I left his name off. So I left his name off.

WES: That's a brave text from a young man and a brave hour from you. You did not send a sermon at 1:14. You picked up. Most of us would have written "we'll talk tomorrow" and gone back to sleep and called that wisdom.

JONATHON: I wanted to write tomorrow. My thumb was already on it. Then I thought about being nine and wanting someone to pick up. So I picked up. He asked, "Does God hate me?" I said, "He's not disgusted by you. He's after you." I do not have a better paragraph than that. His apartment is three bedrooms, three screens, and a couch nobody sits on. I told him to sit on the couch with the roommate and leave the third screen in a drawer. He wrote the next morning, "we sat. it was awkward. we sat anyway."

WES: Awkward and sitting is the whole Christian life some weeks. If the dark gets bigger than a habit, if a man starts thinking about ending his life, he should call 988, or a pastor, or a doctor, today. We are not playing with that. A Thursday text is not a substitute for a crisis line. Hear me. 988. Then a brother. Then this table if you want. In that order.

JONATHON: I said that to him too. He said he was not there. I believed him and I still told him the number. I would rather be the uncle who overdid it than the uncle who was cool. Dani remembered him at five in a Spider-Man costume and she texted him a Sunday lunch invite. He wrote, "not yet. soon." I am glad he said not yet instead of yes and then hiding. Not yet is honest. We will keep the texts.

## SEGMENT 2 · The fog again

WES: Mine is quieter and I have done it longer. Evenings, eight to ten. Tractor-restoration videos and the weather radar app. Last Wednesday my wife asked me something three times. Then she said, "You're doing the fog again." She had not said the fog in about fifteen years. The first time, 2011, took Mr. Abernathy and two years. I thought I had retired it. I had just moved it to a smaller screen.

JONATHON: The fog is a clean name. I do not have one that good. I have "just checking." That's a lie in three syllables. What did you do after she said it?

WES: I put the phone on the fridge. I went to the back porch for twenty minutes. No phone. She talked about her sister. I listened the first night like a man taking a test. The second night I asked a question I did not already have the answer to. The third night she fell asleep in the chair. I sat there and I watched the dark and I did not open the radar. The radar is useless in the dark anyway. I just wanted a thing I could refresh. Refresh is how I leave a room without standing up.

JONATHON: Dani's dad is seventy-four. Thirty years of golf on TV with the sound low. When we were dating she said, "My dad's been in the room my whole life, and I don't know him. I'm marrying you because you talk." Last Christmas he asked Ruby to teach him a card game. He is trying, late. I do not want to become the golf with the sound low. I can feel the itch in my thumb at supper. Ruby still has the basket. Some nights I still reach.

WES: The fan is done. The house job now is the back door. Still sticks. I still have to lift it to get to that porch. The other I'll-get-to-it is a glass I can hold in a chair while a woman talks about her sister. I will not let a radar become fourteen months. I told my youngest I am on the porch after supper this week. He said, "Can I come." I said yes, some nights. A sixteen-year-old on a porch is better than a radar I already know.

[BOARD: Refresh is how a man leaves without standing up.]

WES: We have a word, the fog. If you do not have a word, borrow ours. Tell the person who lives with you, "If I go there, say the word." Then do not punish her for saying it. She is not nagging. She is doing the Thursday text out loud.

## SEGMENT 3 · The Word

WES: We're about halfway, and I want the Bible open, because the heart is the well, not the screen. Proverbs four, verse twenty-three.

JONATHON: I have used guard your heart as a reason to hide. That's not what it means. Hiding is how I got a third screen and a couch nobody sits on.

WES: "Above all else, guard your heart, for everything you do flows from it." The heart is not a mood. It is the spring. If the spring is a radar and a late video and a lonely apartment, the week will taste like that. Guarding is not a lock and a secret. Guarding is a porch, a brother, a basket, a number on the fridge. Paul already told us not to let the sun go down on anger. This is the quieter twin. Do not let the sun go down on a drift you will not name.

[BOARD: The spring tastes like whatever you refresh.]

JONATHON: Everything you do flows from it. Including the way I coach first base. We have a laminated lineup now. Harper leads off. Harper's dad throws. I stand at first and I try to be a man who is actually there, not a man refreshing a score in his head. The first game is Saturday the twentieth. I want to be in that dirt, not in a pocket.

WES: Then be in the dirt. Guarding your heart this week might look like grayscale, or a charger in a hallway, or twenty minutes of porch. We will give you the tool Friday. Today is the name. Itch or drift. Which one is yours. The itch is the thing you keep reaching for. The drift is the way you leave without standing up. Some men have both. Name the one that owns eight to ten. I have been naming other men's screens for years and leaving my radar alone because a radar looks like weather, and weather looks like homework. It is not homework. It is a way to stay slightly gone. If your thing looks responsible, a forecast, a score, a work email at nine, I want you to be more suspicious of it, not less. The respectable drift is the one that lasts fifteen years.

JONATHON: I told Dani the verse this week and she said, "Then stop refreshing the leaderboard in your head at bath." She is right. I can be in the dirt at first base and still be in a pocket I did not take out of my jeans. The heart can leave while the body stays. That's the part that scares me more than a late site, because nobody can see it and I can still collect credit for being present. Ruby can see it. She always can. If I am gone she looks at me the way she looked at the drawer. I do not want that look on a Saturday in March.

## SEGMENT 4 · Pushback

WES: Keep pushing.

JONATHON: A man is going to hear porn and radar and think we just made a list of bad hobbies. His wife wants him to rest. A video about a tractor is not a crime. Draw the line.

WES: Draw it. Rest is a gift. A video can be rest. The drift is when she asks three times and you do not hear her. The itch is when the thing you reach for owns the hour you said belonged to them. Rest looks at a person. Drift looks at a glass. I am not coming for your tractor. I am coming for the third ask.

JONATHON: Second. I told a young man I have been there, and I will not tell this room the rest. Some guy is going to want the story so he can measure his against mine. I am not giving him a scoreboard.

WES: Good. Do not give him a scoreboard. Give him a number and a roommate and a Thursday text. The details that make you look honest can also become a new plaque. You already know about plaques.

JONATHON: Third. What if the person who lives with you is the drift? Dani's dad is in the room and gone. I cannot porch him into talking. I can ask Ruby to teach him a game and I can refuse to become him. That's a smaller assignment than fixing a seventy-four-year-old.

WES: That's the assignment. You cannot drag a man off a fairway he has sat on for thirty years. You can be the man who talks. You can be the man who answers a late text. You can be the man who hears the third ask. Stop waiting for him to go first. Go first in your own chair. I have waited on my older brother, on a title, on a fan, on a fog I swore I had finished. Waiting felt like patience. It was drift with a better name. If you are waiting to feel like talking, you will get thirty years of golf with the sound low. Talk tonight. Clumsy. Short. Then stop and listen longer than you talked.

JONATHON: I keep wanting a clean category. Itch is dirty. Drift is sad. One I can repent of in a sentence and one I can blame on being tired. They're cousins. My nephew's late site is itch. My leaderboard at bath is itch wearing a work shirt. Wes's radar is drift wearing a chore. If I let myself pick the nicer cousin I will not change a Thursday. So I am picking both and I am doing the hallway anyway. If I slip I will say slip. I told my nephew I would. I do not get to keep a higher standard for a twenty-two-year-old than I keep for a man who turned thirty-seven in February and knows better.

JONATHON: I am texting my nephew every Thursday. Day five today. I told him slip is not the end. Silence is closer to the end. I am also putting my charger in the hallway starting tonight, because the nightstand is a recliner I can reach without standing. Ruby already approved the hallway. She said it like a sheriff.

WES: Sunday is Mom's first pot roast in a long time. We will tell you Friday who came and who prayed. I want that on the calendar so I do not drift past a table I already said yes to.

JONATHON: And I have a Bluebonnets game Saturday the twentieth. I want to walk out to a kid first if a kid needs it, not look at the other dads. I have been the other dad in my head already. That's a drift that happens in daylight. I already know which twin is scared of the ball. I already know I will want to look competent for the parents. Competent is a cousin of the coach voice. If I catch myself performing I will kneel in the dirt and ask a girl about her lizard. That is a contrary minute I can actually do.

WES: Name which one is yours this week. Itch or drift. Write it. Tell one person. Then do one contrary hour. Porch. Hallway. A call you answer. A couch you sit on. Do not wait to feel holy. Stop waiting. The feeling is not the door. I will tell you Friday whether I cheated, because I have already learned how to turn a porch into a story I tell instead of a porch I sit on. If I only talk about the porch I am still in the fog. The chair is the test. The dark is the test. The third ask is the test. Everything else is a meeting I already chaired.

WES: Look around your house this week. If a woman has to ask three times, you already have your card. If a boy is sitting on a couch nobody uses, you already have your hour. Do not shop for a nicer sin. The first one that makes your thumb restless is the assignment. I keep wanting to pick a sin from 2011 so I can feel like I already beat this. 2011 is done. Wednesday is not. Wednesday I did not hear her twice, and then I did not hear her a third time, and then she said the fog. That is the assignment. Not a memoir. A Wednesday. Write the Wednesday. Tell her. Sit on the porch. If you skip the porch, say you skipped it. Do not tell a prettier story on Friday than the one you lived on Wednesday. We have already had enough plaques. Write the true Wednesday. Then sit down anyway, even if the chair is cold and you already want the radar back.
