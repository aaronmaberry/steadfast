## OPEN

[COLD OPEN. No music under. Max seated at the table. A carpenter's level lies on the table in front of him. Lower third: Max | Steadfast.]

[Max picks up the level, holds it flat, watches the bubble.]

This is a level. Every man who has ever built anything owns one. It is the simplest tool in the box. A bubble floating between two lines. It doesn't have an opinion. It doesn't care how you feel about the wall. It doesn't care how hard you worked on the wall. It tells you one thing: is this true, or is it off?

[Sets it down.]

Here is a strange fact about most men I know. They own a level for their walls and they have never once been handed a level for their lives. Nobody ever put a tool in their hands and said, "Here. Set this on your manhood. See if it's true."

So they build. They build careers and marriages and families and reputations, and they build them by eye. By feel. By whatever the guys around them are doing. And a lot of it looks fine from across the room. And then the weight comes, because the weight always comes, and the wall that was built by eye starts to lean.

Last week I told you that you are not crazy. The confusion about manhood is real, and it is not a defect in you. It is what happens when a world loses the definition.

This week I hand you the level.

My name is Max. This is Steadfast. Twenty-four weeks, building men who don't move. This is week three, and it is the week the whole rest of the series stands on.

[Lower third: Week 3 of 24 | The Four Marks]

Here is the sentence. Same one every week, open and close. Listen to it this time like you're hearing it for the first time.

A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.

Four marks. That's the level. Today I am going to show you where those four marks come from. I am going to show you the first man in history and the exact moment he dropped every one of them. And I am going to show you a second Man who picked every one of them up and carried them all the way to a cross and out the other side.

If you get this week, the next twenty-one weeks will make sense. If you don't, they'll just be a list. So stay with me.

## CLAIM

[Straight to camera. Slow down.]

Here is the claim. Twice.

Manhood is not an age, a feeling, or a body. It is a calling with four marks, and every one of them was dropped by the first man and carried perfectly by the second.

Again. Manhood is not an age, a feeling, or a body. It is a calling with four marks, and every one of them was dropped by the first man and carried perfectly by the second.

Let me unpack the first half before we go to the text.

Manhood is not an age. You do not become a man at eighteen. Plenty of forty-year-olds have never gotten there, and plenty of nineteen-year-olds have.

Manhood is not a feeling. Most of the men I respect most did not feel like men when they did the bravest thing they ever did. They felt sick. They did it anyway.

Manhood is not a body. A body makes you male. It does not make you a man. If it did, manhood would be a matter of muscle, and the strongest man in a prison yard would qualify while a faithful man in a wheelchair wouldn't. That's obviously false.

Manhood is a calling. It is something God summons a male toward. It has a shape. It can be described. It can be aimed at. It can be missed. And because it has a shape, it can be measured. That's the level.

Now the second half of the claim. Dropped by the first man. Carried by the second. That's the text. Open your Bible.

## TEXT

[On screen: Genesis 2:15-17. Max opens Bible.]

Genesis chapter 2, verse 15. This is the first assignment ever given to a man.

[Read.]

"The LORD God took the man and put him in the garden of Eden to work it and keep it. And the LORD God commanded the man, saying, 'You may surely eat of every tree of the garden, but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you shall not eat, for in the day that you eat of it you shall surely die.'"

Three things are handed to Adam before Eve ever shows up. Watch them, because they map straight onto the four marks.

He is given work. "To work it and keep it." The Hebrew word for "keep" is the word for guard. It's the same word used for a watchman on a wall. So the first man's job description is cultivate and protect. Make it flourish and keep it safe.

He is given a word. God speaks the command to the man directly. The man hears it first. He is responsible to know it and to carry it.

And he is given a warning. "You shall surely die." There is a real enemy, a real danger, and the man has been told about it.

Now go to chapter 3. I'm going to read the fall slowly, because I want you to see the man in it. Most people read this story and see a serpent and a woman. I want you to find the man.

[On screen: Genesis 3:1-13.]

Verse 1. "Now the serpent was more crafty than any other beast of the field that the LORD God had made. He said to the woman."

To the woman. Hold that.

The serpent asks whether God really said they couldn't eat from any tree. The woman answers. The serpent tells her, verse 4, "You will not surely die," and promises, verse 5, that she will be like God. Then verse 6.

"So when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was a delight to the eyes, and that the tree was to be desired to make one wise, she took of its fruit and ate, and she also gave some to her husband who was with her, and he ate."

Who was with her. The man was there. The whole time.

Verse 8. "And they heard the sound of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day, and the man and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God among the trees of the garden."

Verse 9. "But the LORD God called to the man and said to him, 'Where are you?'"

To the man. Not to the woman. Not to the serpent. God comes looking for the man.

Verse 10. "And he said, 'I heard the sound of you in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked, and I hid myself.'"

Verse 11. God asks him straight: "Have you eaten of the tree of which I commanded you not to eat?"

Verse 12. Listen to the first man's answer.

"The man said, 'The woman whom you gave to be with me, she gave me fruit of the tree, and I ate.'"

[Close the Bible. Look up.]

There it is. Four marks, four failures, in thirteen verses. He was there and did nothing. He blamed his wife and his God in a single sentence. He hid instead of leading. And he traded the whole garden for a piece of fruit.

Every man since has inherited that reflex. Paul says it plainly in Romans 5:19: "For as by the one man's disobedience the many were made sinners, so by the one man's obedience the many will be made righteous." One man's disobedience. One man's obedience. Two men. Two Adams. And the whole story of manhood is caught between them.

So let's take the four marks one at a time. For each one I'm going to do the same four things: tell you what it means, show you where Adam dropped it, show you where Jesus carried it, and name the counterfeit, because every one of these marks has a fake version that men fall for.

## MOVEMENTS

### Movement 1: Rejects passivity

[On screen: "her husband who was with her" Genesis 3:6]

What it means. Passivity is the refusal to act when action is required. Notice I did not say laziness. Lazy men are easy to spot. Passive men often look busy. Passivity almost never shows up as doing nothing. It shows up as doing something else. It shows up as waiting for more information, keeping the peace, staying out of it, letting her handle it, letting the church handle it, letting somebody who's better at this handle it. It dresses itself up as patience and humility and neutrality, and underneath it is a man who saw what needed to be done and did not do it.

Where Adam dropped it. Genesis 3:6, "her husband who was with her." The serpent is talking to his wife. The serpent is misquoting the word that God gave to him directly. He was told to guard the garden, and the most dangerous thing that ever entered the garden is standing in front of him, lying to his wife, and he says nothing. Not one word out of the man's mouth in the entire temptation. The first sin of the first man was not eating. It was silence.

Where Jesus carried it. Go to another garden. Gethsemane, Luke 22. The enemy is there too, and the stakes are infinitely higher. And the second Adam does not stand by. He prays, verse 42: "Father, if you are willing, remove this cup from me. Nevertheless, not my will, but yours, be done." That is not a man being swept along by events. Listen to how he describes his own death in John 10:18: "No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of my own accord." Nobody took anything from Jesus. He walked toward it with his eyes open. He acted.

The counterfeit. The fake version of rejecting passivity is aggression. Some men hear "reject passivity" and think it means get loud, get in people's faces, break things. No. Aggression is just passivity's twin brother; both of them are a man who won't do the hard, right thing. The aggressive man won't do it because he's out of control, and the passive man won't do it because he's out of the fight. Rejecting passivity means initiative. It means when you see it, you move toward it, calmly, on purpose, in the right direction.

[Beat.]

Picture a man at his own dinner table. His fourteen-year-old says something to his mother that is flat-out disrespectful. Everybody at the table feels it. The man has two options that look like doing nothing: keep eating, or wait to see if his wife handles it. And he has one option that is manhood: put the fork down and say, "Son. We don't speak to your mother like that. Try again." Ten seconds. That's rejecting passivity. It almost never requires a speech. It requires a man who was with her, and this time, spoke.

### Movement 2: Accepts responsibility

[On screen: "Where are you?" Genesis 3:9]

What it means. Responsibility is answering for what has been entrusted to you. It has two parts, and men usually only want one of them. The first part is answering for what you did. The second part is answering for what is yours to tend, whether or not you caused the problem. Your wife's flourishing is yours to tend. Your children's souls are yours to tend. Your body, your money, your work, your word, your walk with God. When something in that field is broken, a man doesn't ask "whose fault is this" first. He asks "what's mine to do."

Where Adam dropped it. Genesis 3:9, God comes and calls to the man. "Where are you?" Notice that God doesn't go to the serpent first. He doesn't go to the woman first. He goes to the one he gave the word to. That is a statement about who is answerable. And Adam's answer in verse 12 is the most efficient blame-shift in human history: "The woman whom you gave to be with me, she gave me fruit of the tree, and I ate." Count the moves. The woman: her fault. Whom you gave: your fault, God. She gave me: I was a victim. And I ate: the only three words of ownership, buried at the end of the sentence, after he's already pinned it on everyone else in the garden.

Men still talk like that. My wife. My dad. My boss. My upbringing. My schedule. My ADHD. My church hurt. And I ate.

Where Jesus carried it. Here is what the second Adam did with responsibility. He took it for sins he did not commit. Isaiah 53:6: "All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD has laid on him the iniquity of us all." Paul says it in 2 Corinthians 5:21: "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin." The first man refused to own what he did. The second Man owned what he didn't do. That is the whole gospel, and it is also the whole model. If Jesus could shoulder the weight of a world he didn't break, you can shoulder the weight of a home you helped break.

The counterfeit. There are two fakes here. The first is control. The controlling man thinks responsibility means everything depends on him, so he trusts no one, delegates nothing, and calls his anxiety leadership. That's not responsibility, that's fear wearing a tie. The second fake is guilt. The guilt-soaked man thinks feeling terrible is the same as owning it. It isn't. Adam felt terrible. He hid. Ownership isn't a feeling. It's a sentence: "That was mine. I was wrong. Here is what I'm going to do about it."

[Beat.]

You want to know if a man has this mark? Listen to him when something goes wrong. The man who accepts responsibility says "I" before he says "they." Every time.

### Movement 3: Leads courageously

[On screen: "the man and his wife hid themselves" Genesis 3:8]

What it means. Leadership is going first toward what is right and taking people with you. Courageous leadership is doing it when it costs you. It is not a title. It is not a personality type. Some of the best leaders I've watched are quiet men who don't like the front of the room. Leadership is a direction. It is where your feet go when the situation gets hard, and whether anyone can follow you there.

Where Adam dropped it. Genesis 3:8. "The man and his wife hid themselves." He did lead. He led her into the trees. When the sound of God came walking in the garden, the man had one job: take his wife by the hand and walk toward the voice. Fall on his face and say, "It was me. I stood there. I let it happen. Do what you must to me." Instead, verse 10, "I was afraid." Fear took the lead, and the man followed fear, and his wife followed him, straight into hiding. That's the anti-leadership of every passive man since: not the absence of leading, but leading in the wrong direction. Away from God. Away from the hard conversation. Away from the light.

Where Jesus carried it. Mark 10:32: "And they were on the road, going up to Jerusalem, and Jesus was walking ahead of them. And they were amazed, and those who followed were afraid." Walking ahead of them. Toward Jerusalem, where he knew exactly what was waiting. The disciples are scared out of their minds and he is out front. Then John 18. The soldiers come to the garden with torches and weapons. Verse 4: "Then Jesus, knowing all that would happen to him, came forward." Came forward. And verse 8: "If you seek me, let these men go." That is the whole mark in one scene. He goes first, and he covers the men behind him.

The counterfeit. Domination. Jesus names it directly in Mark 10:42: "those who are considered rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them." Then, verse 43, "But it shall not be so among you." The dominating man mistakes being obeyed for leading. He gets compliance in the house and loses every heart in it. The other fake is the reverse: cowardice dressed as humility. "I don't want to impose." "Who am I to say?" "I'm just trying to serve." Sometimes that's real humility. Often it's a man who doesn't want to take the hit that going first requires. Real leadership looks like verse 45: "the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many." Serve, and give your life. First to the towel and first to the cross.

[Beat.]

In your house this week, somebody has to be the first one to say, "Let's pray." Somebody has to be the first one to say, "I was wrong last night." Somebody has to be the first one to say, "We need to talk about money," or "We're going to church this Sunday," or "I don't think that friendship is good for you." If that somebody is not you, then you're leading, brother. Just not anywhere good.

### Movement 4: Expects the greater reward, God's reward

[On screen: "for the joy that was set before him" Hebrews 12:2]

Now the fourth mark, and I want to tell you up front that this one is different from the other three. The first three are things you do. This one is why you can keep doing them. Pull this one out and the other three collapse. Rejecting passivity with no reward in view becomes exhaustion. Responsibility with no reward in view becomes a crushing weight. Courage with no reward in view becomes recklessness, or it just quietly burns out. This mark is the engine.

What it means. To expect the greater reward is to live for God's "well done" over every lesser payoff. It means you do the right thing, in the dark, when nobody's watching, when it costs you now, because you actually believe there is a God who sees and who pays, and that what he pays is greater than anything the lesser road is offering. Hebrews 11:6: "whoever would draw near to God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him." The Bible is not shy about reward. Faith means believing God is a rewarder.

Where Adam dropped it. Look again at verse 6 of Genesis 3. Good for food. A delight to the eyes. Desired to make one wise. Three rewards, all immediate, all real. And the serpent's promise on top: you will be like God. Adam was standing in a garden where God had said "you may surely eat of every tree," where he walked with God in the cool of the day, where the reward on offer was God himself, forever. And he traded all of that for a lesser reward he could hold in his hand right now. That's the trade every man makes when he sins. He does not choose evil for evil's sake. He chooses a smaller reward, sooner, over the greater reward, later.

Where Jesus carried it. Hebrews 12:2: "looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith, who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is seated at the right hand of the throne of God." For the joy set before him. Jesus did not endure the cross by gritting his teeth and pretending it didn't hurt. He endured it by looking past it to a reward, and calculating that the reward was worth the cross. The second Adam ran the math the first Adam wouldn't. Moses did the same math, Hebrews 11:26: "He considered the reproach of Christ greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt, for he was looking to the reward."

The counterfeit. Two fakes again. The first is obvious: the lesser rewards. Applause. Comfort. The bonus. The screen. The woman who isn't your wife. Every one of them is a real reward, and every one of them is smaller than what God is holding. The second fake is sneakier, and it catches religious men: no reward at all. Just duty. Just grit. "I don't do it for a reward, I do it because it's right." That sounds noble and it collapses in about four years. Jesus didn't run on duty. He ran on joy set before him. Matthew 6:6: "your Father who sees in secret will reward you." Colossians 3:24: "from the Lord you will receive the inheritance as your reward." God wants you to expect it.

[Beat.]

So a man who has this mark can watch the guy with the boat and not lose his mind. That's Psalm 73 from last week. He can work heartily at a job nobody thanks him for. He can say no to the screen at midnight and feel the loss and not feel robbed, because he knows the ledger isn't closed yet. He is living for an audience of One, and he expects that One to pay.

[Shift. Straight to camera. This is the guard rail.]

Now let me say the most important thing in this lecture, and I need you to hear it before we go to application. These four marks are not a ladder you climb to earn your way to God. Adam already proved no man climbs that ladder. The second Adam climbed it for you. Romans 5:19, one man's obedience makes the many righteous. Paul says in 1 Corinthians 15:49 that just as we have borne the image of the man of dust, we will bear the image of the man of heaven. That's the order. You are not trying to become a man so God will accept you. God has accepted you in Christ, and now he is making you into the Man who did it right. The four marks are not the entrance fee. They are the family resemblance.

## APPLICATION

[Shift posture. Practical register. On screen: Name it / Do this week / Tell a brother / Carry this]

Four moves. Same as every week.

### Name it

Name your weak leg. Every man has one mark that is weaker than the other three, and he usually knows which one it is within about two seconds of hearing the list. Some of you are responsibility men who will own anything, but you're passive; you'd rather take the blame than start the hard thing. Some of you are courageous men who will go first anywhere, but you're doing it for the applause, not for God. Some of you reject passivity all day at work and go completely silent in your own house.

Which one is it? Say it. "My weak leg is _____." Not all four. One. You can't fix four things this week. You can start on one.

### Do this week

Three things.

One. Memorize the sentence. All four marks, in order, word for word, by Friday. Write it on a card. Say it while you shave. You should be able to say it without looking before next week's lecture, because from here on I'm going to assume you can.

Two. Take the four-mark audit. Score yourself one to five on each mark. Be honest, nobody's watching. Then do the harder part: ask your wife, or if you're not married, ask the man who knows you best, to score you on the same four. Don't argue with the score. Just look at the gap between yours and theirs. The gap is where the work is.

Three. Take one action on your weak leg before Sunday. If it's passivity, do the thing you've been putting off; you already know what it is. If it's responsibility, say "that was mine" to the person who needs to hear it. If it's courage, go first in one hard conversation. If it's reward, pick one hidden act of obedience this week that no human being will ever know about, and do it for the Father who sees in secret.

### Tell a brother

Say the sentence to a brother, out loud, to his face. Then tell him your weak leg. Then ask him his. That's the whole conversation. It'll take three minutes and it'll be the most honest thing that's happened between you two in a year.

If you sent the coffee text last week, this is what you talk about at coffee.

### Carry this

One line for the week.

"The first man dropped them. The second Man carried them. Walk in his steps."

Say it when you feel the pull to stand there silent. Say it when the blame is on the tip of your tongue. Say it when it's time to go first and you don't want to. The first man dropped them. The second Man carried them. Walk in his steps.

## CLOSE

[Return to seated, direct to camera. Slow. Level back in frame on the table.]

Let me land this.

[Picks up the level.]

A level doesn't build the wall. It just tells you the truth about the wall. That's what these four marks are. They will not make you a man by themselves. They will tell you the truth about where you're off, and they will point you at the Man who is perfectly true, and they will show you what you're becoming as he works on you.

The first man was given work, a word, and a warning, and when the moment came he stood there silent, blamed his wife and his God, led his family into the trees, and traded the garden for fruit.

The second Man walked into a garden of his own, said "not my will but yours," carried sins that weren't his, walked ahead of frightened men toward a cross, and did it all for the joy that was set before him.

You are between those two men. You've got the first one's reflexes and the second one's Spirit. Every week from here on is about learning to move like the second.

Here's the level. Same sentence every week.

A real man rejects passivity, accepts responsibility, leads courageously, and expects the greater reward, God's reward.

Next week we take the first mark and go deep. It's called "Stop Waiting," and it's about a man who sat by a pool for thirty-eight years with a very good excuse. Some of you have a very good excuse too. Subscribe and hit the bell so you don't miss it, because every week of this build stands on the one before it.

[Beat.]

Stand firm. Act like men. Be strong.

I'll see you next week.
